## Provisions observed in the scan sequence

- **Art. 1, pp.19–21:** definitions include establishment, high/medium/limited-risk establishments, blind spots, safe driving, Ministry, competent authority and Labour Law 14/2025.
- **Art. 2, p.22:** applies to establishments/branches with 50 or more workers regardless of employment relationship/mode or affiliation; separate geographically or administratively separated worksite is counted independently at the threshold.
- **Art. 3, pp.22–23:** employer/board chair/responsible manager must provide a safe workplace, approve an OSH policy, supply human/financial/technical resources, define roles/authority, support training/awareness and worker participation.
- **Arts. 4–5, pp.24–26:** establish the OSH functional unit, prevent retaliation against its staff for performing their duties, and define duties including policy implementation, standards, risk controls, incident analysis, inspections, training, emergency planning and regulatory compliance.
- **Art. 6, p.27:** small/limited-risk establishments below 50 workers have duties under the accompanying table, including safety resources and training; medium/high-risk establishments below 50 workers must appoint a qualified OSH supervisor or specialist as stated in the article/table.
- **Art. 7, pp.27–28:** establishes OSH committees and describes membership/functions, including incident investigation, risk controls, training and emergency readiness.
- **Arts. 8–10, p.29:** notifications to competent authorities, committee meeting cadence (reported as at least monthly for high/medium-risk and at least every two months for limited-risk, with a 48-hour emergency meeting trigger), quorum/decision rules. Verify every count and exception against the image.
- **Arts. 11–15, pp.30–31:** committee minutes and records; central committee and branch-committee arrangements; training requirements; cancellation of prior Decision 134/2003; publication/effective-date clause.
- **Arts. 16–20, pp.31–32:** specialist/supervisor/technician qualification and training requirements; includes different degree/experience categories by role and risk class.
- **Table 1, pp.33–42:** establishments/sites by sector and risk category. It enumerates industries and service sectors across a long table; examples of high-risk groups include petroleum/refining, petrochemicals, basic chemicals, explosives/pyrotechnics, fertilizer/agrochemical production, industrial gases/pressurized gases, heavy metals/smelting, glass, cement/thermal materials, ceramics, pharmaceuticals and battery/energy-related manufacturing. Later pages include water/sewerage, radiation/nuclear activities, waste management, construction/demolition, transport/communications, workshops, tourism, restaurants, education/research, finance/insurance and administrative/service activities. The complete table must be transcribed row by row before a real establishment is classified.
- **Table 2, p.43:** specifies the required numbers of OSH specialists, safety supervisors and technicians according to risk class and worker-count bands. The table is visible but its cells must be zoomed/transcribed and checked against the official PDF before storing any exact staffing ratios.
- **Table 3, p.44:** identifies activities requiring continuous pollutant monitoring, including chemical industries, chlorine/caustic-soda production, petroleum/gas/petrochemical activities, certain solvent industries, cement, ammonia refrigeration, sewage/waste treatment, underground work and hazardous-material storage. Listed substances include chlorine (Cl2), ammonia (NH3), hydrogen sulphide (H2S), nitrogen dioxide (NO2), sulphur dioxide (SO2), carbon monoxide (CO), methane (CH4), VOCs and lower explosive limit (LEL). Capture exact rows and gas combinations before creating operational requirements.

## Implementation circulars / transition

The Ministry's 27 October clarification says:
- current OSH specialists should not be harmed or excluded solely because of the decision;
- previously recognized qualifications and acquired legal positions should be respected;
- holders of other university degrees may be considered where they have specialized postgraduate OSH diplomas, extended practical experience or recognized certificates, under level-specific requirements;
- the Ministry directed the competent central administration to issue periodic circulars standardizing implementation and status-regularization procedures.

**Search outcome as of 2026-10-29:** the official clarification is located, but no separate periodic implementation circular was found in targeted searches of the Ministry site or indexed public sources. Do not claim that the circulars do not exist; record them as “announced but not located publicly.” The official clarification is an administrative interpretation, not a substitute for any circular or the operative text.
