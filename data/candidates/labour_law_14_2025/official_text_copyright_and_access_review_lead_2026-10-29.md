# Labour Law 14/2025 — legal-text copyright and source-access review lead

**Prepared:** 2026-10-29  
**Status:** Materially improved legal research lead; not a legal opinion or blanket reuse clearance.

## Finding: Egyptian copyright law excludes official legal documents from protection

WIPO Lex's consolidated record for Egypt's Law No. 82 of 2002 on the Protection of Intellectual Property Rights, amended through Law No. 178 of 2020, reproduces Article 141. Because that consolidation is current only through 2020, any post-2020 amendments and current Egyptian-law interpretation must be checked before relying on this exclusion for a commercial product. Article 141 states that protection does not cover official documents, whatever their source or target language, including laws, regulations, resolutions and decisions, international conventions, court decisions, arbitral awards, and decisions of administrative committees with judicial competence. It also states that creative selection/arrangement of collections may be protected.

Sources:
- WIPO Lex English record and consolidated statutory text: https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/22066
- WIPO Lex Arabic record: https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/ar/legislation/details/22066
- Article 141 appears in the official-text record; use the Arabic statutory text and obtain Egyptian counsel confirmation before relying on it for product deployment.

## What this may mean for the Labour Law corpus

The text of Law 14/2025 as an official law is a strong candidate for the Article 141 exclusion. This is a significant lead for the legal-text copyright question, and it means the project should not treat the secondary host's missing licence as automatically proving that the statute itself is copyrighted.

However, do **not** collapse four distinct issues into one:
1. **The official legal text:** likely within Article 141's official-document exclusion, subject to counsel confirmation and the exact material used.
2. **A website's access terms and technical workflow:** terms may still govern use of that service, accounts, and bulk retrieval. Article 141 alone does not authorize bypassing a request-a-copy workflow or violating access controls.
3. **A publisher's editorial additions or creative compilation:** editorial notes, arrangement, commentary, annotations, and a creative collection may have separate protection.
4. **A PDF scan / file and data acquisition:** retain source provenance and avoid copying third-party commentary, logos, layout, or editorial material when a clean text transcription from an authorized official source is sufficient.

## Extension to the 2026 OSH ministerial decisions — candidate lead only

Article 141's reproduced list includes official resolutions and decisions as well as laws and regulations. That creates a plausible legal-text exclusion lead for the operative text of Ministerial Decisions 248–252/2026 too, but this note is **not** a determination that the exact secondary-hosted transcriptions, scans, page images, annotations, or compilation may be copied or commercially reused. The cited consolidation is only through 2020, and a qualified Egyptian lawyer must confirm the current law and its application to the intended product use.

For Decisions 248–252, keep separate records for:
- the official operative text of each decision and its Gazette publication;
- the secondary publisher's HTML transcription and any editorial additions;
- reproduced page images, watermarks, formatting, tables and compilation structure;
- the source website's access/collection terms and any restrictions on systematic retrieval;
- the product's planned internal storage, RAG indexing, quotation/display and commercial use.

The Article 141 lead does not authorize bypassing the official catalogue's request-a-copy workflow, and it does not make a secondary reproduction equivalent to the official Gazette. Use an ordinarily accessible official text route where available, preserve provenance, and leave the reuse gate unresolved until the exact intended use has been assessed.
