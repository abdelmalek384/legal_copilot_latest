## Source hierarchy for this workstream

1. **Official Gazette / Amiri Press text or original Gazette scan** — primary legal text. Record issue, date, instrument number, exact article, printed page and stable URL. Searchable text is useful for comparison, but does not prove the PDF bytes were downloaded or visually checked.
2. **Official Ministry of Labour publications** — useful for official copies, implementation guides and public-facing explanations. A guide/FAQ is explanatory material, not a substitute for the enacted text.
3. **Official government text for related instruments** — Amiri Press search pages for Cabinet, Labour Ministry and Ministry of Justice decisions. Each instrument must be checked for publication, effective date, amendments, repeal and scope.
4. **Institutional repositories** — Amiri Press / Egyptian Knowledge Bank catalogue metadata can identify issue/date/document, even where the PDF is request-a-copy only. Catalogue metadata proves the listing, not the contents of a locked PDF.
5. **Secondary transcriptions** — Manshurat and other public legal repositories can locate articles and reveal possible OCR/transcription conflicts. Use as a comparison lead only; resolve conflicts against primary text before promoting a rule.
6. **International/legal indexes** — FAOLEX and Library of Congress summaries can help discover enactment/effective dates and identify repeal context. They are secondary metadata/summaries, not the primary Egyptian legal source.

## Actionable alternative workflow

1. **Build a source inventory before importing rules.** Capture instrument number/year, issuing authority, official URL, publication date/issue/page when known, effective date, amendment/repeal relation, access mode, retrieval date, and a content hash only when the bytes were actually downloaded.
2. **Use searchable official text for provisional comparison.** Extract each article/instrument into a separate candidate source file with URL and retrieval timestamp. Preserve original Arabic text; do not silently normalize or repair OCR.
3. **Cross-check against an independent public transcription.** Flag exact token differences and retain both snippets. Do not resolve a conflict by majority vote or by choosing the more plausible wording.
4. **Track time-bounded instruments as timelines.** For remote-work decisions, create one timeline entry per decision and explicitly mark months covered. No inference that a September extension applies in October.
5. **Separate legal text from explanatory publications.** Ministry FAQs, guides and international summaries may help answer discovery questions but must be labelled explanatory/secondary and must not override an article.
6. **Treat Gazette catalogue-only items as leads.** Record that the issue/document is listed, but leave instrument text and legal effect unresolved until a public text is found or an authorized copy becomes available. Do not bypass request-a-copy access controls.
7. **Run currentness checks on each high-impact rule.** Search later instruments by number, issuing authority, relevant article, and repeal/amendment language; retain unresolved gaps rather than treating search absence as proof no amendment exists.
8. **Keep release gates hard.** No candidate rule becomes authoritative merely because two websites agree. Require primary-source pinpoint, source-use assessment, currentness review, contradiction resolution, and qualified Egyptian-lawyer approval for release.
