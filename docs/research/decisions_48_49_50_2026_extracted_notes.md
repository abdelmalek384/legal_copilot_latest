## Decision 48/2026 — workplace nurseries

The decision's eight articles concern nurseries affiliated with workplaces.

- Employers using 100 or more female workers at one location must establish a nursery or contract with one to care for workers' children under four.
- Establishments employing fewer than 100 women within a 500-metre radius must cooperate to establish a nursery or contract with a licensed nursery.
- Nursery premises must be close to the workplace where possible, dedicated to childcare, accessible to children with disabilities, and separated from pollution/noise sources.
- The employer must obtain a nursery licence from the Ministry of Social Solidarity.
- Worker contributions are stated as 4% of wage for the first child, 3% for a second child concurrently, 2% for a third child concurrently; actual costs after the third child are borne by the female worker.
- If establishing a nursery is not feasible, the employer may pay childcare costs to a licensed nursery chosen/used by the worker.
- Article 8 says the decision takes effect the day after publication.

## Decision 49/2026 — seven additional annual-leave days

- Article 1 provides a seven-day increase in paid annual leave for workers in hazardous, arduous, health-damaging work and remote areas.
- Article 2 enumerates covered technical work and industrial sectors, including chemical and petrochemical production, metals/heavy engineering, high-risk food processing, textiles/dyeing, electronic manufacturing, extractive industries, energy/radiation, waste/environmental management, agriculture/research, healthcare exposure, and construction.
- Article 3 lists remote areas, including North Sinai, South Sinai, Red Sea, Matrouh, New Valley, Toshka and surrounding areas, East Oweinat, the area from Zafarana/Ras Gharib to the Egyptian-Sudanese border in the south around Halayeb with a five-kilometre depth, and newly designated industrial zones.
- Article 4 requires the competent administrations to review the covered work and areas periodically and update them at least once every three years.
- Article 5 says the decision takes effect the day after publication.

The detailed industry list is extensive and should be represented as article/table-level source chunks only after the complete annex is captured and lawyer-reviewed.

## Decision 50/2026 — employment and training of children

The decision runs from Gazette pages 13–24 and contains 18 articles plus annex tables.

Key extracted rules:
- Art. 1: a child is a person under 18; age is proved by birth certificate, national ID, or another official document.
- Art. 2: employing a child is prohibited before completion of basic education or age 15, whichever is later. Vocational training may begin at age 14, subject to the decision's safeguards and no assignment of work during training.
- Art. 3: prohibits employment/training in work harmful to physical or mental health, safety or morals, work obstructing education, unlawful work, and worst forms of child labour under ILO Convention 182.
- Art. 4: prohibits employment/training under 18 in work, professions, industries and environments listed in Annex 1, as well as work whose nature or circumstances may endanger health, safety or morals.
- Art. 5: permitted child work is capped at six hours daily; one or more breaks totalling at least one hour are required, arranged so the child does not work more than four continuous hours. Overtime, weekly rest-day work and public-holiday work are prohibited. Work between 7 p.m. and 7 a.m. is prohibited. Child workers receive seven additional annual-leave days, which cannot be split, postponed or withheld.
- Art. 6: initial medical examination before work/training, at employer expense.
- Art. 7: periodic medical examination at least annually and at end of service; health card and records are required.
- Art. 8: workplace OSH requirements include ventilation, lighting, clean water, sanitation, changing/food/rest facilities and first aid; safeguards include protection from exploitation and separation of boys and girls.
- Art. 9: employer provides necessary treatment and insurance against work injury/occupational disease, and arranges safe transport between residence and workplace.
- Art. 10: employer explains work hazards and prevention, supplies suitable personal protective equipment and trains the child to use it.
- Art. 11: employer supplies a healthy daily meal without charge, unless a better workplace food system exists; cash substitution is not allowed.
- Art. 12: employer must post the child-work rules, notify the labour office of each child's details and work, maintain a child worker/training register, retain official age/health documents, issue training cards to trainees under 15, and provide suitable separate accommodation where accommodation is provided. A child may not sleep at the workplace.
- Art. 13: vocational rehabilitation bodies notify labour offices about trained children with disabilities; labour offices keep records and assist with suitable placements.
- Art. 14: prohibits exploitation and requires the child's best interests to be considered.
- Art. 15: parents/guardians may not employ a child contrary to the Labour Law and this decision.
- Art. 16: labour inspectors must continuously inspect and monitor child employment/training and treatment.
- Art. 17: after consultation, additional prohibited work may be added to Annex 1; Annex 1 must be reviewed at least every five years or when needed.
- Art. 18: decision takes effect the day after publication.
- Annex 1 contains prohibited environments, activities and industries, including hazardous heat, radiation, explosives, dangerous machinery, electricity, heavy loads, mining/quarrying, hazardous waste, domestic service, pressurized gases, transport, cargo handling, and specified tasks across agriculture, entertainment, tourism, vehicle repair, textile/garment/leather, and food processing.
