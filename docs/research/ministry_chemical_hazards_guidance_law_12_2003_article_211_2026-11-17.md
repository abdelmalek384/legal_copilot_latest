# Ministry chemical-hazards guidance — Law 12/2003 Article 211 cross-reference

Research date: 17 November 2026  
Source: Ministry of Labour, Central Administration for Occupational Safety and Health and Work Environment Protection, General Administration for Work Environment Protection, *Health Effects of Harmful Chemicals* (Arabic title: التأثيرات الصحية للمواد الكيميائية الضارة).  
PDF: https://www.labour.gov.eg/media/2kppyusg/التأثيرات-الضارة-للمواد-الكيميائية.pdf  
Date printed in the presentation: 30 August 2023.  
Source class: official Ministry technical/inspector guidance presentation; not a signed decision, Gazette text, or independently verified exposure-limit standard.

## 1. Critical numbering distinction

The guide's inspector-enforcement footer expressly cites **Article 211 of Labour Law 12/2003** for chemical-risk controls. This is not Ministerial Decision 211/2003 on physical, mechanical, biological and chemical hazards. Keep the two references separate:

- Law 12/2003, Article 211: chemical-risk duties of the establishment and branches.
- Ministerial Decision 211/2003: separate instrument; Ministry guides cite its Articles 2–3, 5–6, 10–13 and 32 for other hazard-specific provisions.

The guide says the establishment must:
1. Keep worker exposure below the maximum permissible concentration for chemicals and carcinogenic substances.
2. Keep stored quantities of hazardous chemicals below each substance's threshold quantity.
3. Provide precautions for transport, storage, handling, use and disposal of hazardous chemicals/waste.
4. Maintain an inventory register of hazardous chemicals with substance data and a workplace-exposure monitoring record.
5. Label every chemical used at work with scientific and commercial names, chemical composition, hazard level, safety precautions and emergency measures; obtain relevant information from the supplier.
6. Train workers in safe handling of hazardous chemicals/carcinogens, their risks and prevention methods.

This is a useful Ministry summary of the statutory duties. It does **not** supply the maximum permissible concentration schedule or threshold-quantity table needed to operationalize the law.

## 2. Chemical categories and exposure routes

The presentation groups harmful chemicals into:
- heavy metals;
- dusts;
- gases;
- organic solvents;
- vapours/fumes.

It describes entry routes as inhalation, skin absorption, ingestion and injection. It discusses effects on respiratory, nervous, circulatory, digestive and reproductive systems, and identifies silica/asbestos dust, lead, manganese, iron, solvents, ammonia, chlorine, nitrogen oxides, hydrogen sulphide and carbon monoxide as examples.

These are values printed in the guidance presentation. The source's test method, temperature/pressure conditions and standard reference are not provided in the extracted text. Do not use these as a substitute for a current gas-detection standard or a confined-space permit criterion.

## 5. Dust and respirator table — unit and layout caution

The guide includes a table whose heading refers to dust concentration in micrograms per cubic metre and respirator types, while the adjacent header text mentions overcoming a concentration of 50 mg/m³. The extracted rows are:

| Dust concentration as printed | Respiratory protection as described |
|---:|---|
| ≤500 µg/m³ | Air-purifying respirator with high-efficiency filter |
| ≤1,250 µg/m³ | High-efficiency air-purifying respirator or helmet with continuous-flow system |
| ≤2,500 µg/m³ | Full-face, tightly sealed respirator with high-efficiency filter |
| ≤50,000 µg/m³ | Air-purifying half-mask operating with pressure on demand |
| ≤100,000 µg/m³ | Air-purifying respirator with face covering, pressure on demand |
| Unknown area, concentration may reach 500,000 µg/m³ | Full-face supplied-air respirator operating with pressure on demand |

The extracted table is not internally self-explanatory (all rows use “less than or equal to” thresholds, while the accompanying 50 mg/m³ header and respirator classes need validation). Do not convert this table into a PPE selection rule. Check the original slide image, the referenced respirator standard and the substance-specific exposure limit before use.

## 6. Control hierarchy

The presentation depicts the hierarchy of controls:
1. Elimination.
2. Substitution.
3. Engineering controls (including ventilation and isolation).
4. Administrative controls (job rotation, housekeeping, permits to work, training and documentation).
5. Personal protective equipment as the last line of defence.

It also discusses workplace risk assessment, exposure measurement, incident/illness records, safety data sheets (MSDS), and training.
