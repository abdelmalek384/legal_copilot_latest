### Article map

- **Article 1:** Council may form specialist subcommittees from members or non-members; membership should preserve balanced tripartite representation among government, employers' organizations and trade unions.
- **Article 2:** Council powers include:
  1. Setting national minimum wages across sectors, considering worker/family needs, living costs, balance between parties and productivity.
  2. Reviewing the minimum wage at least annually, or as needed, based on economic/social conditions, inflation, prices and living costs.
  3. Setting the minimum periodic annual increment, not less than 3% of insured wage, and rules for payment under the Labour Law.
  4. Reviewing employer requests to reduce or exempt the periodic annual increment for emergency economic circumstances; decision within 30 days after referral to the Council, under Council criteria and balancing both parties' interests.
  5. Notifying relevant ministries of acceptance/rejection of reduction/exemption requests.
  6. Studying wage structures by sector/profession/activity and proposing solutions, including attention to vulnerable groups and low-wage regions.
  7. Studying international/regional wage standards and recommendations and relevant economic statistics.
  8. Approving publication of periodic detailed reports explaining economic/social data and consultations used in wage reviews.
  9. Developing a monitoring system for economic/social effects of minimum-wage decisions, including employment, inflation, poverty and living standards.
- **Article 3:** Council meets at least every six months or when needed; quorum is a majority; decisions by majority of attending members; chair has casting vote on tie.
- **Article 4:** Planning and Economic Development Minister establishes the technical secretariat, chaired by a Ministry representative not below Director-General level, with four specialists from Planning and Labour.
- **Article 5:** Secretariat monitors economic/social/labour indicators, prepares studies and impact assessments, monitors implementation with the Ministry of Labour, receives and examines employer requests to reduce/exempt the periodic increment, notifies employers of decisions by registered letter or electronic means, prepares draft decisions/agendas/minutes, maintains records, and reports to the Council. It meets at least every three months; chair submits a semiannual report.
- **Article 6:** Council chair sets meeting allowances for Council/subcommittee/secretariat members within legal rules and with Finance Ministry approval.
- **Article 7:** repeals Prime Minister Decision 2659/2020 and conflicting provisions.
- **Article 8:** published in the Official Gazette and effective the day after publication.

### Critical distinction

Article 2(4)–(5) concerns employer requests to reduce or be exempt from the **periodic annual increment** for emergency economic circumstances. It does not itself create an exemption from the national minimum wage. Do not conflate the two.

### Article map — all eight articles

1. **Article 1:** Council may form specialist subcommittees from members or external persons; composition must balance government, employer organizations and trade unions.
2. **Article 2:** nine functions: national minimum-wage setting; annual minimum-wage review at least once yearly; periodic annual increment floor of 3% of insured wage; employer requests for reduction/exemption in emergency economic circumstances (decision within 30 days); notification of relevant ministries; wage-structure studies; international wage standards; publication of supporting reports; impact monitoring of employment, inflation, poverty and living standards.
3. **Article 3:** Council meets at least every six months or when needed; majority quorum and voting; chair has casting vote on tie.
4. **Article 4:** Minister of Planning and Economic Development forms the technical secretariat, chaired by a Ministry representative at Director-General level or above, with four specialists from Planning and Labour.
5. **Article 5:** eleven secretariat functions, including monitoring economic/social/labour indicators, studies, impact assessment, implementation monitoring, receiving/examining employer exemption requests, notices, draft decisions, agendas/minutes and records. Secretariat meets at least quarterly; its chair submits a semiannual report.
6. **Article 6:** Council chair sets attendance allowances for Council, subcommittee and secretariat members within legal rules and with Ministry of Finance approval.
7. **Article 7:** expressly repeals PM Decision 2659/2020 and conflicting provisions.
8. **Article 8:** publication in Official Gazette; effective the day after publication.

### Composition / repeal reconciliation

Labour Law 14/2025 Article 101 itself specifies the National Council for Wages composition, including the tripartite representation and named public bodies. PM Decision 3165/2026 therefore governs the Council's work system and functions, not the full membership list. Its repeal of PM Decision 2659/2020 removes the prior PM-level composition/competency instrument; the new statutory Article 101 is the current composition anchor. Do not retain PM2659/2020 as current composition authority.
