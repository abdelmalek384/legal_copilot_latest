# Supreme Constitutional Court — public-service salary and social-insurance judgments

**Snapshot:** 17 November 2026  
**Source:** official Supreme Constitutional Court judgment HTML indexed on https://www.sccourt.gov.eg/

## 1. Case98/43 Judicial Year2026 — Law18/2015 Article61

Official judgment locator: https://www.sccourt.gov.eg/DjangoPortalResources/Rules_Html/17937_43_98_1_2.html

- Session: 7 March 2026.
- Challenged: Article61 of Civil Service Law18/2015 and Article64 of Civil Service Law81/2016.
- Facts: a tax employee was held in pretrial detention from 6 September 2013 to 21 December 2016 and was finally acquitted. He sought the half salary withheld during detention.
- The SCC had already ruled on Law81/2016 Article64 in case100/43 (4 November 2023), so it treated that part as already decided.
- The Court declared Law18/2015 Article61 unconstitutional insofar as it deprives an employee held in pretrial detention of half salary for the detention period when criminal responsibility is excluded by a final judgment or unappealable judicial decision.
- The judgment reasons from the presumption of innocence, property, right to compensation for pretrial detention, access to courts and public-service protections.
- The Court notes that Law18/2015 operated from 12 March 2015 to 20 January 2016 following parliamentary treatment of the presidential decree-law; the ruling therefore concerns historical salary claims during that validity period.

**Publication/effective date:** the exact Gazette issue and publication date for case98/43 were not retrieved in this pass. Do not assume the date from the session date.

## 2. Case100/43 Judicial Year2023 — Law81/2016 Article64(1)

Official judgment locator: https://www.sccourt.gov.eg/SCC/faces/Rules_Html/14082_43_100_1_2.html?timestamp=1708046420398

- Session/publication: 4 November 2023.
- Challenged: first paragraph of Article64 of Civil Service Law81/2016.
- Facts: an employee of the Ministry of Awqaf sought half salary withheld during pretrial detention after a final acquittal.
- Holding: Article64(1) is unconstitutional only insofar as it deprives an employee held in pretrial detention of half salary for the detention period where criminal responsibility is excluded by final judgment or unappealable judicial decision.
- The Court grounds the result in equality, justice, property, presumption of innocence, access to courts, defense and constitutional compensation for pretrial detention.
- The judgment does not invalidate the entire Article64 or all salary suspension rules.

**Publication and effect:** Official Gazette issue44 (repeated), 4 November 2023. The Court fixed the day following publication as the date of effects: 5 November 2023.

## 3. Case188/35 Judicial Year2023 — Law79/1975 Article20(2) and Table9

Official judgment locator: https://www.sccourt.gov.eg/SCC/faces/Rules_Html/13552_35_188_1_2.html?timestamp=1708041510138

- Session: 8 July 2023.
- Challenged: Article20(2) of Social Insurance Law79/1975, as replaced by Law130/2009, and attached Table9.
- Facts: 17 former employees who retired early sought recalculation of pensions. The challenged provision used a different actuarial factor for early retirement than for other insured persons.
- Holding: Article20(2) was declared unconstitutional; Table9 fell with it because it was inseparable from the invalid provision.
- The Court reasoned that persons facing the same insured risk (end of service) must be treated using a unified actuarial factor; the differing factor for early-retirement pensioners violated equality and social justice.
- The judgment itself notes that Law79/1975 was later repealed by Law148/2019, but repeal does not prevent review of provisions applied to historical legal positions during their period of force.

**Temporal effect:** the judgment specifies the day following publication, without retroactive disturbance of settled legal positions. The exact Gazette issue/date and effective day have not yet been retrieved. A correction order is dated 1 August 2023; do not mistake that correction date for the original publication date.

## 4. Combined currentness rules for the product

1. **Civil Service Law81/2016 Art64(1):** apply the limited constitutional invalidity from 5 November 2023 to cases within its exact scope.
2. **Civil Service Law18/2015 Art61:** apply case98/43 only to the historical period in which Law18/2015 was in force, subject to the judgment's publication/effective date once verified.
3. **Civil Service Law81/2016 Art69(6):** separate case99/43 (8 March 2025) limited invalidity and case2/44 (2026) finality; do not conflate with salary-under-detention cases.
4. **Social Insurance Law79/1975 Art20(2) and Table9:** historical only following Law148/2019 repeal, but the constitutional invalidity remains material for pension claims arising under the old regime.
5. A constitutional judgment must be linked to the exact statutory version, publication date, effect date, transitional period and final operative wording. A general tag such as “unconstitutional” is insufficient.
