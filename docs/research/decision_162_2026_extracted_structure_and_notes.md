## High-value provisions extracted

### Establishments covered / internal regulation content
- Art. 2: every employer with 10 or more workers must prepare a workplace regulation tailored to its activity.
- Required subjects include job advertising/selection/recruitment, training, contracts, service files, performance evaluation, promotion, transfers/assignments, wage protection, hours/rest/overtime, work patterns/digital transformation, leave, safe work environment including disability, termination, worker duties/investigation, violations and disciplinary sanctions.
- Art. 3 requires inclusion of benefits already actually granted before preparation, whether arising from internal decisions, approved regulations, contracts, or settled practice.
- Arts. 7–9: submission to competent Labour Directorate/Relations Office; union gets copy within three working days; union has 15 days to comment (silence is treated as approval); Directorate reviews and may object in writing where law is breached or existing benefits reduced; employer has up to five working days to address objections; if no ratification or objection within 30 days from receipt, the regulation is treated as effective from the following day.
- Art. 10 establishes a central Ministry committee to unify principles and resolve differences over workplace regulations.

### Worker protections / internal rules
- Art. 14: workplace regulation supplements the Labour Law, its implementing decisions, and individual/collective contracts; where provisions conflict, the more favourable worker benefit applies.
- Art. 15: requires safeguards against forced/compulsory labour and expressly addresses withholding national ID/passport, restricting movement, unlawful excessive hours and unlawful wage deductions.
- Art. 16: requires prevention of harassment, violence and bullying, complaint/investigation mechanisms and stronger sanction where the offender is a supervisor.
- Arts. 17–19: anti-discrimination and protection of worker rights/acquired benefits (inspect full text before extracting each exact ground).
- Art. 21 (as reflected in secondary explanatory guide): probation capped at three months and may occur only once with the same employer. Verify exact article text in the Gazette before using this as a standalone rule.

### HR / wage / working conditions
- Arts. 28–34: define roles, job classification, objective performance evaluation, promotion, and limits on transfer/secondment/assignment; transfers should not reduce wage or grade.
- Art. 35: wage system must align with job grade, skill and competence, ensure equal pay for men and women for work of equal value, and provide periodic increases no less than statutory minimum.
- Arts. 36–37: wage-protection safeguards, lawful deduction/attachment limits, and payment methods.
- Arts. 38–41: work hours, rest, shift schedules, flexible/remote work, leave, overtime and work on rest days/public holidays.
- Art. 42: requires OSH rules for workers and establishment, including suitable emergency/evacuation arrangements for workers with disabilities.
- Arts. 43–45: internal dispute resolution, communication, complaints and grievances.

### Investigation / discipline
- Art. 46: alleged violations must relate to work; investigation starts within seven days of discovery and ordinarily concludes within three months unless new evidence appears; no sanction after 30 days from conclusion; wage deduction for one violation is capped at five days' basic wage and total deductions for sanctions in one month cannot exceed five days' basic wage.
- Art. 47: written investigation, hearing the worker and considering defence; oral investigation may be used for warning or deduction of no more than one day's basic wage, with its content recorded in the sanction decision.
- Art. 48: disciplinary dismissal only by judgment of the competent labour court for gross error, after lower sanctions are exhausted where the nature of the violation allows.
- Arts. 49–58: disciplinary schedule, graduated sanctions, notice to worker, repeat violation within one year, and division of sanctioning authority.
- Art. 58: labour court has exclusive jurisdiction to impose dismissal; employer/delegate imposes other sanctions; establishment manager may impose warning and deduction up to three days.
- Art. 59: financial sanctions must be recorded in a paper/electronic register and their proceeds placed in a separate account for workers' social, cultural and sports purposes.

### Termination / union protection
- Art. 60 lists separate termination events: expiry of fixed term or task, notice-based termination, written mutual agreement, death or incapacity, deemed resignation by absence, statutory resignation, dismissal by labour-court judgment, retirement age, and final conviction in specified offences subject to the court's suspension-of-sentence rule.
- Art. 61 requires separate treatment of each termination ground; do not mix deemed and express resignation, resignation and notice, or termination and disciplinary dismissal.
- Art. 62 prohibits inserting a notice period for resignation into the workplace regulation; notice may be agreed only for mutual termination/settlement.
- Art. 63 restricts seeking dismissal of a worker because of union activity unless the establishment proves the dismissal request is unrelated to that activity.
