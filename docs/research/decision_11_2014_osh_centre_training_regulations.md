# Decision 11/2014 — National OSH Centre training regulations (full public Gazette transcription)

Research date: 17 November 2026  
Instrument: Ministerial Decision 11/2014 amending the training regulations of the National Centre for Occupational Safety and Health Studies and Work Environment Protection.  
Issuer: Minister of Manpower and Migration / Chair of the Centre's Board.  
Signed: 3 August 2014.  
Gazette: Egyptian Gazette (الوقائع المصرية), issue 226, year 187, Wednesday 8 October 2014.  
Text locator: https://www.laweg.net/framePlain.aspx?ItemID=92501&NID=112727&Type=6&action=ViewActivePages  
Source class: publicly accessible third-party transcription displaying the Gazette heading and 22 operative articles, including fee tables. This is not the original signed PDF or a licence for commercial reuse.

## 1. Instrument structure and operative contents

The transcription contains 22 articles across seven chapters. It regulates the National Centre's training operations, programme fees, trainee eligibility, delivery, assessment, trainers, staff remuneration and expenditure.

### Chapter I — General rules (Articles 1–2)

**Article 1 — programmes and fees.** The Centre organizes training for OSH and work-environment personnel using a table of programme type, duration, fee and trainee conditions. The published 2014 table states:

| Basic OSH technician | 4 weeks | EGP 600 | USD 600 |
| Basic OSH committee member | 2 weeks | EGP 300 | USD 300 |
| Advanced OSH specialist | 3 weeks | EGP 600 | USD 600 |
| Advanced OSH technician | 3 weeks | EGP 600 | USD 600 |
| Advanced OSH committee member | 2 weeks | EGP 400 | USD 400 |

For a private in-establishment programme, the text states a fee equal to 60% of the table value, while the establishment bears the other specified delivery expenses (lecturer/trainer/supervisor compensation, printing, travel, transport and accommodation) under an agreement. These are **historical 2014 amounts**, not current fees.

**Article 2** allows the Centre to design special programmes for establishments after consultation on objectives, trainees, duration, topics, financing and execution plan, subject to the stated internal approvals.

### Chapter II — Programme planning and seminars (Articles 3–5)

- **Art. 3:** the Centre prepares an annual programme plan with proposed courses, numbers and dates; the Board approves it and the Centre publishes it.
- **Art. 4:** normally 15–40 trainees per programme; evening delivery is allowed when needed; a smaller group may be accepted if costs can be covered.
- **Art. 5:** the Centre conducts or participates in seminars, conferences and discussion sessions to promote OSH culture.

### Chapter III — Trainee eligibility (Articles 6–7)

- **Art. 6:** OSH personnel candidate conditions are tied to Ministerial Decision 263/2010. Where a qualification is not expressly specified, admission depends on qualification, level and experience, with Centre Director approval and later Board presentation.
- **Art. 7:** non-Egyptian trainees may be admitted to programmes suited to their qualifications, experience, level and responsibilities after paying the listed fees.

### Chapter IV — Programme delivery (Articles 8–13)

- **Art. 8:** each programme has an administrative supervisor and a technical supervisor.
- **Art. 9:** the daily schedule is approved by the Centre Director; daily training may not exceed six hours.
- **Art. 10:** training, discussion, observations, research and examinations are in Arabic; English may be used for specialized technical curricula when the requesting entity wants it.
- **Art. 11:** assessment occurs during and at the end of programmes, by reference to Decision 263/2010. A trainee with attendance below 80% of total training hours is barred from examinations. Failed or missed examinations may be repeated for a fee equal to 20% of the programme fee.
- **Art. 12:** one completion certificate is issued without charge; a replacement certificate costs EGP 75; an English certificate on request also costs EGP 75.
- **Art. 13:** certificate design follows the model approved by the committee formed under Ministerial Decision 248/2003; the Centre Board may approve updates.

### Chapter V — Lecturers, trainers and supervisors (Articles 14–16)

- **Art. 14:** lecturers/trainers are selected by Centre Board decision from current/former Centre researchers, Ministry personnel and qualified national experts; CVs and supporting qualifications/experience documents are required.
- **Art. 15:** the administrative supervisor is normally a training specialist in the General Training Department; the technical supervisor is a Centre technical employee at least at senior researcher grade. The text also specifies advance notification of lecture topics and submission of teaching material.
- **Art. 16:** lecturers/trainers submit printed and electronic course material at least one month before the training year. Specialist committees review it, the Centre Director approves it, and it is distributed to trainees at the start of each programme.

### Chapter VI — Training remuneration and programme administration (Articles 17–19)

- **Art. 17:** sets historical remuneration for lecturers/trainers, research evaluators, general programme supervision, regional branch management, technical/administrative supervisors, programme secretariat, finance review, library support, signage design, audiovisual support, maintenance, service staff, cashier/driver, examiners and invigilators. The amounts are per hour, week, programme, trainee, paper or other specified unit; do not normalize them into a single flat fee.
- **Art. 18:** requires a trainee feedback survey under a Centre Director-approved form.
- **Art. 19:** seminar and discussion-session costs are charged to the training budget.

### Chapter VII — Spending authority (Articles 20–22)

- **Art. 20:** the Centre Director allocates remaining training-budget amounts and annual-course revenue above target to education development, training rooms, Board compensation and distinguished training staff, within the stated scope.
- **Art. 21:** conflicting provisions are repealed.
- **Art. 22:** publication in the Egyptian Gazette and application from publication during the 2014/2015 training year.

## 2. Cross-references and source-text discrepancies

The preamble cites:
- Presidential Decision 333/2003 reorganizing the National OSH Studies Centre.
- Labour Law 12/2003.
- Ministerial Decision 134/2003 on OSH units/committees and training.
- **Ministerial Decision 25/2003** amending Article 18 of Decision 134/2003.
- Ministerial Decision 263/2010 on OSH training curricula and the licensing/conditions for training providers.

The Article 1 fee-table trainee-condition cells repeatedly cite **Decision 623/2010**, while the preamble and Article 6 cite **Decision 263/2010**. Preserve this literal discrepancy. Do not silently change 623 to 263; compare the Gazette scan and underlying instruments.

A separate Ministry legislation-index crawl lists **Decision 25/2010** with a similar Article 18 amendment description. The 2014 Gazette transcription, however, explicitly cites **Decision 25/2003**. The register therefore keeps 25/2003 and 25/2010 as distinct compound keys pending source resolution.

## 3. Relationship to Decisions 232/2010 and 216/2016

- Decision 232/2010 is described in public metadata as amending Articles 18 and 19 of Decision 134/2003.
- Decision 216/2016's three-article Gazette transcription replaces the first paragraph of Article 18 of Decision 134/2003, naming later OSH training institutions and basic/advanced/specific/specialized training.
- Decision 11/2014 governs the Centre's internal training programme administration and fee/remuneration arrangements, and its preamble cites 134/2003, 25/2003 and 263/2010.
- Decision 249/2026 is the later Law 14/2025-era OSH functional-units/committees instrument. Its interaction with the historic training-centre rules must be checked clause by clause; do not infer wholesale repeal.
