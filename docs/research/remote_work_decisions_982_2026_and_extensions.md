# Remote-Work Decision Series — Prime Minister Decision 982/2026 and Monthly Extensions

Research date: 17 November 2026  
Status: structured legal research note; not lawyer-reviewed; rights not cleared.

## Executive finding

Prime Minister Decision 982/2026 created a time-limited Sunday remote-work arrangement for April 2026. It was extended month by month through September by five further Prime Minister decisions: 1305/2026 (May), 1572/2026 (June), 2033/2026 (July), 2377/2026 (August) and 2590/2026 (September).

On 30 September 2026, the Cabinet officially reported approval of a **draft decision to stop applying Decision 982/2026**. The Cabinet source confirms approval of the draft, but the numbered final Prime Minister decision and its Gazette publication were not located in this pass. Therefore, record the Cabinet approval as an official government decision-stage announcement, not as the final signed repeal instrument. Contemporary reporting says the weekly Sunday arrangement was to stop from October, but obtain the final signed instrument before asserting the exact legal repeal/effective date.

- **Date/publication:** 2 April 2026; Official Gazette issue 14 supplement.
- **Official Amiri Press indexed text:** https://alamiria.com/Sec/TashTxt?id=X+ynf8GR0jI%3D
- **Official DSpace Gazette record:** https://dspace.id.com.eg/items/1255d539-9ac2-491c-9c03-a7330ff891b8

### Article-by-article extraction

**Preamble:** cites the Constitution; Industrial Establishments Labour Regulation Law 133/1961; Child Law 12/1996; Rights of Persons with Disabilities Law 10/2018 and its Executive Regulations; Law 149/2019 on civil-society work; and Labour Law 14/2025 and its implementing decisions. The decision was issued on the Minister of Labour's proposal and after Cabinet approval.

**Article 1 — scope and employer duty**
- Applies to all establishments and entities subject to Labour Law 14/2025.
- Natural-person and legal-person employers must implement its provisions.

**Article 2 — covered activities and Sunday remote work**
- For April 2026, the listed establishments/entities had to apply remote work on every Sunday for all their employees, starting Sunday 5 April 2026, provided workflow was not adversely affected:
  1. Telecommunications and information-technology establishments, subject to the detailed activity exclusions below.
  2. Financial and accounting services.
  3. Marketing, media and digitization.
  4. Real-estate services.
  5. Entities carrying out remote-training operations.
  6. Non-service civil associations and foundations.
  7. Offices of trade unions, labour federations and employers' federations.
- The IT/telecommunications category excludes outsourcing, call-centre services, business-process management, software/electronics design, production and development, and technology activities whose nature requires physical attendance for operation, maintenance, technical security, operation/management of technical infrastructure or data centres, or direct/indirect field technical support.
- The second paragraph extends the arrangement to other sectors/activities for employees in administrative jobs, works and departments that do not affect workflow. Examples expressly include administrative/office services, human resources, accounts, and legal affairs.

**Article 3 — excluded sectors / staffing by operational need**
- Without prejudice to Article 2's second paragraph, workers in the following establishments/entities are excluded from the decision's application and are operated according to need:
  - service sectors;
  - health sector;
  - transport sector;
  - infrastructure (drinking water, sanitation, petroleum and natural-gas stations, electricity, etc.);
  - industrial and productive sectors and their auxiliary services;
  - university and pre-university education (non-governmental universities and schools).
- Do not misread this as a universal right to work remotely for all employees or all sectors.

**Article 4 — preservation of better conditions**
- The decision does not prejudice better rights, wages, benefits or operational systems already established for covered workers, whether arising from legislation, regulations, systems/agreements, decisions or employment contracts.

**Article 5 — technical committee and monitoring**
- A technical committee chaired by the Minister of Labour, with representatives from the Ministries of Communications and Information Technology, Social Solidarity, Education and Technical Education, Higher Education and Scientific Research, Investment and Foreign Trade, and Industry.
- It monitors implementation and prepares a weekly report of its work, recommendations and implementation mechanisms. Its chair submits the report to the Prime Minister for any necessary decisions.

**Article 6 — publication and commencement**
- Published in the Official Gazette; competent authorities implement it.
- Effective from the day after publication.
- Signed at the Cabinet on 2 April 2026.

## 3. Cabinet approval to stop the Sunday remote-work arrangement

- **Official Cabinet source:** https://www.cabinet.gov.eg/Meeting/Details/11910
- **Meeting date:** 30 September 2026.
- The Cabinet's own report says it approved a **draft Prime Minister decision** to stop applying Decision 982/2026, which provided remote work every Sunday for the employees of the establishments and entities specified in that decision.
- This is strong official evidence of the Cabinet's decision-stage action and explains why the September extension was the last extension identified in this series.
- **Unresolved:** the numbered final Prime Minister decision, signed text, Gazette issue/date and exact commencement clause were not located in this retrieval pass. Keep the final cessation instrument as a source-acquisition task.
- Contemporary press reports state the arrangement stopped from October 2026, but these are not a substitute for the signed final instrument.

## 4. Ministry of Labour private-sector monitoring circular — April 2026

- **Official IDSC newsletter:** https://idsc.gov.eg/upload/NewsLetter/AttachmentA/13536/الإثنين%206%20إبريل%C2%A02026-%20عدد%20رقم%201271-%20السنة%20السادسة.html
- The newsletter says the Minister of Labour announced a circular directing Labour Directorate heads to monitor implementation of Decision 982 in private-sector establishments during April.
- Monitoring tasks included checking application in establishments, recording obstacles and inquiries, collecting data on sectors with higher/lower compliance and reasons for non-compliance.
- The newsletter does not disclose a circular number, full text, signed copy or date beyond the April 2026 announcement. Do not invent a circular number or convert this monitoring notice into an ongoing obligation after the April period.

## 5. Legal-data implementation controls

1. Model Decision 982 and each monthly extension as separate instruments with explicit valid-from/valid-through periods.
2. The September extension ends 30 September 2026; do not infer October continuation from the September decision.
3. The 30 September Cabinet source is approval of a draft cessation decision, not itself the final signed instrument. Store its status as `cabinet_approved_draft_final_instrument_not_retrieved`.
4. Do not infer that Article 2 creates a permanent individual right to remote work; the arrangement was sector/activity-specific, Sunday-specific and time-limited.
5. Preserve Article 4's protection of better existing worker rights/benefits and Article 3's exclusions.
6. Keep the April private-sector monitoring circular separate from the Prime Minister decisions.
