## 1. Case 2/44 Judicial Year 2026 — Civil Service Law Art69(6)

**Official full judgment:** https://www.sccourt.gov.eg/DjangoPortalResources/Rules_Html/18013_44_2_1_2.html  
**Session:** Saturday 4 April 2026.

### Subject
The case concerned termination of a public employee's service for absence without permission for 30 non-consecutive days in a year, under Article69(6) of Civil Service Law81/2016, and the implementing Article176 of PM1216/2017.

### Material facts and procedural posture
The underlying employee had been terminated by the Real Estate Tax Authority under Decision1191/2018 after non-consecutive absence attributed to illness. The Administrative Court referred the constitutional question concerning Article69(6) and Article176.

### Constitutional holding and procedural result
The SCC stated that it had already decided the constitutional issue in case99/43 Judicial Year2025 on 8 March 2025. The earlier ruling held Article69(6) unconstitutional **only to the extent that it failed to allow an employee whose service was terminated for 30 non-consecutive days' absence to submit an excuse for the days for which no excuse had been provided**. The Court rejected the challenge beyond that scope.

The 2026 Court therefore:
- Treated the dispute as ended regarding Article69(6), because the issue had already been conclusively adjudicated.
- Rejected the remaining challenge to Article176 of the Executive Regulation.
- Emphasized the binding effect of SCC judgments under Constitution Article195 and SCC Law48/1979 Articles48–49.

### Effective date
The 2025 judgment was published in Official Gazette issue10 (repeated), 10 March 2025. It fixed the day following publication as the date for its effects: **11 March 2025**.

### Product/legal-research treatment
- Do not state that all of Article69(6) is invalid.
- Preserve the precise unconstitutional omission and the continuing remainder of the provision.
- A post-11 March 2025 termination decision must be analyzed against the amended constitutional rule and any later legislative amendment.
- The 2026 judgment confirms res judicata; it does not create a new substantive invalidity.

## 2. Case 115/25 Judicial Year 2026 — defamation and insult provisions

**Official full judgment:** https://www.sccourt.gov.eg/DjangoPortalResources/Rules_Html/18301_25_115_1_2.html  
**Session:** Saturday 1 August 2026.

### Subject
A journalist challenged Penal Code Articles171, 302, 303 and306 after a criminal complaint alleging online defamation and insult. The underlying prosecution arose from online articles published in June and July 2001.

### Statutory text reproduced in the judgment
The judgment reproduces:
- Article302's definition of defamation, including the good-faith exception for criticism of public officials/public-service persons concerning official duties, subject to proof of truth.
- Article303's monetary penalty ranges for defamation, including the enhanced range where the victim is a public official and the offence relates to the public function.
- Article306's monetary penalty range for insult.

These quoted values are the statutory wording reproduced in the 2026 judgment; they must still be compared with any later Penal Code amendments before being used as current penalty advice.

### Constitutional disposition
- The challenge to Article171 was inadmissible because that provision was not included in the constitutional plea before the referring trial court.
- The challenge to Articles302, 303 and306 was inadmissible because the SCC had already rejected the same constitutional challenge in case16/24 Judicial Year2025, session 1 September 2025.
- The final disposition was that the case was inadmissible.

This is not a new merits ruling on whether the provisions are constitutional; it applies the finality and binding effect of the earlier judgment.

## 3. Case 99/43 Judicial Year 2025 — underlying Art69(6) invalidity

The full underlying judgment page was not directly retrieved in this pass. Its operative holding is quoted by the later official full judgment in case2/44.

The quoted holding:
- Declared Article69(6) of Law81/2016 unconstitutional insofar as it did not authorize an employee whose service was terminated for 30 non-consecutive days' absence to submit an excuse for the absence periods for which no excuse had been provided.
- Rejected the challenge beyond that limited scope.
- Fixed the day after publication as the date for the effects of the judgment.
- Was published in Official Gazette issue10 (repeated), 10 March 2025.

Do not infer that the full article or the entire absence-based termination ground was struck down.

## 4. Case 16/24 Judicial Year 2025 — defamation provisions

The underlying full judgment was not directly retrieved. Its operative outcome is quoted by the later official full judgment in case115/25.

The 2026 judgment states that case16/24, session 1 September 2025, rejected the constitutional challenge to Penal Code Articles302(1) and (3), 303(1) and306, as amended by Laws93/1995 and147/2006. It was published in Official Gazette issue35 bis (A), 3 September 2025.

The 2026 judgment applies the absolute binding effect of the earlier constitutional ruling and treats the same constitutional challenge as already resolved.

## 5. What this first tranche establishes

- Two complete official SCC judgment texts were directly opened: cases2/44 (2026) and115/25 (2026).
- Two earlier judgments (99/43 and16/24) have their operative outcomes quoted in those later official judgments, but their own original pages have not yet been retrieved.
- The judgments demonstrate why case-law ingestion must store exact docket, judicial year, session date, statutory version, operative order, publication date and temporal effect.
- A later judgment may quote an earlier holding; that is not the same as having the earlier judgment's full text.
