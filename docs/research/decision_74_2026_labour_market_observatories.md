## Article-by-article extraction

### Article 1 — central coordinating labour-market observatory

Creates a unit at the Ministry's headquarters named the **Central Coordinating Labour Market Observatory** (المرصد المركزي التنسيقي لسوق العمل).

Its stated purposes are:
1. Provide national labour-market information to support employment-oriented public policy.
2. Coordinate existing regional labour-market observatories and any new ones created as work needs require.
3. Help policymakers align education and training outputs with actual labour-market needs.

### Article 2 — central observatory powers

The central observatory:
- supervises regional labour-market information observatories and monitors their operation;
- collects regional research results, reviews, publishes and preserves them appropriately;
- monitors labour-market changes and the development of required skills and qualifications;
- coordinates field research and collection/updating of necessary information;
- issues and publishes periodic labour-market information reports by sector, geography or nationally;
- coordinates research directions, topics and field research, and supplies research needs with the Ministry's relevant departments and regional observatories;
- approves annual regional research plans and follows their implementation;
- studies expansion of regional observatories in governorates;
- proposes training programmes for regional-observatory and central-observatory members;
- works to provide external funding.

The official index excerpt also describes plans/research for specified worker groups and comprehensive reports on their characteristics, work conditions, challenges and opportunities; obtain the authorized full copy to verify the exact wording and whether this is a separate subparagraph.

### Article 3 — central observatory board

The decision establishes a board of directors for the central observatory, chaired by the Minister of Labour. The searchable text exposes membership categories including:
- the Ministry of Labour;
- relevant government ministries/entities;
- the Small, Medium and Micro Enterprise Development Agency;
- relevant investors' associations or the relevant business association;
- the Federation of Egyptian Industries;
- the Federation of Chambers of Commerce;
- labour trade-union organizations.

The board may seek assistance from persons with practical or technical expertise and university faculty or specialist research centres.

**Extraction gap:** the official search snippet begins the membership enumeration at item 8 and shows items 8–12; the complete list of the first seven members and any exact institutional titles must be checked against the restricted PDF or page images before encoding.

### Article 4 — board powers

The indexed text identifies at least:
1. Approving annual budgets for regional labour-market information observatories, considering prior budgets and current-year needs, and reviewing/discussing budget items.
2. Approving financial regulations for operation of regional labour-market information observatories.

Do not assume there are no additional subparagraphs until the signed full text is checked.

### Article 5 — existing regional observatories

Existing regional labour-market information observatories continue operating. The central coordinating observatory must provide the technical, financial and administrative support needed for their objectives.

Staff seconded to those observatories continue for the periods in their secondment decisions (one or two days per week); those days count as official working days. The relevant Labour Directorate or Ministry issues formal letters confirming the arrangement. Each regional observatory must maintain attendance/presence/absence controls and report them to the central observatory.

### Article 6 — creation of new regional observatories

New governorate-level observatories may be created on the central observatory's proposal. They report administratively and financially to the central observatory. Their location may be:
- the Labour Directorate headquarters;
- an investors' association, as agreed; or
- another location selected by the central observatory in coordination with the parties.

### Article 7 — regional observatory functions and confidentiality

Regional observatories collect and process labour-market data for their governorate. The data types and collection methodology are set every six months by the steering committee after coordination with the central observatory.

They must preserve the confidentiality of information sources and share information in accordance with laws governing data protection and confidentiality. Functions include:
- periodically collecting current local labour-market data on occupational and skill requirements and sectoral changes (industrial, agricultural, service and other sectors);
- processing data into technical reports about labour-market information and needs for the research topic;
- cooperating and exchanging information with relevant bodies and ministries;
- creating communication channels among private-sector, public-sector and government stakeholders for labour-market analysis and needs.

### Article 8 — regional steering committee

Each regional observatory is managed by a steering committee co-chaired by the relevant Labour Directorate director and a private-sector representative. The accessible indexed text identifies representatives of the Labour Directorate and other relevant local stakeholders. Secondary reporting specifies representatives from:
- the Labour Directorate;
- the Directorate of Education and Technical Education;
- the investors' association or business association;
- the Productivity and Vocational Training Authority;
- the SME Development Agency;
- the local chamber of commerce;
- the local Federation of Industries; and
- an academic from a local academic institution.

The precise complete membership and any voting/quorum rules should be checked against the full signed text.

### Article 9 — responsibilities of government partners

The Ministry of Labour, other ministries and government entities must:
1. Participate in periodic regional-observatory steering committee meetings through representatives and deputies, with at least two senior employees.
2. Assign at least one or two employees to work at the observatory for two days weekly (or one day if two is not possible), and make staff available for capacity-building training.
3. Participate in conferences launching regional labour-market studies.
4. Provide all data/information needed for studies and outputs and facilitate additional collection.
5. Distribute regional observatory outputs (data, analysis, recommendations and reports) to labour-market stakeholders in the governorate.
6. Coordinate with all relevant bodies, including investors' and business associations and labour unions.
7. Participate in board meetings.

### Article 10 — responsibilities of investors' and business associations

Investors' associations and business associations must:
- participate in steering committee meetings through at least two representatives/deputies;
- continue providing existing observatory premises at investors' associations; if unavailable or refused, the premises shift to the competent Labour Directorate;
- participate in central-board and governorate steering committee meetings;
- assign at least one or two employees for two days weekly (or one day if not possible) and support capacity-building;
- provide data and information needed by observatory teams and facilitate further collection;
- organize meetings with sectoral economic committees where necessary;
- participate in conferences launching regional studies;
- cooperate with the Ministry of Labour, Ministry of Education and Technical Education, CAPMAS and the Productivity and Vocational Training Authority to sustain regional observatories;
- support implementation of recommendations in policy briefs derived from observatory reports.

### Article 11 — funding

The Training and Qualification Fund established under the Labour Law finances the establishment and operation costs of the central coordinating observatory and regional labour-market information observatories, after approval by its board. The Fund's Secretary-General and the Director-General of the Ministry's General Department for Labour Market Information take the necessary legal steps.

### Article 12 — observatory resources

Resources include:
- allocations through cooperation with the Training and Qualification Fund under the required legal procedures;
- resources allocated by the board;
- consideration/fees for approved labour-market research and studies;
- donations, gifts and grants accepted by the board under applicable rules;
- proceeds/consideration from publishing research and studies;
- other funding from partner entities or development projects after board approval and under applicable legal rules.

### Article 13 — permitted expenditure

Expenditure may cover:
1. Supplies, materials, publications and office equipment required for the work.
2. Field-visit travel, accommodation and subsistence.
3. Telephone and internet services.
4. Conferences and workshops.
5. Administrative hospitality and cleaning.
6. Other new expenses, after board approval.

### Article 14 — spending controls

Financial limits and controls for Article 13 expenditure are set after Ministerial approval and on the proposal of the Director-General of the General Department for Labour Market Information.

### Article 15 — staff performance and training

The General Department for Labour Market Information monitors the performance of staff in regional and central observatories and proposes training courses to develop their skills based on needs reported by steering committees or new assignments. It coordinates with the central observatory and partners to develop operating mechanisms in line with labour-market developments and available resources.

### Article 16 — working groups

Depending on workload and operational needs, administrative/technical committees or working groups may be formed, chaired by the Director-General of the General Department for Labour Market Information and including financial, legal, technical and administrative experts. They may call on other experts as needed. Their functions include preparing central-board work, following implementation of decisions/recommendations and coordinating partner entities.

### Article 17 — publication and commencement

The decision is published in Al-Waqa'i al-Misriyya and enters into force on the day after publication. The decision is dated 15 April 2026; publication is reported on 17 May 2026, making 18 May 2026 the stated commencement date.

## Legal Copilot relevance and safeguards

- **Labour-market evidence:** this is a formal basis for a national/regional labour-market information and research infrastructure, including periodic reports and skill-demand information. It is potentially useful as a source-discovery pointer for employment and skills analysis, not itself a substantive rule governing private employment disputes.
- **Data confidentiality:** Article 7 expressly requires regional observatories to preserve the confidentiality of information sources and share information in accordance with data-protection/confidentiality laws. Do not treat the decision as permission to scrape, ingest or disclose individual-level labour-market records.
- **Data provenance:** any reports/data obtained from observatories should carry the original source, date, methodology, geography, definitions and any confidentiality/licence restrictions. Aggregated official statistics and confidential source data must be treated differently.
- **Research outputs:** Article 12 allows the observatory to receive funding and consideration for research/publications. This does not grant third parties a licence to copy or commercially reuse its reports.
- **Rights/legal status:** official searchable text and DSpace metadata are located, but the official PDF is restricted/request-copy. No rights clearance and no lawyer review. Do not mark as verified or import a copied corpus into production.
