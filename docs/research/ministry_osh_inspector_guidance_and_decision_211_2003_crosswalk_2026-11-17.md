## 2. Thermal exposure values reproduced in the Ministry bulletin

The Ministry's physical-hazards bulletin presents these thermal exposure values in degrees Celsius, separated by light, medium and heavy work:

| Work/rest schedule as printed | Light work | Medium work | Heavy work |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Continuous work | 30.00 | 26.70 | 25.00 |
| 75% work – 25% rest | 30.06 | 28.00 | 25.90 |
| 75% work – 25% rest | 31.40 | 29.40 | 27.90 |
| 75% work – 25% rest | 32.20 | 31.10 | 30.00 |

**Source transcription warning:** the Ministry PDF extraction repeats “75% work – 25% rest” for all three intermittent-work rows. Do not guess whether later rows should say 50/50 or 25/75. Preserve the printed label and flag the table for visual comparison with the PDF page and Decision 211/2003 itself.

The bulletin describes:
- Light work: operating machinery while standing/sitting and performing light manual work.
- Medium work: walking with light loads, pushing or pulling.
- Heavy work: digging, loading or climbing with loads.
- Measurement: black-globe thermometer, wet-bulb thermometer and dry-bulb thermometer.
- Scenarios: outdoor work in sunlight, covered/shaded work, and intermittent thermal exposure with rest periods.

It recommends periodic measurement, more rest, ventilation, drinking water, health education and medical surveillance for workers exposed to high temperatures. These are Ministry guidance statements; they do not replace the original decision or current technical standards.

## 3. Confined-space guidance — Ministry technical limits

The Ministry confined-space bulletin identifies the following figures:

| Parameter | Figure printed in Ministry guide | Unit / condition |
|---|---:|---|
| Hydrogen sulphide (H₂S) | 10 | ppm |
| Carbon monoxide (CO) | 35 | ppm |
| Oxygen | 19.5–23.5 | % by volume |
| Flammable atmosphere | below 10 | % of lower flammable limit (guide wording varies between “not exceed 10%” and “less than 10%”) |

The bulletin states that a confined-space entry permit should identify, at minimum:
- location and name of the confined space, and purpose of entry;
- date and validity period;
- entrants, outside attendants and responsible supervisor;
- potential hazards and isolation/control method;
- acceptable entry conditions for oxygen, flammables and toxic substances;
- measurements and inspections before and during entry;
- rescue resources, communications, equipment and PPE;
- any additional conditions needed to make the work safe.

It also calls for atmospheric testing before entry, mechanical ventilation, an outside attendant dedicated to monitoring, continuous monitoring of valves/switches and immediate evacuation in an emergency. The guide points to Articles 3 and 4 of Decision 134/2003 for work-permit requirements.

**Important:** these are values and controls in a Ministry guidance bulletin. They have not been matched to the complete Decision 211/2003 text, a current Egyptian standard, or an up-to-date international standard in this research pass. Do not publish them as current binding legal limits without that review.

## 4. Electrical hazards — Ministry guide contents

The Ministry electrical-risk bulletin covers static and dynamic electricity, Ohm's law, electrical shock, exposure pathways, preventive measures and inspection tasks. It cites Article 32 of Decision 211/2003 in its inspector-enforcement footer.

The guide's current-effect table prints:

| Current (mA) | Classification printed | Effect summary printed |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 or less | Safe | Not felt by a person |
| 1–8 | Safe | Shock without pain; person can withdraw/control muscles |
| 8–15 | Unsafe | Painful shock; withdrawal/control possible |
| 15–20 | Unsafe | Painful shock; loss of control of nearby muscles |
| 20–50 | Unsafe | Immobility, severe pain/muscle contraction, breathing difficulty |
| 50–100 | Dangerous | Cardiac disturbance; guide states potentially fatal |
| 100–200 | Dangerous | Guide describes fatal outcome |
| 200 or more | Dangerous | Severe burns/contraction and immediate death described |

The bulletin also recommends low voltage below 42V for certain hand tools/electrical tools exposed to specified risks, insulation, periodic testing, earthing, equipment isolation before work, fire equipment and work-permit controls.

These current/effect bands are guidance text, not a substitute for electrical engineering standards or medical risk assessment. Preserve overlapping boundaries and the source's classifications; do not turn the table into an automatic legal determination.

## 5. Radiation — Ministry guide contents

The radiation bulletin distinguishes ionizing and non-ionizing radiation, discusses X-rays, gamma, alpha and beta, exposure pathways, shielding, distance and time, and includes a UV exposure table:

| Radiation amount as printed | Exposure duration per day |
|---:|---:|
| 0.1 | 8 hours |
| 0.2 | 4 hours |
| 0.4 | 2 hours |
| 0.8 | 1 hour |

**Unit warning:** the table header is extracted as “Mw/cm3”, while the surrounding paragraph describes UV intensity in microW/cm² or mW/cm². This is a material dimensional inconsistency. Do not normalize or ingest the table until the original PDF image and an authoritative radiation-protection standard are checked.

The Ministry guide cites Articles 10–13 of Decision 211/2003 in its inspector-enforcement footer. It does not reproduce those articles in full.

## 6. Emergency planning / fire guidance

The Ministry emergency-plan bulletin describes risk analysis, monitoring, alarm, reporting, response, resourcing, first aid/medical services, event recording, incident-site control and equipment maintenance.

It also prints the following egress guidance:
- each exit-route unit is 22 inches (56 cm) wide;
- capacity per unit: 100 persons for straight routes and 60 for sloping routes;
- minimum two exits for 50 to fewer than 500 occupants, three for 501 to fewer than 1,000, and four for more than 1,000;
- floor illumination near emergency exits at least 5 foot-candles;
- exit-sign letters at least 6 inches (15 cm) high.

The bulletin does not identify the legal instrument/source standard for every egress figure in the extracted text. It must not be treated as the complete Egyptian building/fire code. The inspector footer cites Article 3 of Decision 211/2003 and Article 215 of Law 12/2003 for emergency planning.
