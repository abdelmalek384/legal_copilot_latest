## Decision 249/2026 — OSH units and committees

- **Official title:** identifies establishments and branches required to create OSH functional units and committees.
- **Scope lead:** secondary sources report application to establishments with 50 or more workers, with each geographically/administratively separate worksite counted independently if it reaches the threshold. The signed decision must be checked for the exact threshold and risk tiers.
- **Risk classes:** high, medium and limited risk; the decision is reported to require units/committees, safe-driving standards and employer OSH policy/management resources.
- **Employee protection:** the Ministry's official clarification of 27 October 2026 states that existing OSH professionals should not be harmed, excluded, or stripped of acquired legal/professional positions. It recognizes relevant specialist degrees and other graduates with postgraduate OSH qualifications, extended experience and recognized certificates; professional levels include OSH Specialist A and B. The Ministry said circulars would be issued to unify implementation.

## Decision 251/2026 — OSH expert/consultant licensing: expanded substantive extract

- Gazette issue 225 supplement (A), 12 October 2026. DSpace bundle metadata lists the signed decision, but the PDF is restricted/request-copy.
- Secondary article-level reporting: https://www.youm7.com/story/2026/10/28/حماية-أرواح-العمال-أولوية-وزارة-العمل-تحدد-مهلة-24-ساعة-للإبلاغ-عن-الحوادث-داخل-مواقع-الإنتاج/7590406
- Additional detailed secondary summary: https://www.a7walegypt.com/38829
- The decision requires a Ministry licence to practise OSH/environmental-safety expertise and consultancy. It creates Consultant A and Consultant B categories.
- Consultant A: relevant bachelor's degree in engineering, pharmacy, human medicine, veterinary medicine, sciences, applied health sciences, or environment/waste management; practical experience at least 20 years (secondary report states 15 years for relevant PhD holders); approved international OSH certificate; digital transformation/ICDL certificate; Ministry tests; good standing/criminal-record and drug-test requirements.
- Consultant B: same or approved academic fields; practical experience at least 15 years (secondary report states 10 years for relevant PhD holders); approved international certificate and required computer/Ministry tests.
- Application requires a paper/electronic dossier including CV, original qualification, ID, criminal record, experience evidence, professional certificates and drug-test certificate. A central Ministry licensing committee reviews applications; a licensing register and updated electronic database are maintained, with licensed consultants published on the Ministry website.
- Procedure: review within 15 days; notify applicant of outcome within three working days; incomplete documents can be completed; refusal must be reasoned and appealable.
- Licence lasts three years, renewable. Fees: Consultant A EGP 30,000 issue / EGP 25,000 renewal; Consultant B EGP 25,000 issue / EGP 20,000 renewal. Persons with disabilities receive a 50% fee reduction. Renewal requires work history for the previous three years.
- Cancellation grounds include loss of eligibility, false documents/data, serious professional breach, a conviction affecting honour/trust, and positive drug-use findings.
- Status: substantive secondary extraction only. The actual Gazette text and annexes must be obtained and compared before relying on degree/experience reductions, fee schedule, appeal periods or cancellation grounds.

**Publication:** Egyptian Gazette / الوقائع المصرية, issue 225 supplement (A), 12 October 2026, pages 19–44.  
**Secondary article and page-image host:** https://www.almasdar.com/175150

### Article map

- **Art.1 — definitions:** establishment; high-, medium- and limited-risk establishments; blind spots; safe driving; Ministry; competent authority; Law14/2025.
- **Art.2 — threshold and separate sites:** applies to establishments and branches with 50 or more workers, regardless of employment relationship or work pattern. A geographically or administratively separate worksite is treated as a separate establishment for this purpose if it has 50 or more workers.
- **Art.3 — employer/management duties:** provide a safe work environment; adopt and periodically review an OSH policy suited to activity and approved standards; comply with OSH legislation; allocate human, financial and technical resources; define roles/responsibilities; build safety culture through training and worker participation; periodically evaluate the OSH management system; monitor pollutants; apply safe-driving controls; notify the OSH administration of serious events; and maintain required records.
- **Art.4 — functional OSH unit:** each covered establishment/branch must create a specialist OSH functional unit. Staffing size and composition are linked to establishment risk, workforce and the attached staffing schedule. Unit personnel are treated consistently with comparable employees in material/financial benefits and report to the employer, board chair or responsible manager. Retaliatory measures for carrying out OSH duties are prohibited.
- **Art.5 — unit duties:** implements the establishment OSH policy and procedures; proposes technical specifications and PPE; checks equipment and protective measures; studies operations and hazards; classifies and assesses risk; conducts/coordinates environmental measurements; plans emergency response; issues/reviews work permits; inspects equipment and work areas; monitors vehicle/driver safety; follows maintenance and fire-protection systems; investigates accidents and occupational disease; trains and raises awareness; participates in planning and procurement; and advises management on corrective measures.
- **Art.6 — establishments below 50 workers:** limited-risk establishments below 50 workers must ensure the prescribed OSH protections and the owner/employer must complete the specified OSH training from a provider recognized under Article20. Medium/high-risk establishments below 50 must appoint an appropriately qualified OSH supervisor/technical person under the decision. Do not generalize this into an exemption from basic OSH duties.
- **Art.7 — workplace OSH committee:** establishes committee composition for covered establishments, including management/department representatives, civil-defence representation where available, the establishment doctor where available, the OSH unit head and worker representation; disability representation is considered where possible. The employer/manager chairs or appoints the chair as permitted by the text.
- **Art.8 — notification:** employer/manager notifies the competent Labour Directorate of the committee's formation and changes to membership.
- **Art.9 — meetings:** sets recurring meetings according to risk class and requires an extraordinary meeting within 48 hours after a serious accident or confirmed occupational disease.
- **Art.10 — quorum and voting:** sets attendance/quorum and decision-making rules, including worker representation and a casting vote for the chair in the event of a tie.
- **Art.11 — agenda and submissions:** members submit written/electronic proposals in advance for inclusion in the meeting agenda.
- **Art.12 — committee records:** requires a dedicated, numbered record of committee work, recommendations and decisions, available to the competent OSH administration/inspectors.
- **Art.13 — follow-up/notification:** requires the committee chair/secretariat to notify the competent OSH office of committee formation/meetings and maintain the specified follow-up record.
- **Art.14 — member replacement:** repeated absence without acceptable excuse triggers replacement under the prescribed procedure.
- **Art.15 — central/branch committees:** provides for a central OSH committee at governorate/enterprise level where there are multiple branches, and defines branch committee composition and coordination.
- **Art.16 — OSH specialist qualifications:** sets academic/professional qualification requirements for specialists in high-risk establishments, including the listed relevant disciplines and equivalent technical qualifications.
- **Art.17 — OSH supervisor qualifications:** sets qualifications for supervisors in medium-risk establishments and relevant operations, including a relevant degree/qualification and experience conditions.
- **Art.18 — OSH technician qualifications:** sets technician qualification conditions for establishments and activities in the attached risk schedules.
- **Art.19 — staff training:** requires training of specialists, supervisors, technicians, committee members and responsible managers according to role and risk, including basic/advanced/specific/specialized training paths.
- **Art.20 — training providers and National Centre:** assigns the Ministry/National Centre and licensed providers the relevant training and development functions; training pathways and deadlines are linked to the new licensing regime under Decision252/2026.
- **Art.21 — express repeal:** expressly repeals Ministerial Decision134/2003 on establishments required to create OSH functional units/committees and the rules followed in that regard, plus conflicting provisions.
- **Art.22 — commencement:** publication in the Egyptian Gazette and commencement the day after publication. The signed decision date shown on the final page is 9 September 2026; publication is 12 October 2026; commencement is therefore 13 October 2026.

### Annex 1 — establishment risk classification

The first schedule classifies activities/sites into high, medium and limited risk. The high-risk schedule includes (among others) petroleum/refining and petrochemicals; basic chemicals and paints/solvents; fertilizer and pesticide manufacture; explosives and flammable gases; metal smelting/foundries and high-temperature operations; glass/cement/ceramics; hazardous-waste and battery recycling; high-pressure/high-temperature equipment; power generation and distribution; water/wastewater operations with hazardous agents; nuclear/radiological activities; hazardous-waste treatment; major construction/demolition; transport/logistics operations involving dangerous goods; industrial maintenance/repair; large-scale food/catering; and other activities involving substantial fire, explosion, toxic, biological, mechanical or electrical risk.

The medium-risk schedule includes activities such as food/beverage manufacture without major hazardous processes, textiles/leather/wood/furniture, printing/packaging, ordinary workshops and manufacturing, retail/warehousing, healthcare and education sites without high-risk operations, ordinary construction/maintenance, and other activities with controllable operational hazards. Limited-risk examples include routine offices, low-hazard retail/services, ordinary administrative workplaces and other activities without high-risk processes. The complete table must be transcribed row-by-row before assigning a real establishment's risk class.
