## Decision 187/2025 — approval and withdrawal of employee resignation

- Ministry of Labour announcement (15 Sep 2025): https://www.labour.gov.eg/ar/الأخبار/وزير-العمل-ي-صدر-قرار-ا-وزاري-ا-يوضح-ضوابط-وآليات-اعتماد-الإستقالات-العمالية-طبق-ا-لقانون-العمل-الجديد/
- SIS-hosted Ministry document: https://mediadr.sis.gov.eg/bitstream/handle/123456789/111571/MOM20251001_1201.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=4
- Secondary article-level record: https://www.eastlaws.com/legislation-full-text/ar/egypt/labor/14-09-2025/no-187?id=2177898&type=1
- Nine articles reported by the Ministry. Article 1 requires a written resignation signed by the worker or special attorney and approved by a Labour Relations Office; it is serially registered, stamped, dated and returned to the worker. Article 2 gives the Labour Office stamp official effect and allows a republican emblem stamp at the worker's request. Article 3 requires national ID verification, employer/worker details and the worker's own signature; an employer cannot submit the resignation for the worker. Article 4 says service ends only by acceptance or expiry of ten days from submission, subject to the worker continuing work pending decision. Article 5 allows withdrawal within ten days from acceptance under the stated process. Article 6 excludes mutual consensual termination where both parties execute a release agreement that records payment of all financial entitlements. The Ministry's PDF snippet also supplies part of Article 6; remaining Articles 7–9 need full capture and Gazette issue/page confirmation.
- Status: official Ministry announcement and SIS-hosted Ministry document; partial text, not complete Gazette-matched corpus. Do not infer publication/effective date from the announcement date.

### Decision 267/2025 — apprenticeship / vocational progression

- Title: بشأن تنظيم أحكام التدرج. Gazette issue 295 supplement (D), published 30 Dec 2025; reported effective 31 Dec 2025.
- Full secondary transcription: https://lawhub.info/eg/?p=17307
- Twenty-two articles. Articles 1–3 define the apprentice as a person engaged to learn a profession/craft for remuneration under an agreement, apply the system to all establishments and economic activities, and describe practical/theoretical vocational learning. Article 4 creates a central committee chaired by the Minister to set strategy, integrate apprenticeship into education/training/work policy, assess skill demand, monitor programme performance and encourage private-sector partnerships. Article 7 sets a minimum age of 14, subject to child-employment protections, and prohibits hazardous work/training or interference with education. Articles 8–10 require a written agreement in three copies, duration normally 1–3 years divided into at least three stages, staged assessment and progressively increasing remuneration; final-stage pay must not be below the applicable minimum wage for the occupation. Article 11 bars restrictive covenants preventing entry into the labour market. Article 12 allows either party to end the agreement on at least three days' notice and requires employer notification/possible reassignment where the apprentice is unsuitable. Articles 13–16 require apprentice files, stage reports and a completion certificate with skill level. Articles 17–19 state employer and apprentice duties, including OSH and work-injury insurance. Article 20 applies statutory hours/rest/leave rules. Article 21 provides the statutory gratuity under Labour Law Article 172 for apprenticeship years before age 18 when conditions are met. Article 22 provides litigation-fee and stamp-duty exemptions for specified apprenticeship disputes/documents.
- Status: full secondary transcription found and Gazette issue confirmed in SIS metadata; article-by-article comparison with authorized Gazette PDF still needed.

### Decision 270/2025 — workforce data from new and existing establishments

- Gazette issue 295 supplement (D), signed 12 Nov 2025; published 30 Dec 2025; reported effective 31 Dec 2025.
- Full secondary transcription: https://lawhub.info/eg/?p=17306
- Three articles. Article 1 requires new covered establishments to send the Labour Directorate, within 30 days after starting work, a paper/electronic detailed workforce statement by qualification, occupation, age band, nationality, sex and wage. In each following January, they update changes, vacancies arising from replacement/expansion, abolished jobs and forecast staffing needs by education/professional category; the Directorate forwards the specified data to the National Social Insurance Authority. Article 2 applies the initial workforce statement to establishments already operating when the decision takes effect, within 30 days, and requires January updates. Article 3 publication/effectiveness.

### Decision 288/2025 — continuous-work exceptions and arduous-work rest

- Gazette issue 295 supplement (D), signed 11 Dec 2025; published 30 Dec 2025; reported effective 31 Dec 2025.
- Full secondary transcription: https://lawhub.info/eg/?p=17305
- Two chapters: cases where work must continue without a standard break for technical/operational reasons, and arduous work where breaks count as actual working time. Article 1 normally requires one or more meal/rest periods totalling at least one hour and prevents more than five consecutive hours of work. Article 2 allows exceptions where technical or operating circumstances require continuous work. Article 4 requires rest periods counted as actual working time for specified arduous occupations, including high-heat metal furnaces, explosives, glass melting, gas/electric metal welding, mercury mirror silvering, certain lead processes and other listed hazards. The exact complete list and Article 3 safeguards must be extracted from the full text before use.

### Decision 289/2025 — working hours in industrial establishments

- Gazette issue 295 supplement (D), signed 11 Dec 2025; published 30 Dec 2025; SIS-hosted Ministry document: https://mediadr.sis.gov.eg/bitstream/handle/123456789/117208/MOM20251227_0415.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=4
- Secondary full text: https://lawhub.info/eg/?p=17304
- Two articles. Article 1 states that, without prejudice to better terms in applicable law/contracts/internal regulations, actual work in industrial establishments may not exceed eight hours per day or 48 hours per week, excluding meal/rest periods. It allows overtime for unusual or exceptional operating needs subject to Labour Law Article 121. Article 2 publication/effectiveness from the day after publication.
- The SIS repository identifies the document as a Ministry of Labour text and makes the PDF publicly accessible; the Gazette issue metadata identifies issue 295 supplement (D). Confirm the signed Gazette page and reconcile references to prior industrial-hours instruments before treating it as current.
  
### Decision 290/2025 — intermittent work allowing presence above ten and up to twelve hours

- Gazette issue 295 supplement (D), signed 11 Dec 2025; published 30 Dec 2025; reported effective 31 Dec 2025.
- Full secondary transcription: https://lawhub.info/eg/?p=17303
- Four articles. Article 1 normally limits the span from start to end of work to ten hours and counts rest as presence time if the worker remains at the workplace. Article 2 defines intermittent work as work with pauses/waiting periods and lists, among other examples, passenger/goods transport, agricultural warehouses, ship mooring/navigation lights/repair during transit, emergency healthcare, logistics waiting, online technical support/digital services with intermittent requests, and data-centre/cloud-computing monitoring. Article 3 permits special scheduling up to 12 hours' presence, requires overtime payment under Labour Law Article 121 for actual work beyond ordinary hours, and a paper/electronic register of ordinary and overtime hours, breaks and presence. Article 4 publication/effectiveness.
- The inclusion of digital support and data-centre/cloud-monitoring work is notable but must be checked against the original Gazette schedule and its precise wording before classification.

### Decision 291/2025 — overtime grounds and controls

- Gazette issue 295 supplement (D), signed 11 Dec 2025; published 30 Dec 2025; reported effective 31 Dec 2025.
- Full secondary text and PDF: https://lawhub.info/eg/?p=17302 and https://lawhub.info/eg/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/lawhub.info-eg-dec-291-2025.pdf
- Five articles. Article 1 permits overtime beyond ordinary hours to meet unusual work necessities or exceptional circumstances, with notification to the Labour Directorate within seven days and specification of the required period. Overtime pay may be agreed individually/collectively but may not be less than ordinary hourly wage plus 35% for daytime and 70% for nighttime. If work occurs on weekly rest, the worker receives double pay for that day and another compensatory day within the following week. Presence at the establishment may never exceed 12 hours. Article 2 requires a paper/electronic register of ordinary/overtime hours, reasons, day/night classification, pay and evidence of payment. Article 3 requires overtime to be rotated and kept to the narrowest limits without harming worker health or weekly rest. Article 4 directs the Supreme Council for Social Dialogue, within three months, to set annual overtime maxima by economic activity/sector in consultation with unions and employers. Article 5 publication/effectiveness.
- This decision operationalizes Labour Law Article 121. The annual cap is assigned to a subsequent Council action; do not invent a numerical annual cap from this decision.

## Additional text completion — Decisions 187, 288 and 292

### Decision 187/2025 — Articles 7–9 located

Additional reporting from Al-Youm Al-Sabea and El-Watan provides the remaining provisions:
- Article 7: resignation or consensual termination agreement must reflect the worker's free will, without coercion; employer must settle worker rights and financial entitlements.
- Article 8: under Labour Law Article 175, employer must provide a service certificate recording employment start/end dates, job type and benefits; at the worker's request it may also state wage and reason for termination. It must be supplied within 15 days from request. Employer must return deposited papers, certificates, tools and clearance documents upon request when employment ends.
- Article 9: decision applies from its issue date (14 September 2025) and repeals conflicting prior provisions.

### Decision 288/2025 — complete six-article list located

The complete Law Hub transcription shows six articles. Article 2's fourteen listed categories where a standard rest break may be displaced for technical/operational continuity include: water/electricity/sewerage/cleaning/security utilities; power-generating machinery; passenger/goods transport by rail/road/inland waterways/air (with specified ancillary office work excluded); cargo loading/unloading; ship mooring/navigation lights; ship supply/repair at berth or Suez Canal transit; port tugs/water/fuel barges and manoeuvring/cargo; hospitals/medical centres/treatment facilities; funeral preparation/burial; ceramics/porcelain; bakeries; pharmaceutical manufacturing; pharmacies; and certain digital platforms such as delivery and ride-hailing/on-demand transport.
Article 3 requires compensatory rest, access to drinks/light food, preservation of ordinary/overtime hours and pay, no more than six consecutive actual work hours (five for drivers), and full OSH protections. Article 4 lists 20 arduous-work categories where rest counts as actual working time, including metal furnaces, explosives, glass melting, gas/electric welding, mercury mirror silvering, lead processes, battery mixing, operating/repairing power machinery, asphalt/rubber, tanneries, biological-waste fertilizer stores, slaughtering/rendering, animal-bone charcoal, toxic dust/fumes and construction in extreme weather. Article 5 requires OSH measures and controls for physical, mechanical, biological, chemical and other risks. Article 6 is publication/effectiveness.
Source: https://lawhub.info/eg/?p=17305 . Secondary transcription only; authorized Gazette comparison remains required.

### Decision 292/2025 — complete seven-article text located

- Official Gazette issue 291 supplement, 25 Dec 2025; signed 4 Dec 2025; reported effective 26 Dec 2025.
- Full secondary text: https://lawhub.info/eg/?p=17022 .
- Articles 1–4 define preparatory work, complementary work, guarding and cleaning. Preparatory work includes machinery/equipment/technical-system readiness, raw-material/logistics preparation and digital infrastructure/network activation before the scheduled shift. Complementary work includes repairs needed to avoid disruption of the next shift, operations that cannot be stopped (including oil-well drilling, power plants, data centres and 24-hour technical support), time-sensitive loading/storage/distribution and completion of technical processes such as purification/cooling/final digital documentation. Guarding includes guards, firefighters and electronic/cybersecurity monitoring. Cleaning includes sanitation/disinfection of vital areas and equipment.
- Article 5 caps actual work in these categories at 48 hours per week. Article 6 caps overtime at 12 hours per week, with no more than two hours per day, subject to Labour Law Article 121. Article 7 publication/effectiveness.
- This text contains technology and cybersecurity-related classifications, but do not map job titles to the categories without counsel review and primary-text confirmation.
