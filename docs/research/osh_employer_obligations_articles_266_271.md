## Article 266 — pre-employment medical and capability examination

Every establishment and its branches must arrange:
1. Initial medical examination before the worker starts, to confirm health and fitness for the assigned work.
2. Capability examination to assess physical, mental and psychological suitability for the work's requirements.

The examinations follow the applicable health-insurance rules. A joint decision by the Minister responsible for labour and the Minister responsible for health must set the fitness, health-safety, mental and psychological capability levels used for these examinations.

**Instrument gap:** no joint Labour/Health decision setting the standards was located in the current register. Do not invent fitness thresholds or medical test panels.

## Article 267 — training and hazard awareness

The establishment and its branches must:
- train workers in the proper/safe methods for performing their occupations;
- inform each worker, before starting work, of occupational hazards;
- require use of prescribed protective measures;
- provide suitable personal protective equipment (PPE); and
- train workers in PPE use.

The establishment may not charge workers or deduct any part of wages for the protective equipment required.

This is a direct statutory duty. Decisions 252/2026 (OSH training/licensing) and 250/2026 (OSH standards) are related implementation sources but do not remove the employer's direct obligation.

## Article 268 — worker duties regarding protection

Workers must:
- use the PPE supplied;
- take care of equipment in their custody;
- follow instructions intended to preserve health and prevent occupational accidents; and
- refrain from obstructing safety instructions, misusing or altering protective equipment, or damaging equipment intended to protect themselves and co-workers.

The duty operates without prejudice to other applicable laws.

## Article 269 — daily workplace inspection and medical follow-up

Each establishment and its branches must:
1. Conduct periodic daily inspection in every work shift, especially in hazardous areas, to detect occupational risks and prevent them; maintain a paper or electronic register for this purpose.
2. Examine a worker's illness complaint and determine its relationship to the type of work through the establishment doctor, if one exists.
3. Coordinate with the Health Insurance Authority to conduct periodic medical examinations for all establishment workers to preserve physical, mental and psychological fitness, maintain safety, detect occupational diseases early and conduct examination at end of service, under applicable health-insurance systems.

**Product implications:** a compliance checklist should capture shift-by-shift inspection, high-risk area checks, risk/near-miss entries, complaint referral, doctor involvement and Health Insurance coordination. The Law does not itself specify the exact register schema or data-retention period in this article; do not invent those fields or periods.

## Article 270 — first aid, nurse and doctor thresholds

Every establishment must provide first-aid means for workers.

Where the establishment has more than **50 workers** in one place, one town, or within a radius of **15 kilometres**, it must:
- employ one or more qualified nurses for nursing/first aid on every shift;
- assign a doctor to examine workers at a location prepared for that purpose; and
- provide necessary medicines free of charge.

If the worker is treated in a government or charitable hospital in either of the statutory cases, the establishment must pay the hospital's treatment, medicines and accommodation expenses.

The procedures for determining those treatment, medicine and accommodation expenses must be set by a joint decision of the Minister responsible for labour and the Minister responsible for health.

**Instrument gap:** the joint expense-calculation decision was not located. Confirm how the threshold is counted across a workplace, town or 15-km radius before automated compliance decisions.

## Article 271 — transport, food and accommodation in remote areas

Employers using workers at sites inaccessible by ordinary transport must provide suitable transport at the employer's expense.

Employers using workers in areas remote from urban centres must provide:
- suitable food; and
- appropriate accommodation, with some accommodation allocated for married workers.

A joint decision by the Minister responsible for labour, the relevant Ministers and employer/worker organizations must:
- identify the remote areas;
- set housing conditions/specifications;
- identify food types and quantities for each worker; and
- set the employer's corresponding cost.

The workplace may adopt a mutually agreed meal system with the competent union or worker representative, subject to administrative approval. Meals may not be replaced wholly or partly with cash.

**Instrument gap:** Decision 49/2026 identifies hazardous/difficult work and remote areas for the seven-day additional annual-leave rule under Article 124. That does not, by itself, establish the full Article 271 remote-area list, housing standards, meal specifications or employer contribution. No complete joint Article 271 decision was located.

## Data and privacy safeguards

- Daily inspection and medical-health data may contain sensitive worker information. Collect only what is necessary for the statutory purpose, apply strict access controls, separate medical records from general case/workforce records and follow applicable data-protection and health-insurance rules.
- Do not expose individual medical diagnoses in general HR dashboards or employer-facing AI summaries unless a lawful basis and role-specific access permit it.
- Do not treat Decision 74/2026's labour-market observatory data mandate as permission to access individual medical or worker records.
- Preserve evidence of inspections and compliance without unnecessarily copying medical records into the legal corpus.
