# Supreme Constitutional Court — public-official criticism and publication fines

**Case:** 244/23 Judicial Year2026  
**Session:** 7 March 2026  
**Official full judgment:** https://www.sccourt.gov.eg/DjangoPortalResources/Rules_Html/18148_23_244_1_2.html

## 1. Subject and procedural background

Two journalists challenged Penal Code Articles302, 303, 306 and307 after prosecution for public defamation/insult and publication of allegations about a public official. The prosecution also invoked Article185, which criminalizes insulting a public official because of official duties.

The Court considered:
- Article185 (insulting a public official).
- Article302(2) (public-interest criticism of a public official).
- Article303 (defamation penalties).
- Article306 (insult).
- Article307 (doubling fine ranges for specified publication offences).

## 2. Public-interest criticism safe harbor — Article302(2)

The Court upheld the statutory conditions for lawful criticism of a public official, person with representative public status or person entrusted with public service. The safe harbor requires all three:
1. The criticism is made in good faith.
2. It does not go beyond the official duties, representation or public service.
3. The defendant proves the truth of each act attributed to the official.

The Court reasoned that official work is a public concern and citizens may criticize shortcomings and unlawful conduct, but the safe harbor balances public transparency against protection of the official's reputation. The provision is a statutory cause of justification when all conditions are satisfied.

**Operational rule:** do not treat mere good faith as sufficient. The statutory defense also requires subject-matter connection to official duties and proof of truth. Conversely, do not omit the defense where all three conditions are met.

## 3. Insult of public officials — Article185 and publication multiplier — Article307

The judgment reproduces Article185 as amended by Law147/2006:
- Fine not less than EGP10,000 and not more than EGP20,000 for insulting a public official/person with public representative status/person entrusted with public service because of official duties.

Article307, as amended by Law93/1995, doubles the minimum and maximum fine for specified offences committed through publication in a newspaper or printed publication. In its application to Article185, the Court records the resulting range as:
- EGP20,000 minimum.
- EGP40,000 maximum.

The Court upheld the provisions, reasoning that the sanction is a fine (not imprisonment), has minimum/maximum bounds that allow judicial individualization, and is proportionate to the reach of newspaper publication.

**Currentness caution:** these amounts are the version reproduced in the 2026 judgment. Before quoting them as current, check any later Penal Code amendments and the exact mode of publication. The judgment itself does not create a new penalty; it upholds the cited statutory wording.

## 4. Relationship with earlier constitutional cases

The judgment states:
- Case60/22 Judicial Year2024 (session 6 July 2024) rejected a challenge to Penal Code Article303(2) and Article307 insofar as applied to it; published Official Gazette issue27 (repeated), 7 July 2024.
- Case16/24 Judicial Year2025 (session 1 September 2025) rejected challenges to Articles302(1),(3),303(1),306 and Article307 insofar as applied to the latter provisions; published Official Gazette issue35 bis (A), 3 September 2025.
- The 2026 Court applied the binding absolute authority of prior SCC judgments and declared the repeated challenge inadmissible to the extent already decided.

## 5. Result

The Court rejected the constitutional challenge. It upheld Article185, Article302(2) and Article307 insofar as applied to Article185; it treated repeated challenges to other provisions as already resolved by earlier SCC judgments.

This judgment is not authority for saying every possible criminal defamation or insult prosecution is constitutional in every application. Its scope is the challenged statutory provisions and the arguments before the Court.
