# Court of Cassation appeal 5280/87 — Gas Egypt absenteeism and termination

**Hearing:** 2026-08-25  
**Court:** Court of Cassation, labour circuit (reported in news coverage)  
**Appeal:** 5280/87 judicial year  
**Underlying case:** 214/2012, Shebin El-Kom Court of First Instance  
**Governing statute:** Labour Law 12/2003, Art. 69(4), and Art. 71; Civil Code principles were also considered. This is a historical-law precedent, not a direct interpretation of Law 14/2025.

## Extracted procedural facts and holding (secondary report)
- The worker was absent from 4 August 2011 through 16 August 2011, was warned about the absence, and did not provide the employer with medical reports before the termination decision.
- The Court reportedly applied Art. 69(4) of Law 12/2003: absence without lawful excuse exceeding 20 intermittent days in a year or 10 consecutive days may constitute gross misconduct; prior written registered-mail warning is required after the statutory period (10 days for intermittent absence; 5 days for consecutive absence).
- The Court distinguished dismissal as a disciplinary sanction from termination by the employer under the applicable contractual/civil-law rules. It reportedly held that the Labour Law Art. 71 procedure requiring referral to the labour court applies to dismissal as a disciplinary sanction, not every employer-initiated termination.
- The Court set aside the Court of Appeal's award of EGP 50,000 compensation and dismissed the worker's appeal, upholding the first-instance dismissal of the claim.
