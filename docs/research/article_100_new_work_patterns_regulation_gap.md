# Labour Law 14/2025 Article 100 — New Work-Pattern Regulation Gap

Research date: 17 November 2026  
Status: no general Article 100 ministerial decision located. The Ministry's own September 2026 activity shows the regulatory framework was still being developed. Do not treat the temporary Sunday remote-work PM decision series or workplace-bylaw rules as a complete substitute.

## Statutory requirement

Article 96 defines new work patterns to include:
- remote work;
- part-time work;
- flexible work (same required hours arranged non-contiguously or changed by agreement in timing/number/location);
- job sharing; and
- other forms specified by the Minister.

Articles 98–99 allow workers under these patterns to work for multiple employers or for themselves alongside employment, subject to confidentiality, and require a clear written paper/electronic employment contract (with the relationship provable by all means).

**Article 100** requires the Minister responsible for labour, after consultation with labour organizations and employer organizations, to issue the decisions necessary to regulate new work patterns, identify their forms, provide model employment contracts and workplace regulations, set methods of proving the employment relationship, and mechanisms for each party to obtain their rights. The statutory text sets a deadline of no more than six months from issuance of Law 14/2025.

## Why other decisions do not fully satisfy Article 100

1. **PM Decision 982/2026 and its five monthly extensions** created a temporary Sunday remote-work arrangement for specified sectors during April–September 2026. They do not regulate all new work patterns nationally, and they were time-limited.
2. **Ministerial Decision 162/2026** requires workplace bylaws to address flexible/remote work where adopted by an establishment, including hours, performance duties, monitoring and evaluation. This is an internal-work-regulation mechanism, not the full national framework Article 100 delegates to the Minister.
3. **Ministerial Decision 272/2025** regulates recruitment agencies and electronic recruitment platforms. It does not by itself regulate the employment relationship for workers actually performing platform work or freelancing.
4. The October 2026 Ministry/Communications MOU and digital-platform discussions are policy/implementation steps, not binding rules.

## Minimum content needed in the missing Article 100 instrument

The law expressly calls for:
- forms and definitions of new work patterns;
- model paper/electronic contracts and model workplace-regulation clauses;
- proof of employment relationship and allocation of evidentiary burdens;
- rights and obligations of worker and employer;
- hours, availability, monitoring, equipment and expense allocation;
- wages, payment timing, overtime/rest and leave treatment;
- social-insurance and health-insurance treatment;
- occupational safety, ergonomic and work-injury controls in remote settings;
- data confidentiality, cybersecurity and employer access to devices/accounts;
- multiple-employer and side-work confidentiality/conflict rules;
- platform-work status, control/management indicators and transparent remuneration;
- dispute/complaint mechanisms and records;
- model documentation for enforcement and inspection.

This is an issue checklist, not a proposal to invent rules. The actual decision must be sourced and reviewed before product logic is built.

## Legal Copilot controls

- Keep Article 100 status as **delegated instrument not located**.
- Do not use PM 982 as the general rule for remote work.
- Do not infer that every freelancer/platform worker is an employee or that none is an employee; classify using the statutory relationship and actual facts.
- Use Decision 162 only for workplace-bylaw duties it actually states.
- Keep the MOU, Ministry press releases and Careerians article as guidance/source leads, not binding legal authority.
- Do not create contract templates or automated compliance outcomes from the missing rules.
