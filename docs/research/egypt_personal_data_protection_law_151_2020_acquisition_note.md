## Official PDPC guidelines actually extracted

### Data Subject Consent Guidelines v1.1 — 26 January 2026

The PDPC guideline identifies six lawful bases under Law 151/2020 Article 6:
1. Consent of the data subject.
2. Performance of a contractual obligation.
3. Compliance with a legal obligation.
4. Legitimate interest.
5. Establishment, exercise or defence of a legal right.
6. Execution of court judgments or orders from competent investigative authorities.

The guideline says consent is not automatically the correct basis in every context. Valid consent must be personal, explicit, informed, specific and freely given. Sensitive personal data requires additional conditions, including written consent; processing children's data requires guardian-related safeguards. Requests should be in Arabic, intelligible, concise, affirmative, granular, separate and prominent. Pre-ticked boxes and double-negative wording are identified as poor/invalid patterns. Consent withdrawal must be easy and free, and consent records should preserve who consented, when, what information was given, the affirmative action and any withdrawal.

Official PDF: https://pdpc.gov.eg/assets/pdf-data/Guidelines/DSConsent.pdf

### Privacy Notice Guidelines v1.1 — 26 January 2026

The PDPC guideline distinguishes a privacy notice from a consent request and from an internal privacy policy. A privacy notice is a transparency mechanism regardless of which lawful basis is used. Minimum contents include:
- Identity and contact details of the data user/controller.
- DPO contact details.
- Categories of personal data collected.
- Lawful basis and purposes.
- Recipients/categories of recipients.
- Legal basis and destination countries for cross-border transfers, where applicable.
- Retention period or criteria.
- Data-subject rights and how to exercise them.
- Source of indirectly collected data, where applicable.
- Right to complain to the PDPC.

The notice should be Arabic-first, intelligible, transparent, concise, prominent and accessible. The guideline states that direct collection requires notice at the moment of collection; indirect collection requires notice as promptly as possible and no later than 30 days, with earlier notice in some communication/disclosure cases.

Official PDF: https://pdpc.gov.eg/assets/pdf-data/Guidelines/Privacy%20Notice.pdf

### Non-binding status of guidance

The PDPC guideline disclaimer expressly says the Centre may update or replace its guidance and templates; the guidelines are not binding administrative or regulatory decisions and do not replace the Law or Executive Regulation. Store these as official interpretive guidance, below the Law and Executive Regulation in the source hierarchy.

## Currentness and grace-period issue

Secondary legal updates state that Decision 816/2025 introduced a one-year reconciliation/compliance period, with the end date described as 31 October or 1 November 2026 depending on the source's date calculation. As of this snapshot (17 November 2026), that period appears to have elapsed, but **do not assert an exact deadline or enforcement consequence until the Arabic promulgation article and Gazette date are checked and any PDPC extension/decision is searched**.

MCIT's official news item dated 4 November 2026 confirms that the PDPC participated in a forum held on 26 October 2026 addressing implementation of Law 151/2020 and Decision 816/2025, including data classification, lawful bases and data-subject requests. This demonstrates regulator activity, not a binding enforcement circular.
