## Preamble

The decision cites Labour Law 14/2025 and ILO conventions ratified by Egypt. It was issued after taking the opinions of the National Council for Women and the National Council for Childhood and Motherhood and presentation to the Supreme Council for Social Dialogue.

## Article-by-article map

### Article 1 — equality and equal pay

All provisions regulating workers' employment apply to women without discrimination. Men and women are entitled to equal pay for work of equal value, including all wage components: cash/in-kind benefits, allowances, incentives, bonuses and other elements.

### Article 2 — purpose

The decision aims to provide measures to protect maternity and childhood and address occupational safety and health risks affecting women workers.

### Article 3 — prohibited work during pregnancy and breastfeeding

Women may not be employed during the legally defined pregnancy and breastfeeding periods in work/conditions containing risks that could harm reproductive health, the fetus or children's health.

The official indexed text identifies chemical risks from handling solid, liquid and gaseous chemicals, including work associated with:
- lead and mercury and their related industrial processes/areas;
- asphalt and its derivatives;
- rubber manufacture;
- insecticide manufacture;
- fertilizer manufacture;
- vinyl chloride;
- fertilizers/hormones and related compounds.

The secondary transcription also identifies further risk groupings:
- biological risks;
- physical risks;
- engineering/mechanical risks, including manual lifting/dragging and certain manual load-handling activities.

The secondary schedule includes examples such as manual loading/carrying and moving goods on handcarts. The complete Article 3 annex/list is not reproduced here because every listed occupation, process and condition must be transcribed and QA-checked from the Gazette pages. Do not treat the examples above as an exhaustive list.

### Article 4 — administrative/supervisory work during pregnancy and breastfeeding

Without prejudice to statutory maternity rights, women may work during pregnancy and breastfeeding in administrative and supervisory jobs that do not expose them to the risks referred to in Article 3.

### Article 5 — work outside pregnancy/breastfeeding periods

Outside pregnancy and breastfeeding periods, women may work in the occupations referred to in Article 3 if all OSH/work-environment requirements are met and the prescribed safety limits, conditions and precautions are followed for physical, mechanical, biological, chemical and other hazards.

Failure by an establishment or its branches to take the required precautions is treated as an imminent danger to workers' health. The competent administrative authority may close the establishment wholly or partially, or stop machinery, until the danger is removed.

### Article 6 — night work at the woman's request

Women may, at their request, work at night in any type of establishment, provided measures are taken to protect health, assist with family responsibilities and provide necessary healthcare to avoid work-related health problems.

### Article 7 — transfer to daytime work

The employer must provide alternative daytime work for a woman worker:
1. During a period of at least 16 weeks before and after childbirth, including at least eight weeks before the expected delivery date.
2. During any additional period, beyond the above, supported by a medical certificate stating that it is necessary to protect the health of the mother or child during pregnancy or after childbirth.

### Article 8 — industrial night-work window

Subject to Article 7, women's night work in an industrial establishment or branch during the period from **10:00 p.m. to 7:00 a.m.** is governed by the safeguards and controls in the decision.

### Article 9 — industrial-establishment definition

For the decision, an industrial establishment is a project/facility owned or operated by a public- or private-law person for producing, transforming or processing raw/primary materials. Examples include:
- mines, quarries and other mineral-extraction work;
- establishments manufacturing, modifying, cleaning, repairing, decorating, preparing for sale, dismantling or destroying materials, including shipbuilding and electricity/power generation, conversion or transmission;
- construction and civil-engineering establishments, including construction, erection, restoration, maintenance, modification and demolition.

### Article 10 — exceptions from Articles 8 and 9

The decision excludes certain specified works/conditions from Articles 8 and 9, including force-majeure cases where work stops for reasons outside the employer's control that could not have been predicted and are not naturally recurring. The secondary text also identifies certain health/social-care activities where women are not performing manual work. The full exception list should be checked against the official pages.

### Article 11 — transport, first aid and protection for night work

The employer must provide transport for workers during night periods and safe transportation for women workers, first-aid facilities (including transfer to treatment where necessary), and safeguards for protection, care, transport and security of women workers.

### Article 12 — written approval and night inspection

The employer must apply to the competent Labour Directorate for written approval to employ women at night in permitted cases. The application must state:
- reasons for night work;
- the jobs/activities in which night work will occur; and
- the safeguards adopted.

The Directorate must maintain a database of establishments employing women at night, conduct night inspections and provide periodic reports to the Ministry's competent departments.

### Article 13 — preservation of better conditions

The decision does not prejudice better benefits or conditions for women workers under individual/collective employment contracts, establishment bylaws or other regulations, or custom.

### Article 14 — impact studies

The Ministry's Gender Equality and Women's Economic Empowerment Unit, coordinating with relevant Ministry departments, the National Council for Women, the National Council for Childhood and Motherhood, employers' organizations and labour unions, must prepare impact-assessment studies to monitor implementation.

### Article 15 — publication, commencement and repeal

The decision is published in Al-Waqa'i al-Misriyya, takes effect on the day after publication and repeals any prior provision/decision inconsistent with it.

## Product and compliance implications

- **Do not implement a blanket women-work prohibition.** Article 1 expressly applies general employment protections without discrimination and guarantees equal pay for work of equal value.
- Model the Article 3 restrictions as time-bound pregnancy/breastfeeding protections tied to specified risk categories and jobs; model Article 4 administrative/supervisory work separately.
- Article 5 creates a conditional route for work in listed occupations outside pregnancy/breastfeeding periods when required safety measures exist, with potential closure/machinery-stop powers if safeguards are absent.
- Night work is not categorically prohibited: Article 6 permits it at the woman's request with protections; Articles 7–12 create transfer, industrial-hour, transport, approval and inspection safeguards.
- Preserve better contractual/collective/customary terms under Article 13.
- The Article 3 risk annex must be fully transcribed before any job-matching, compliance checklist or automated refusal logic is built. A partial list could create unsafe false negatives.
