### Decision 300/2025 — conciliation in collective labour disputes
Title: إجراءات وقواعد التوفيق في منازعات العمل الجماعية. Gazette contents places it at printed page 45. Sixteen articles define collective dispute, collective bargaining, worker representative, conciliation and competent authority. A request follows failed collective bargaining and includes negotiated issues, agreed/not agreed points, supporting documents and negotiation minutes/summary. It should be made after at least one month from the start of bargaining without agreement. The first session is set within five days after application, with at least three days' notice. Deliberate absence for more than three consecutive sessions can prompt the authority to contact the relevant worker/employer organization. During conciliation, parties generally cannot take measures/decisions on the dispute except urgent temporary measures. Confidentiality restrictions cover enterprise information and conciliation minutes. If unresolved within 21 days from commencement, parties may seek referral to the mediation/arbitration centre under Labour Law Art. 218. All 16 articles located in the third-party-hosted Gazette facsimile; authorized-copy comparison remains open.

### Decision 301/2025 — objective criteria for selecting workers in partial closure/reduction
Title: المعايير الموضوعية لاختيار من سيتم الاستغناء عنهم من العمال حال إغلاق المنشأة جزئياً أو تقليص حجمها أو نشاطها. Gazette contents places it at printed page 50. Individual DSpace record: https://dspace.id.com.eg/items/f5fa9b6f-f745-4ee5-90e5-ba5d498252a3 . Six articles apply to serious economic, technical or organizational partial closure/reduction decisions. If no current collective agreement contains objective selection criteria, the employer must seriously consult the relevant union, if any, for at least seven working days after the enterprise decision and before implementation. Principles include transparency, objectivity/non-discrimination, balancing enterprise interests with workers' family/social stability, and giving the union relevant data. Article 4 lists indicative priorities: seniority absent objectively justified technical reasons; skills, multi-role capacity, scarce specialties and performance over the previous three years; disciplinary sanctions/poor performance; family circumstances/dependants; and retention considerations for workers supporting a disabled person, a person of short stature, an orphan, or workers with serious health conditions. Article 5 preserves collective-agreement criteria and requires legal notice and payment of entitlements, including unused-leave cash equivalent and minimum compensation under Labour Law Art. 241. Employer must notify the competent authority of the decision, reasons and applied criteria. All six articles located in the third-party-hosted Gazette facsimile; authorized-copy comparison remains open.

## Decision 214/2025 — employment contract conditions and controls

Issued 5 October 2025; published in Egyptian Gazette issue 249 on 6 November 2025. DSpace record: https://dspace.id.com.eg/items/8ebcec10-0c45-4fbf-9a56-44ca409dc6c4 . Searchable Gazette facsimile hosted by a third party: https://cdn.vetogate.com/UploadCache/libfiles/565/0/0x0o/228.pdf .

Nine articles located. Employer prepares written Arabic contracts in four copies, with the fourth copy lodged with the competent Labour Directorate on paper or electronically. Electronic submission is from the company's official approved email and includes company registration/tax records, employer identity, company/workplace and insurance details, contract count, a worker list (names, national IDs, wages, occupations and fixed-term duration), and each contract as a separate PDF. Renewals of fixed-term contracts and notices of expiry/non-renewal/termination are filed within 15 days. No filing is required for indefinite-term contracts made before the Labour Law took effect on 1 September 2025; pre-commencement fixed-term contracts are filed at first renewal. Article 6 lists core contract particulars; Article 7 addresses foreign workers who do not know Arabic; Article 8 provides Ministry/directorate advice to distinguish employment from other legal relationships; Article 9 sets publication/effectiveness and repeal of conflicting prior provisions.

## Decision 259/2025 — payment of workers' entitlements on dissolution/closure/insolvency

Issued 4 December 2025; published 25 December 2025 in Gazette issue 291 supplement; catalogue indicates effectiveness from 26 December 2025. Official DSpace bundle: https://dspace.id.com.eg/items/37add437-802d-4848-8a24-8e064db22fcc . Full secondary transcription: https://lawhub.info/eg/?p=17016 .

Eight-article secondary text. Employment-related sums receive priority over debtor assets; social-insurance contributions are included among workers' rights. A judicial judgment or legally competent authority decision is required for dissolution/closure/reduction and should set a payment period not exceeding one year. Within 30 days, employer/liquidator/trustee inventories wages and entitlements and pays in one lump sum if funds suffice. If not, available funds are paid with regard to seniority or agreed criteria and the balance scheduled within the maximum period. Monthly reports and Labour Directorate monitoring are required. In necessity cases a directorate representative may assist with calculation/payment. Article 7 treats acts intended to undermine payment obligations as void; the directorate warns and allows up to 15 days to correct, then refers the matter to the Labour Court. Workers/representatives may also sue and seek removal of a liquidator or bankruptcy trustee. Full secondary text plus official bundle metadata; primary Gazette comparison remains outstanding.

## Decision 299/2025 — individual amicable-settlement committee

Issued 4 December 2025; included in Gazette issue 291 supplement metadata dated 25 December 2025. Official bundle: https://dspace.id.com.eg/items/37add437-802d-4848-8a24-8e064db22fcc . Full secondary transcription: https://lawhub.info/eg/?p=17023 .

Seven articles. Individual disputes concerning the Labour Law or related laws may be referred for amicable settlement within ten days from arising. Committee chaired by head of competent Labour Directorate (or delegate), with worker/representative and employer/representative. Committee should finish within 21 days. A signed settlement minute goes to the urgent-matters judge at the Labour Court for an enforceable order. If settlement fails, referral may be made to the Labour Court; registry sets hearing within 20 days of referral request. For dismissal disputes, court decides the urgent request within three months of first hearing; if papers prima facie support worker, employer may be ordered to pay wages from dismissal up to six months; decision final. If dismissal was for union activity, reinstatement is ordered if requested. Complete secondary transcription found; Gazette page/scan and rights checks remain open.
