## 2. New operative content extracted from the September 2026 bank CDD procedure

The 42-page document states in its preamble that the Unit's Board approved the September 2026 edition to replace the previous February 2020 version. It applies to banks operating in Egypt, their foreign branches, and branches of foreign banks operating in Egypt.

### Customer identification and thresholds
The extracted procedure requires CDD when establishing a business relationship; conducting an occasional transaction above USD 15,000 or equivalent (including linked transactions); carrying out an occasional wire transfer of any value; where there is suspicion of money laundering/terrorist financing; or where the bank doubts the accuracy or adequacy of previously obtained identification data. It prohibits anonymous or fictitious-name accounts.

### Beneficial ownership
For a legal-person customer, the natural person who ultimately owns or controls the customer is identified, including indirect ownership/control and control through means other than direct shareholding. The procedure sets a 25% ownership/control starting point, followed by analysis of other means of control and, if no natural person is identified through those tests, the relevant senior managing official. It also addresses legal arrangements, trusts, settlors, trustees, protectors and beneficiaries.

### Risk-based CDD
The bank must document and update its ML/TF/PF risk assessment at least annually and when needed, classify customers at minimum as high, medium or low risk, and apply enhanced measures for higher risk. Simplified CDD is allowed only where low risk is established through appropriate analysis and not where suspicion or high-risk scenarios exist.

### Continuous monitoring and records
The bank must monitor transactions continuously, use electronic scenarios to detect unusual, complex or unusually large transactions lacking apparent economic/legal purpose, screen relevant negative lists before relationships and occasional transactions and throughout the relationship, document results, and implement targeted financial sanctions. Electronic record systems must permit retrieval and tracking of changes.

### Customer data refresh
The extracted text sets five years as the maximum update interval for low-risk customers, with shorter periods for higher-risk customers, and earlier refresh where transaction patterns change materially, risk rises, suspicion exists, documents are doubtful or ownership/control changes.

### Third parties and banking agents
The procedure permits reliance on third parties and banking agents only under stated controls; the bank retains ultimate responsibility. The agent must send electronic copies of documents within three business days and paper copies within thirty days. The bank must set interim risk limits and stop the account where documents are not delivered within the prescribed time.

### Electronic identity
The separate e-KYC procedure establishes a digital identity workflow using the approved digital financial identity system. The first phase is limited to natural persons who complete the process themselves and are the beneficial owners. The text covers required data, authentication, proof of possession of the mobile device, live image/liveness checks, identity-document matching and secure electronic authentication.

## 3. PEP guidance — September 2026

The six-page Unit guide is titled:
**إرشادات للقطاع المصرفي بشأن إعادة تصنيف العملاء من الأشخاص ذوي المخاطر بحكم مناصبهم العامة بعد ترك مناصبهم**

It covers foreign and domestic politically exposed persons (PEPs), their family members and close associates, and reassessment after leaving public office. The extracted guide sets minimum post-office periods of ten years for foreign PEPs and five years for domestic PEPs and senior international-organisation officials, after which the bank must conduct an individual risk assessment rather than automatically removing PEP status. Enhanced measures include senior-management approval, reasonable steps to establish source of wealth and source of funds, and enhanced ongoing monitoring.

**Caution:** This PEP time-period summary is from the secondary-hosted full PDF and must be cross-checked against the official Unit-hosted file before being used in a legal decision engine.

## 4. CBE circular dated 28 September 2026 — implementation direction

CBE's official circular index confirms three circulars of 28 September 2026:
- Procedures for due diligence of banks' customers.
- Procedures for due diligence of money-transfer companies' customers.
- Procedures for due diligence of foreign-exchange companies' customers.

Official CBE PDF links:
- Banks: https://www.cbe.org.eg/-/media/project/cbe/listing/circulars/2026/september/circular-dated-28-september-2026-regarding-kycs-of-banks’-customers.pdf
- Money-transfer companies: https://www.cbe.org.eg/-/media/project/cbe/listing/circulars/2026/september/circular-dated-28-september-2026-regarding-kyc-for-money-transfer-companies.pdf
- Foreign-exchange companies: https://www.cbe.org.eg/-/media/project/cbe/listing/circulars/2026/september/circular-dated-28-september-2026-regarding-kyc-for-foreign-exchange-companies.pdf

The CBE search index and Bankawy reproduction of the bank circular report the following implementation matters:
- Apply the September 2026 CDD procedures to current and new customers.
- Replace the February 2020 bank CDD edition; the financial-inclusion, mobile-payment and prepaid-card procedures replace their respective March 2020 editions.
- Update customer acceptance policies and board approvals; review policies at least every two years.
- Identify and verify beneficial owners and the purpose/nature of the relationship.
- Screen lists before establishing a relationship and before occasional transactions, irrespective of value, and continue screening through the relationship.
- Enhanced due diligence for high-risk customers and PEPs; risk-based simplification only where permitted.
- For banks, CDD for occasional transactions above USD 15,000, linked transactions above that threshold, and occasional wire transfers of any value.
- Preserve records, transaction-monitoring evidence, and analysis results; conduct staff screening/training and independent review.
- Apply data refresh intervals proportionate to risk; five years is the maximum for low-risk customers under the mirrored procedure.
- Digital identity onboarding and updates are subject to the dedicated e-KYC procedure and system rules.

**Source limitation:** the official CBE PDF fetch endpoint rejected direct opening. The three direct URLs and CBE listing are retained; the Bankawy article is secondary corroboration, not a replacement for the original circular.

## 5. Current AML source map — add to copilot corpus

Cross-link the six September 2026 procedures and the PEP guide with:
- Law 80/2002, especially Arts 1, 8, 9 bis(1), 14 and 17 bis(1), as amended.
- Executive Regulation 951/2003, especially Arts 3, 21 bis, 22 bis(B), 50 bis(A) and related targeted-sanctions provisions, as amended by Decision 3331/2023.
- FRA Decision 161/2024 for non-bank financial institutions.
- AML Unit Decision 1/2024 targeted-financial-sanctions mechanism.
- Law 8/2015 on terrorist entities/persons lists and its amendments.
- Central Bank Law 194/2020.
- New AML Unit September 2026 CDD procedures and CBE circulars.

Do not conflate these instruments:
- The CDD procedures are Unit-issued operational rules/guidance approved by the Unit's Board of Trustees.
- CBE circulars communicate supervisory implementation requirements to banks and regulated sectors.
- FRA Decision 161/2024 is a binding Board decision for non-bank financial institutions.
- Law 80/2002 and PM Decision 951/2003 remain the statutory and executive-regulation framework.
