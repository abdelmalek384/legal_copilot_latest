## 1. Scope and substantive topics
- Governs selected personal-status issues including divorce, judicial separation, absence/missing persons, maintenance-related matters, lineage and effects of marriage.
- It is not a complete unified Personal Status Code; must be read with Law 25/1920, Law 100/1985, Law 4/2005, Law 2/2006, Law 140/2017, Personal Status Procedures Law 1/2000, Family Courts Law 10/2004 and applicable religious/personal-status jurisdiction rules.
- The 34 articles include:
  - Divorce/repudiation and its documentation.
  - Judicial separation for absence, harm and other grounds.
  - Missing persons and determination of death.
  - Maintenance, child support and accommodation.
  - Custody and visitation (including later amendments).
  - Lineage and evidentiary rules.
  - Compensation/maintenance after divorce.
  - Mahr disputes and related matters.

## 2. Major amendments and exact legal relationships
- Law 103/1958: missing-person provisions.
- Law 44/1979: added Articles 5 bis, 6 bis, 6 bis 2, 18 bis, 18 bis 2, 23 bis and replaced Articles 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 16 and 20.
- Supreme Constitutional Court Case 28/2 Constitutional (judgment published Gazette issue 20, 16 May 1985) declared specified provisions of Law 44/1979 unconstitutional; do not use the 1979 text without applying the judgment.
- Law 100/1985: added Articles 5 bis, 11 bis, 11 bis 2, 18 bis, 18 bis 2, 18 bis 3 and 23 bis; corrects/replaces relevant provisions. Gazette corrigendum published issue 33, 15 August 1985.
- Law 4/2005: amended Article 20 on child custody age.
- Law 2/2006: missing-person provisions.
- Law 140/2017: missing-person provisions.
- Secondary relations index also lists amendments 44/1979, 100/1985, 4/2005, 2/2006 and 140/2017; verify the 103/1958 link and all later amendments in Gazette.

## 3. Important judicial interpretation
- Supreme Constitutional Court judgment Case 5/8 Constitutional (6 January 1996) declared Article 18 bis 3 added by Law 100/1985 unconstitutional to the extent that it required divorced father to provide a suitable residence for children and custodial mother even where children had sufficient present assets or mother already had a residence; it also struck the timing restriction tied to the divorced wife's idda in specified rented-home circumstances.
- This judgment must be represented as a judicial invalidation relation to the relevant statutory text, not as a repeal by Parliament.
- Other constitutional/cassation rulings may modify interpretation of maintenance, custody, visitation, divorce and proof; each needs its own case source and access/reuse review.
