## 2. General provisions — Articles 1–8

### Article 1 — territoriality
> تسري أحكام هذا القانون على كل من يرتكب في القطر المصري جريمة من الجرائم المنصوص عليها فيه.

### Article 2 — specified extraterritorial offences
- Applies to persons who commit abroad an act making them principal/accomplice in an offence committed wholly/partly in Egypt, and specified offences against State security, forgery, currency counterfeiting and related import/export/possession where the currency is legally circulating in Egypt.

### Article 3 — Egyptian citizens committing offences abroad
- Egyptian citizens who commit abroad an act classified as felony/misdemeanour under the Code may be punished under it if they return to Egypt and the act is also punishable under the law of the place where committed.

### Article 4 — prosecution and foreign judgments
- Public prosecution for an offence committed abroad is brought only by the Public Prosecution; no prosecution where foreign courts acquitted or finally convicted and the sentence was served, subject to the statutory text.

### Article 5 — more favourable criminal law
> يعاقب على الجرائم بمقتضى القانون المعمول به وقت ارتكابها. ومع هذا إذا صدر بعد وقوع الفعل وقبل الحكم فيه نهائياً قانون أصلح للمتهم فهو الذي يتبع دون غيره.

- A later law more favourable to the accused before final judgment applies; where a later law decriminalizes the act after final judgment, execution and criminal effects cease, subject to the statutory exception for time-limited prohibitions.

### Articles 6–8
- Criminal penalties do not prejudice civil restitution/compensation rights.
- The Code does not prejudice personal rights established under Islamic Sharia.
- Book I general provisions apply to special criminal laws unless a contrary special provision exists.

## 3. Classification of offences and penalties — Articles 9–38

- Article 9 classifies offences as felonies, misdemeanours and infractions.
- Articles 10–12 define categories by prescribed penalty.
- Articles 13–23 govern principal penalties: death, life/rigorous imprisonment, imprisonment, simple/with-work detention, fines and deduction for pretrial detention.
- Articles 24–31 govern ancillary penalties: deprivation of rights, dismissal from public office, police supervision and confiscation.
- Articles 32–38 govern multiple offences/penalties and aggregation.
- The text includes historical penalty wording and amounts that may have been modified by later special laws; never quote penalty amounts without checking amendments.

## 4. Participation, attempt, recidivism and defences — Articles 39–76

### Articles 39–44 — principals and accomplices
- Define principal, inciter, agreement-based participant and aider; regulate liability where the principal has a personal defence or special circumstance.
- Article 43 covers a probable consequence of incitement/agreement/assistance.
- Article 44 bis criminalizes knowingly concealing property derived from a felony/misdemeanour.

### Articles 45–47 — attempt
- Article 45 defines attempt as commencement of execution with intent to commit a felony/misdemeanour, where completion is prevented/fails for reasons beyond the actor's will; mere intent/preparatory acts are not attempt.
- Articles 46–47 set attempt penalties and the offences for which attempt in misdemeanours is punishable.

### Articles 48–54 — criminal agreement and recidivism
- Article 48 regulates criminal agreements and includes an immunity rule for a participant who reports before any offence/search, subject to its terms.
- The source notes a constitutional judgment invalidating part of Article 48; preserve that constitutional status and exact scope rather than applying the whole article.
- Articles 49–54 define recidivism, repeat-offence consequences and habitual-offender treatment.

### Articles 55–59 — suspended sentence
- Court may suspend execution for a felony/misdemeanour fine or detention not exceeding one year if the offender's character/history/age/circumstances support non-reoffending; reasons must be stated.
- Suspension period is three years from finality; revocation and consequences are regulated.

### Articles 60–63 — justification and defences
- Article 60 lawful exercise of a right.
- Article 61 necessity to avert imminent grave danger where the actor had no role in its occurrence and no other means to avert it.
- Article 62 excludes criminal responsibility where mental/psychological disorder or involuntary drug-induced coma removes perception/choice; reduced capacity is considered in sentencing.
- Article 63 covers public employee acting under superior order or in good faith executing law/official authority, subject to verification/ reasonable-belief requirements.

### Articles 64–73
- The old juvenile provisions are marked repealed; juvenile liability and procedure are governed by Child Law 12/1996 and amendments.

### Articles 74–76
- Pardon of sentence and general amnesty; distinguish individual pardon from legislative amnesty.

## 5. Selected offences and actual text — defamation, insult and publication

### Article 302 — defamation (قذف)
> يعد قاذفاً كل من أسند لغيره بواسطة إحدى الطرق المبينة بالمادة 171 من هذا القانون أموراً لو كانت صادقة لأوجبت عقاب من أسندت إليه بالعقوبات المقررة لذلك قانوناً أو أوجبت احتقاره عند أهل وطنه.

- Criticism of a public employee/representative/person entrusted with public service is excluded from the first paragraph if made in good faith, limited to the functions/public service, and the accused proves the truth of each attributed act.
- Investigation authority/court may order administrative authorities to produce supporting documents.
- The truth defence is limited to the statutory public-function context.

### Article 303 — penalty for defamation
- Fine EGP 5,000–15,000; if against public employee/representative/person entrusted with public service because of their duties, EGP 10,000–20,000 (verify against later amendments and SCC decisions).

### Article 304 — truthful report
- No penalty for truthful reporting in good faith to judicial/administrative authorities of conduct requiring punishment.

### Article 305 — false report
- Knowingly false report made in bad faith is punishable even if it was not publicly disseminated and no prosecution was commenced on the report.

### Article 306 — insult
- Insult without attributing a specific fact but harming honour/reputation is punishable by fine EGP 2,000–10,000 under the source version (verify all later amendments).

### Article 307 — publication
- For offences under Articles 182–185, 303 and 306 committed by publication in newspapers/printed material, minimum and maximum fines are doubled.

### Article 308 — insult/defamation of honour/family reputation
- Where insult/defamation harms personal honour or family reputation, imprisonment and fine apply under the specified articles; publication increases the statutory minimums.

### SCC precedent — case 244/23 Constitutional, 9 May 2026
- Official full judgment:
  https://www.sccourt.gov.eg/DjangoPortalResources/Rules_Html/18148_23_244_1_2.html
- SCC upheld Article 185 and Article 307 within the challenged scope; earlier holdings had already disposed of parts of Articles 302, 303, 306 and 307.
- It discussed good-faith public-interest criticism and the need to prove each allegation about a public function.
- Link to checkpoint 57; do not overgeneralize the judgment.

## 6. Selected offences — privacy, fraud and breach of trust

### Article 309 bis — privacy violation
- The text criminalizes specified unlawful interception/recording/transmission of private conversations or images of a person in a private place without lawful authorization or consent, subject to statutory exceptions.
- It must be read alongside Cybercrime Law 175/2018, Personal Data Protection Law 151/2020, Constitution privacy guarantees and future-effective Criminal Procedure Law 174/2025 Article 80. Do not treat future Article 80 as current.

### Articles 336–339 — fraud/usury-related offences
- Article 336 criminalizes obtaining money/property through fraudulent schemes, false projects/transactions, false documents, misrepresentation of ownership or false identity.
- Articles 338–339 address exploitation of vulnerability/need and usurious lending within the statutory terms.
- Historical monetary amounts in the Code may be superseded by later amendments/special laws.

### Article 340 — misuse of signed blank instruments
- Criminalizes breach of trust by filling in a blank signed/sealed paper with a debt instrument/receipt or other binding document causing harm; if the blank document was obtained unlawfully rather than entrusted, forgery rules apply.

### Article 341 — breach of trust / embezzlement of entrusted property
> كل من اختلس أو استعمل أو بدد مبالغ أو أمتعة أو بضائع أو نقودا أو تذاكر أو كتابات أخرى مشتملة على تمسك أو مخالصة أو غير ذلك إضرارا بمالكيها أو أصحابها أو واضعي اليد عليها وكانت الأشياء المذكورة لم تسلم له إلا على وجه الوديعة أو الإجارة أو على سبيل عارية الاستعمال أو الرهن أو كانت سلمت له بصفة كونه وكيلا بأجرة أو مجانا بقصد عرضها للبيع أو بيعها أو استعمالها في أمر معين لمنفعة المالك لها أو غيره يحكم عليه بالحبس...

- Requires entrusted property in one of the statutory capacities and dishonest appropriation/use/misapplication causing harm; do not confuse with theft or fraud.

## 7. Amendment and version controls

- Manshurat reports last update 15 August 2021 by Law 141/2021.
- WIPO Lex PDF is only amended through Law 21/2018 and is therefore older than the Manshurat consolidation.
- SCC case 244/23 Constitutional (9 May 2026) affects constitutional interpretation of Articles 185, 302–307 within the precise scope of the holding.
- Cybercrime Law 175/2018 creates separate cyber-offences and digital-evidence rules.
- Special laws may create offences and penalties that supplement or displace the Penal Code.
- Do not rely on the old nominal fine amounts in the 1937 text without checking Law 147/2006, Law 141/2021 and later special amendments.
