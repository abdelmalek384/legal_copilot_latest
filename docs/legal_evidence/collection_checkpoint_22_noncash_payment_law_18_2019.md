## Promulgation clauses

- Article 1: the attached law governs use of non-cash payment methods and repeals conflicting provisions.
- Article 2: Prime Minister issues the Executive Regulation based on Finance Minister proposal and with CBE Governor and Cabinet approvals. Until then, existing regulations/decisions continue insofar as not inconsistent.
- Article 3: all persons covered must regularize within six months of the Executive Regulation's effective date. Prime Minister may extend for another period, or exempt some geographic areas or categories, for national-security, force-majeure or emergency reasons.
- Article 4: publication in Official Gazette and commencement the day after publication.

## Substantive provisions visibly inspected

### Article 1 — definitions
- "Non-cash payment method": a payment method that results in credit to one of the beneficiary's bank accounts, including deposit, transfer and debit orders, credit/debit cards, mobile-phone payment or other methods approved by CBE Governor.
- "Bank account": an agreement between a natural/legal person and a bank or other licensed entity to open an account used for cash deposits, cash withdrawals, settlement of cash-equivalent payments or electronic payment units; examples include current, savings, time-deposit, mobile-payment and prepaid-card-linked accounts.
- "Cash financing": financing provided by banks, real-estate-finance or financial-leasing companies, microfinance companies/associations, or other entities licensed to operate in those fields.

### Article 2 — mandatory non-cash payments
- State authorities/agencies, public legal persons, and companies wholly or majority owned by State must pay financial entitlements to members, employees, experts, board/committee chairs and members, and social-insurance contributions through non-cash means, except overseas travel allowances.
- Private legal persons/establishments must pay employee/expert/board/committee entitlements and social-insurance contributions non-cash where their employee count exceeds 25 or their aggregate monthly wages exceed the threshold set by the Executive Regulation.

### Article 3 — covered payment categories
Subject to thresholds set in the Executive Regulation:
- Payments to suppliers, contractors, service providers and other contractors.
- Granting cash financing.
- Distribution of profits arising from capital contributions in companies or investment funds.
- Payments to union members and entitlements of private-insurance-fund members/insurance compensation.
- Aid/donations by charities and other covered organizations.
- Consideration for purchase, lease, exploitation or usufruct of land, real estate or motor vehicles.

### Article 4 — public-facing services
- State authorities, public legal persons and public/private establishments providing public services or managing public utilities must make non-cash acceptance methods available to the public at all service-payment outlets, without additional charge.
- This includes outlets operated through branches, websites, collectors at customers' premises or future channels.
- A charge may be collected for additional electronic service delivery where the service is delivered to the recipient.

### Article 5 — non-cash collection of public dues
Subject to thresholds in the Executive Regulation:
- Taxes, customs, fees and fines.
- Public-service consideration and government claims.
- Cash-financing instalments, insurance premiums and private-insurance contributions.
- Donations/aid and consideration for sale/lease/use of land, real estate and vehicles.

### Article 6 — incentives
- State/public entities and private persons dealing with the public may, with Finance Minister approval, create positive incentives to encourage non-cash payment, including discounts or partial refunds.
- Executive Regulation sets the method/controls.

### Article 7 — penalties
- Violation of Articles 2, 3 or 5: fine not less than 2% and not more than 10% of the amount paid in cash, capped at EGP 10 million.
- Persons who deliberately split payments to evade thresholds are penalized.
- Violation of Article 4: fine from EGP 100,000 to EGP 300,000.
- Fines are doubled for recidivism as stated in the article.
- Do not apply these penalties without checking the exact facts, current thresholds, subsequent amendments and applicable Executive Regulation.

### Article 8 — administrative responsibility
- Without prejudice to criminal liability of natural persons, the responsible administration of a legal person is liable for the fines if it knew of the violation and its failure to perform duties contributed to the offence.
- The legal person is jointly liable with the convicted person for financial penalties and compensation.

### Article 9 — proceeds
- Fine proceeds go to a special account within the Unified Treasury Account to support non-cash payment infrastructure, government entities and public awareness; surplus rolls over year to year.
