# Collection checkpoint 73 — State Private Property Law 168/2025, Regulation 200/2026 and Presidential Decision 480/2026

## 1. Law 168/2025 — full text and official Gazette source

- Law 168/2025 issues the Law on Certain Rules and Procedures for Disposing of Private State Property to hand-occupiers.
- Official Gazette: issue 32 bis (w), 13 August 2025.
- Full Arabic text and original Gazette PDF link:
  https://www.abdelazizandpartners.com/legal-library/state-private-property-disposal-168-2025/
- The legal library states the text was compared word-for-word with the official Gazette electronic text and no word differences were found; review date 14 October 2026.
- Law repeals Law 144/2017 on certain rules/procedures for disposing of private State property.
- Promulgation Article 4: Law commences 30 days after publication.
- Promulgation Article 1 transfers unresolved regularization applications/appeals under Law 144/2017 to new committees under Law 168/2025; prior inspection/examination fees already paid are not charged again; payment consideration follows the prior 2017 Cabinet rules where stated; rejected old-law applicants may file objections within 15 days from the new Executive Regulation's commencement.
- This is a new statutory regime, not merely a continuation of Law 144/2017.

## 2. Actual statutory text — key articles

### Article 2 — eligibility and direct disposition
- Without prejudice to Sinai Development Law 14/2012, competent administrative authority may remove encroachments administratively at the violator's expense and may dispose by direct agreement of State private property under its authority to:
  - hand-occupier who had actually built on the land;
  - person who had actually reclaimed or cultivated the land;
  - qualifying interstitial strips and organizational surplus.
- The relevant occupation/building/reclamation must predate 15 October 2023.
- Ministry of Defence approval is required under the conditions protecting State defence.
- Public Contracts Law 182/2018 applies only where Law 168/2025 has no special provision and where consistent with it.

### Article 3 — application period and permitted disposition forms
- Disposition may be by sale, lease, lease-to-own or licence to use, based on an application filed by the hand-occupier with the competent administrative authority.
- Initial application period: six months from the commencement of the Executive Regulation.
- The President may extend the period for further similar periods, up to a total of three years.
- Application fee maximum EGP 10,000; exact fee categories are set by the Executive Regulation.
- Submission of an application or preliminary procedure creates no legal right for the hand-occupier and no obligation on the State authority to dispose of the land.

### Article 4 — delegation and decision period
- Competent administrative authority may delegate the governor to dispose of land under its authority.
- If the authority has not decided an application within six months after the application period ends, the matter is referred to the Cabinet.
- Governor has authority over land with no identified administrative authority.

### Article 5 — committees and objections
- Competent authority forms technical, financial and legal committees chaired by a director-general-level official.
- Committees examine applications/documents and may request completion.
- A temporary acceptance may be issued within three months where applicant declares the submitted documents/data accurate; after five years from temporary acceptance, field inspection/financial claims may not be made under the stated conditions.
- Committee decisions require approval by the minister/governor/head of authority.
- Objection to the approved committee decision is filed within 15 days of notification.
- Objection committee decides within 30 days; judicial challenge remains available.

### Article 6 — agricultural water allocation
- For agricultural/reclaimed land applications, a representative of the Water Resources and Irrigation Ministry must participate in the committee and approve the water allocation actually used and whether it can continue.
- No increase in water allocation may be demanded except in accordance with Ministry-approved plans.

### Articles 7–8 — land-use restrictions and nullity
- Contract must require use of land for the purpose for which it was disposed.
- A contract violating the required-use clause is absolutely void and automatically rescinded; it cannot be registered.
- Before full payment of price, the transferee may not dispose of or deal with the land; after full payment, any later disposition must still preserve the authorized use.

### Article 9 — proceeds and allocations
- Proceeds/fees are public funds and go to the State Treasury.
- 20% of collected amounts is allocated to the competent administrative authority; for governorate-administered cases, an equivalent 20% goes to the governorate for public/national projects.
- 50% of examination/inspection fees is allocated as specified by Presidential decision.

### Article 10 — occupancy compensation where regularization fails
- Where regularization is refused or removal is temporarily impossible, authority collects occupation compensation without creating any right for the occupier.
- Statutory maximums: EGP 100 per square metre annually for built land and EGP 20,000 per feddan annually for agricultural/reclaimed land, increasing 5% annually, subject to later Cabinet fee-band decision and exemptions.
- If the authority does not collect within two months after Regulation commencement, the governorate must collect and retain 20% for governorate projects.

### Articles 11–14 — criminal proceedings, penalties and reporting
- Article 11: criminal proceedings for encroachment on State private land lapse for those granted disposition under the Law and those who surrender the encroached land within six months from the Executive Regulation's commencement; execution of sentence must be stopped where disposition/surrender occurs during execution.
- Article 12: violation of Article 8 is punishable by a fine of EGP 250,000–1,000,000, restoration/removal at violator's expense and payment of benefit value; repeat fine is doubled.
- Article 13: actual manager of a legal person is punished where knowledge and management failure contributed; legal person is jointly liable for financial penalties/compensation.
- Article 14: quarterly reports on regularization applications are prepared for Cabinet and President.

## 3. Executive Regulation — PM Decision 200/2026

- Official Amiri Press full text:
  https://www.alamiria.com/Sec/TashTxt?id=cEpwOo6BfmY%3D
- PM Decision 200/2026 issues the Executive Regulation of Law 168/2025.
- Signed 18 January 2026; effective the day after publication.
- The Regulation provides:
  - eligible built land: building must be stable/fixed and existing before 15 October 2023; empty fenced land without a fixed building is excluded;
  - eligible agricultural/reclaimed land actually cultivated/reclaimed before 15 October 2023;
  - seasonal rain-fed cultivation: lease or use licence only;
  - interstitial strips/organizational surplus must be adjacent to applicant's property, cause no third-party harm, not damage access/façades and preserve access to public roads;
  - application form 1, ID, correspondence address, certified survey map/coordinates/boundaries, examination-fee receipt and proof of pre-15 October 2023 occupation where available;
  - electronic submission through National Regularization Platform or technology centres at governorates;
  - examination fees by land size/location, inspection fees, field inspection, survey, satellite-image analysis and Ministry of Defence approval;
  - pricing based on land type, area, activity/revenue, comparable price, location, utilities and infrastructure;
  - sale payment: 25% down payment within one month after notice, remaining price in three annual instalments, 7% return from contract to payment; 10% discount for full payment at contract; early payment of remaining instalments within one year waives interest;
  - where necessary, down payment for built land may be reduced to 15%, with instalments up to seven years;
  - ownership is not transferred until full payment and final contract;
  - non-sale dispositions follow contract payment terms;
  - authority may terminate contract and recover land on default;
  - land database, quarterly reports and State land-recovery committee oversight.
- Obtain the original Gazette PDF and all model forms/annexes; do not use the secondary law page's statement that no Executive Regulation exists because the official Amiri Press has issued Decision 200/2026.

## 4. Presidential Decision 480/2026 — latest extension of the application deadline

- Official Gazette repository record:
  https://dspace.id.com.eg/items/03b3721d-4847-4be0-8130-5d4bf38da540
- Gazette: issue 45 bis (c), 10 November 2026.
- Decision 480/2026 extends the period in Article 3(1) of Law 168/2025 for another similar period.
- The original period was six months from commencement of PM Decision 200/2026; reported initial expiry was 18 July 2026.
- Decision 480/2026 grants an additional six months. Secondary reporting describes this as extending the deadline by six months; exact final date should be calculated from the original Gazette commencement and checked against the signed Decision text.
- Decision is not an amendment to the eligibility date (15 October 2023), nor does it automatically grant ownership or regularize any application.
- It only extends the time to file applications; all substantive eligibility, approvals, pricing and payment conditions remain.

## 5. Cross-law relationships

- Law 168/2025 ↔ Law 144/2017 (repealed), PM Decision 18/2017 (legacy pricing/payment rules where transitional provision refers), Public Contracts Law 182/2018, Civil Code 131/1948, Real Estate Registration Law 114/1946.
- Sinai Development Law 14/2012: special geographic restrictions and Defence/security approvals.
- Agriculture Law 53/1966 and Water Resources Law 147/2021: agricultural land/water allocation.
- Desert Lands Law 143/1981, New Urban Communities Law 59/1979, Building Law 119/2008 and Real Estate Tax Law 196/2008.
- Antiquities Law 117/1983, Roads Law 84/1968, Nile Protection Law 48/1982, Environment Law 4/1994 and heritage Law 144/2006.
- Expropriation Law 10/1990 may apply to public-benefit acquisition and must be distinguished from regularization of hand-occupation.
