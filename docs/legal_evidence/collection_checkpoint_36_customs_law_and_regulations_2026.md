## 1. Law 207/2020 — structure and selected text

### Promulgation
- Article 1: applies attached Customs Law without prejudice to international agreements, electricity/petroleum/mining project agreements, customs exemptions under other laws, Special Economic Zones Law 83/2002 and Investment Law 72/2017.
- Article 2: replaces references to Customs Exemptions Law 186/1986 with "Customs Law" throughout existing laws/regulations/decisions.
- Article 3: final settlement of temporary-release goods released before commencement under the old rules, within one year.
- Article 4: Finance Minister to issue Executive Regulation within six months; prior regulations continue insofar as consistent.
- Article 5: repeals Customs Law 66/1963, Customs Exemptions Law 186/1986 and conflicting provisions.
- Article 6: publication and commencement day after publication.

### Definitions — Article 1
- Defines customs territory/line, customs control zone/circle, customs points, carrier, shipping agent, bill of lading, manifest, goods, origin/source, prohibited goods, transit goods, tariff, customs declaration, customs broker, inspection, customs tax, additional tax, customs warehouse, temporary storage, post-clearance audit, dry port, duty-free market, free zone, single window, advance ruling, transit, smuggling, multimodal transport and electronic tracking.
- Additional tax is 1.5% of unpaid customs tax for each month or part from due date until payment (subject to current amendments and application rules).

### Customs Authority and controls — Articles 2–11
- Article 2: Customs Authority controls imports/exports/transit, clearance/release, collects customs/other taxes/fees, manages temporary admission and drawback, enforces laws and protects IP at customs border.
- Authority may track imported goods offered for sale online and goods moving under indirect transit.
- Articles 3–7: customs control zones, customs circles/points, judicial-police powers, searches, boarding transport, manifest requests, seizure and tracking suspected smuggled goods.
- Article 8: importers/exporters/customs brokers/shipping/transport companies and others keep customs records/documents for five years from release (registers from closing date); traders in foreign goods keep proof of origin.
- Article 9: post-clearance audit after written authorization; access records and documents; may re-calculate tax where fraud/deception proved.
- Article 10: investigation of customs enforcement officers requires written request by Minister except flagrante delicto.
- Article 11: performance incentives and use of part of fines/compensation proceeds.

### Customs tax, valuation and exemptions — Articles 12–24
- Article 12: imported goods subject to tariff/customs tax and other dues; no release before customs formalities and payment unless law provides otherwise.
- Article 13: tariff rates/schedules amended by Presidential decision, subject to Parliament approval; treaty tariff rates apply from their effective date.
- Article 14: ad valorem and specific-duty rules; vehicle licence description restrictions for five years after release.
- Article 15: instalment payment up to one year for qualifying machinery/equipment/production lines of production projects, with additional tax; national infrastructure projects may be exempt.
- Articles 16–17: import/export customs valuation; imported goods include actual value plus costs to port of arrival; foreign currency conversion uses CBE rate at declaration registration or Minister's rate.
- Article 18: single-window, advance ruling and out-of-hours/outside-circle services fee capped at EGP 10,000.
- Articles 19–21: full/partial customs exemptions for specified State/security, diplomatic, personal, medical and other categories, subject to conditions and reciprocity.
- Article 22: 5% of value or lower tariff rate for certain machinery/equipment/production lines and specified projects.
- Article 23: 5% rate for specified tourist-transport vehicles under statutory conditions.
- Article 24: restrictions on disposal/use of exempt goods; violations may trigger payment of customs tax and other taxes/fees.

### Customs procedures, transit, temporary admission, warehousing and smuggling
- Later chapters regulate customs declaration, manifest, single-window, pre-arrival data, ACI/ACID, green/yellow/red risk lanes, customs valuation, customs brokers, warehouses, free zones, temporary admission, drawback, transit, post-clearance audit and smuggling offences.
- The law includes criminal and administrative sanctions; exact article-level extraction should be mapped separately from the Executive Regulation and tariff schedules.

## 2. Executive Regulation 430/2021 — text inspected in official consolidated 2024 reference

- Promulgation repeals the 2006 Customs Executive Regulation (Decision 10/2006) and Customs Exemptions Regulation (Decision 861/2005), except customs-circle decisions; models/forms are set by Finance Minister and traded electronically.
- Article 1 definitions include general manifest, cargo extracts, postal parcels, containers, inventory, visual/qualitative verification, examination, matching, green/yellow/red clearance lanes, direct/indirect transit, transaction value, identical/similar goods, deductive/computed value, logistics zones, AEO, financial solvency, ACI/ACID and single-window platform.
- Green lane: low-risk imports/exports released without opening after dues paid and regulatory conditions met.
- Yellow lane: missing documents/clarifications; after completion, risk engine assigns green or red; failure to submit required documents moves to red.
- Red lane: normal inspection, verification and examination.
- Articles 2–12 regulate customs border IP enforcement:
  - IP owner/legal representative may complain to Customs IP Protection Department to stop release of commercial shipments suspected of infringing copyright, trademarks, geographical indications, industrial designs/models, patents and integrated-circuit layout designs.
  - Complaint must include claimant details, shipment/country/bill-of-lading/port/importer/goods details, evidence of infringement and proof of IP ownership/licence.
  - Claimant deposits cash security or bank guarantee equal to one-quarter of shipment value as assessed by Customs.
  - Final release may be suspended for no more than 10 working days; defendant may grieve to Customs Head within 3 working days; claimant must seek judicial provisional measures within the statutory period or goods proceed to release, subject to expenses.
- Regulation further covers ACI/ACID pre-arrival cargo data, manifests, risk selection, transit, customs valuation, temporary admission, warehouses, free zones, AEO programme, recordkeeping and clearance procedures.
- Full 5,778-line text contains annotations/instructions and amendments; do not confuse binding regulation text with non-binding Customs Authority circulars/instructions embedded as comments.
