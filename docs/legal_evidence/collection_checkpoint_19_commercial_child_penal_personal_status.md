# Collection checkpoint 19 — Commercial Code text and child/personal-status source leads

## 1. Commercial Code — Law 17/1999 (full searchable government-hosted text)

- PDF: https://portal.eta.gov.eg/sites/default/files/2024-10/law-17-1999.pdf
- Hosted on the Egyptian Tax Authority portal; document itself identifies the General Authority for Government Printing Offices (Al-Amiriya Press) and "second edition", prepared/reviewed by named Court of Cassation lawyers.
- Searchable full text inspected (3,767 extracted lines).
- The document says the law was published in Official Gazette issue 19 (bis), 17 May 1999.
- Promulgation provisions:
  - Repeals the Commercial Code issued by High Order on 13 November 1883 except Chapter 1 of Book 2 concerning partnerships of persons, which remains.
  - Original commencement was 1 October 1999, while cheque provisions commenced 1 October 2001 after Law 168/2000 amendments to the commencement clauses.
  - The PDF contains footnotes identifying Law 168/2000 changes to the commencement dates and cheque-date transitional rule.
- Structure from the official-hosted contents:
  - Book 1: general commerce (commercial acts, traders, commercial books, commercial register, business/store, stock exchange).
  - Book 2: commercial obligations and contracts (technology transfer, commercial sale, commercial pledge, public warehouses, commercial agency, brokerage, transport).
  - Book 3: banking operations.
  - Book 4: commercial instruments (bills of exchange, promissory notes, cheques and common provisions).
  - Book 5: bankruptcy and preventive composition.
  - The document also includes the retained chapter on partnerships of persons from the 1883 Code.
- Selected actual opening provisions:
  - Article 1 applies the Code to commercial acts and natural/legal persons having trader status.
  - Article 2 applies party agreement, then Commercial Code/other commercial statutes, then commercial custom/usages, then Civil Code; agreements/custom cannot override Egyptian public order.
  - Article 3 where an agreement is commercial for only one party, commercial law applies only to that party's obligations unless otherwise provided.
  - Articles 4–8 identify commercial acts, professional commercial activities and acts ancillary to a trader's business.
  - Article 10 defines traders to include persons professionally carrying on commercial activity in their own name/account and companies in statutory company forms regardless of purpose.
  - Articles 21–29 regulate commercial books and records, retention, court access and sanctions.
  - Articles 34–43 regulate the commercial store/business, its elements, transfer, registration/publicity, debts and non-compete restrictions.
  - Article 47 provides joint liability among persons jointly bound by a commercial debt unless law or agreement provides otherwise.
- This is an unusually useful text acquisition. However, it is not proven to be a fully updated consolidated version: amendment chain, especially Law 168/2000 and later laws affecting bankruptcy/cheques/companies/financial markets, must be reconciled article by article.

## 2. Personal Status Litigation Procedures — Law 1/2000 (full secondary text leads)

- Secondary full-text pages:
  - https://www.mksegypt.org/ar/laws/24131
  - https://www.laweg.net/framePlain.aspx?ItemID=34731&NID=43122&Type=6&action=ViewActivePages
  - https://www.elsayyad.net/Legislation/01/elsayyad_2000-001.pdf
- Official Gazette locator indicated by the secondary source: issue 4 (bis), 29 January 2000.
- Scope: procedures for personal-status and endowment litigation; gap-filling references to Civil and Commercial Procedure Law, Evidence Law and Civil Code rules on estate administration/liquidation.
- Promulgation clause states commencement one month after the day following publication.
- These are secondary-hosted full-text leads, not a verified official Gazette acquisition. Reconcile against Gazette scan, amendments, Constitutional Court decisions and Minister of Justice implementation rules.

## 3. Child Law — Law 12/1996 and Amendment Law 126/2008

- Full base-law text lead: https://www.mksegypt.org/public/index.php/ar/laws/23925
- Secondary PDF/document lead: https://manshurat.org/node/12718
- Official government-hosted English version of Law 12/1996: https://egcert.eg/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/ChildLawno126english_eng.pdf
- The English government-hosted document notes Official Gazette issue 13 supplement, 28 March 1996, and amendment by Law 126/2008, Gazette issue 24 bis, 15 June 2008 (with an additional issue 28 reference on 10 July 2008).
- Law 12/1996 Article 2 defines child for care provisions as a person under 18 years old; Article 3 gives priority to child protection/interests in decisions/procedures concerning childhood.
- Law 126/2008 amendment text lead: https://www.laweg.net/framePlain.aspx?ItemID=8964&NID=7658&Type=6&action=ViewActivePages
- The 2008 amendment substitutes numerous provisions, including Articles 2, 3, 4, 17, 49, 53, 54, 65, 68, 70, 75, 86, 94–99, 101, 104, 107, 111–117, 125, 127 and others; it also amends Penal Code 58/1937 and Civil Status Law 143/1994.
- PM Decision 2075/2010 is listed as the Executive Regulation in secondary indexes; verify exact official Gazette copy and any later amendments.
- Do not rely on the 1996 base law alone or on an English translation as the current authoritative Arabic law.
