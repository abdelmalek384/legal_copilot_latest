# Supreme Constitutional Court — Case 21/30 (2014), Labour Law 12/2003 and social-services funds

**Court:** Supreme Constitutional Court of Egypt  
**Case:** Case No. 21 of Judicial Year 30 (Constitutional)  
**Judgment date:** 13 December 2014  
**Publication:** Official Gazette, issue 51 bis (C), 22 December 2014  
**Acquisition status:** The judgment text is reproduced in a secondary-hosted PDF compilation of labour-fund materials. It is not a retrieved original Gazette binary and has no primary-byte hash in this repository. A later court-text reproduction also summarizes the binding effect and expressly identifies the fall of Article 2 of Decision 217/2003.  
**Trust:** secondary reproduction; text captured; not official-byte matched; not lawyer-reviewed.

## Issues and disposition (structured)

The case challenged parts of Article 223 of Labour Law 12/2003 and provisions of Ministerial Decisions 216/2003 and 217/2003 governing the national Social, Health and Cultural Services Fund.

The reproduced judgment records the following disposition:

1. The challenge to paragraph 2 of Article 223 (the statutory contribution obligation) was rejected.
2. Paragraph 3 of Article 223 was held unconstitutional to the extent it did not set a maximum annual contribution per worker and did not specify the services supplied by the Fund.
3. Paragraph 5 of Article 223 was held unconstitutional to the extent it did not state how the Fund's financial resources were to be handled.
4. The relevant penalty provision in Article 256 was held inapplicable to the extent it enforced the invalidated paragraphs.
5. The judgment set the day after publication in the Official Gazette as the date for the effects of the ruling, subject to the claimant's benefit.

The reproduced judgment's reasoning connects the service catalogue in Article 2 of Ministerial Decision 217/2003 to the invalidated statutory delegation. A later court-text reproduction expressly records that Article 2 of Decision 217/2003 fell as a consequence of Case 21/30. Accordingly, the old Article 2 service catalogue must not be treated as operative authority.

The judgment also invalidated item 1 of Article 2 and Article 7 of Ministerial Decision 216/2003. Decision 16/2012 later expressly repealed Decisions 214/2003 and 216/2003, so those provisions are historical for an additional reason.

## Legal Copilot transition implications

- **Decision 217/2003:** only Article 2 is expressly recorded as having fallen. Do not state that all three articles of Decision 217 were annulled. Article 1 and Article 3 were not separately shown to have fallen in this acquisition pass; their present relevance must be assessed against Law 14/2025.
- **Decision 16/2012:** Article 2 refers to services under Decision 217/2003. That reference does not revive the fallen Article 2 of Decision 217.
- **Law 14/2025:** Articles 272–274 now provide a new statutory framework for establishment-level services and the national Social, Health and Cultural Services Fund. Article 274 itself lists ten service categories. Article 273 requires a Prime Minister decision for the Fund Board and its operating/accounting framework; no such new PM decision was located in this research pass.
- **Do not carry forward the old five-/eight-pound contribution rules or quarterly-payment mechanics from Decisions 216/2003 or 16/2012 as current rules.** The current Law 14/2025 statutory contribution and the new PM Article 273 framework must be analyzed independently.
- The ruling concerns the predecessor Law 12/2003 regime. It is not, by itself, a ruling on the validity of the later Law 14/2025 framework.
