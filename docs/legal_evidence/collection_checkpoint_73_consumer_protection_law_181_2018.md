## 1. Law 181/2018 — scope and consumer rights
- Applies to consumer products and services, including used goods sold by a supplier, but excludes financial/banking services regulated by CBE and non-bank financial markets/instruments under their special laws.
- Repeals Consumer Protection Law 67/2006.
- Economic Courts have jurisdiction over civil/commercial disputes; Administrative Court has jurisdiction over administrative disputes.
- Fundamental rights under Article 2 include health/safety, accurate information, free choice of quality-compliant products at fair competitive prices, dignity, participation in consumer-protection bodies, accessible litigation and fair compensation.
- Supplier definitions cover natural/legal persons, manufacturers, importers, exporters, sellers, lessors, distributors, marketers and electronic-commerce suppliers; advertiser includes digital platforms/media intermediaries.
- Supplier obligations include product safety/quality, clear Arabic disclosures, source/price/characteristics, displayed all-inclusive prices, invoice, no misleading conduct, no concealment of strategic products, and notice of defects/risks.
- Article 8 prohibits withholding strategic products from circulation by concealment, failure to offer for sale, refusal to sell or other means; PM decision designates strategic goods and controls.
- Articles 17–18: consumer may exchange/return goods within 14 days without reasons or expense, subject to statutory exceptions and better contractual/statutory rights.
- Article 19: supplier must notify Consumer Protection Agency within seven days after discovery/knowledge of defect; immediately where health/safety risk, and stop production/dealings and warn consumers.
- Article 21: within 30 days of receipt, consumer may exchange/return goods and recover price if defective/nonconforming.
- Article 22: durable goods warranty at least two years from delivery; if installation/operation required, warranty begins at actual installation/operation, subject to maximum two-month period from receipt.
- Article 24: repeated same defect more than twice in first year that materially affects functionality triggers replacement/refund.
- Article 27: producer, supplier and distributor may be jointly liable for damage caused by product defect/handling/storage/marketing, according to their roles.
- Article 28 invalidates terms that reduce supplier obligations under the law/regulation.
- Article 29 protects consumer data and privacy; disclosure only on express consent or statutory investigative/court authority.
- Articles 30–35 cover instalment sales, early repayment, time-share, repair centres, used goods and used vehicles.
- Articles 36–43 govern distance contracts and pre-contract information, correction/modification window, withdrawal and delivery.

## 3. Law 20/2024 — amendment to Article 71
- Replaces Article 71 of Law 181/2018.
- Article 71 concerns criminal penalties for withholding strategic goods in violation of Article 8.
- Secondary transcription of replacement provision states:
  - Imprisonment of at least one year and a fine of at least EGP 150,000 and not more than EGP 3 million or equivalent value of goods, whichever is greater.
  - Recidivism: imprisonment from two to five years and doubling minimum/maximum fine limits.
  - Court may order confiscation of goods, closure of place for up to six months, publication of judgment in two widely circulated daily newspapers at convict's expense, and cancellation of licence.
- Effective day after publication.
- The Ministry of Finance record confirms the amendment is in force and Gazette issue 14 bis, 5 April 2024. The Gazette PDF retrieval timed out; use the official Gazette copy to verify exact wording before legal use.

## 4. Executive Regulation Decision 822/2019
- Provides detailed procedures for product data, invoices, warranty, repair, replacement/refund, distance contracts, time-share, consumer complaints, agency investigations, product recalls, strategic goods, supplier records, consumer associations and enforcement.
- Full regulation text is available in LawHub/Laweg/WIPO sources; extract article-by-article and compare against the Gazette.
- Regulation and statute must be read together; where law grants 14/30-day rights, do not replace them with different periods in guidance unless a valid statutory/regulatory exception applies.
