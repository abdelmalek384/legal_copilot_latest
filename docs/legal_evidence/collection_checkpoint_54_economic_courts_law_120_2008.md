## 1. Promulgation and institution
- Gazette issue 21 (supplement), 22 May 2008; commenced 1 October 2008.
- Establishes an Economic Court within the territorial jurisdiction of each Court of Appeal.
- Originally formed of first-instance and appellate circuits; Law 156/2024 adds Economic Felony Courts at first instance and appellate level.
- Law 120/2008 applies notwithstanding conflicting general provisions; transfers pending cases to the newly competent economic courts under transition rules.
- Law requires application of Criminal Procedure, Cassation Appeals, Civil/Commercial Procedure and Evidence Laws where no special rule is provided.

## 2. Jurisdiction
- Article 4 lists statutory fields whose disputes/offences fall within Economic Court jurisdiction, including:
  - Companies and company formation.
  - Capital Market Law 95/1992.
  - Central Depository/registry.
  - Investment, commercial agencies, competition, consumer protection.
  - Banking, insurance, financial leasing/factoring, real-estate finance, consumer finance and other non-bank financial activities.
  - Intellectual property and related statutes.
  - Electronic signature, telecommunications, cybercrime, money laundering and other listed economic statutes.
- Exact version of Article 4 and list of special statutes must be extracted from the latest text, including Law 156/2024 additions.
- Economic Court jurisdiction may include civil/commercial claims and criminal offences under statutes listed in Article 4; distinguish jurisdiction type and procedural track.

## 3. Key provisions and amendments

### Original law
- Article 1: establishes Economic Courts and their circuit structure.
- Article 2: transfers cases and appeals based on new subject-matter jurisdiction, with transition exceptions for cases already decided or reserved for judgment.
- Article 3: designates urgent-matters judge to issue provisional orders, urgent relief and payment orders.
- Article 4: defines economic jurisdiction by reference to enumerated statutes.
- Article 5: allocates misdemeanour cases under listed statutes to first-instance economic circuits and appeals to appellate economic circuits; after 2024, felony circuit structure is also specified.
- Article 7: economic courts have jurisdiction over execution disputes and insolvency/restructuring/settlement/bankruptcy disputes as specified.
- Article 8: creates Preparation and Mediation Authority to prepare and mediate civil/commercial economic cases, excluding criminal, appellate, urgent and specified cases.
- Articles 10–12: appeal and cassation arrangements, including special cassation screening chamber and subject-matter rules.

### Law 146/2019
- Adds Economic Court execution judge and execution administration (Articles 7 bis, 7 bis A, 7 bis B).
- Adds/expands the preparation/mediation process and procedural handling of economic disputes.
- Modifies jurisdiction and execution mechanisms.
- Exact Gazette text and affected articles should be mapped.

### Law 156/2024
- Gazette issue 27 bis (d), 10 July 2024.
- Adds Economic Felony Courts at first instance and appellate level.
- Amends Articles 1, 2, 4, 5, 9, 10 and related provisions (verify exact final list from Gazette).
- First-instance economic felony circuits consist of three Court of Appeal judges; appellate felony circuits also consist of three Court of Appeal judges with specified seniority.
- Moves felony jurisdiction under the listed economic statutes to the new economic felony circuits, with appeals before Economic Appellate Felony Circuits.
- Adds/changes court venue and circuit formation provisions and related appeals.
