## 1. Companies Law 159/1981 — current scope
- Governs joint-stock companies, partnerships limited by shares, limited liability companies and one-person companies.
- Gazette issue 40, 1 October 1981; effective after six months.
- Law 4/2018 added one-person companies and updated competent-authority definitions and electronic establishment/services.
- Amendment chain shown by MIFT:
  - Law 212/1994.
  - Law 3/1998.
  - Law 159/1998.
  - Law 94/2005.
  - Law 68/2009.
  - Law 17/2015.
  - Law 4/2018.
- The law sets formation, capital, share issuance, governance, general assemblies, boards, audit, shareholder rights, mergers, divisions, transformation, dissolution and liquidation.

## 2. Executive Regulation 96/1982 — 361 articles
- Promulgated by Decision 96/1982, dated 23 June 1982.
- MIFT official page lists 21 regulatory attachments/amendments; latest displayed is PM Decision 2928/2022.
- Regulation covers:
  - Establishment and incorporation of joint-stock companies, partnerships limited by shares, LLCs and one-person companies.
  - Founders, subscriptions, incorporation documents, capital and share classes.
  - Commercial register and investment authority filings.
  - Board composition, meetings, powers and conflicts of interest.
  - General assemblies, notices, quorum, voting and minutes.
  - Financial statements, auditors, dividends, reserves and shareholder registers.
  - Bonds, securities, transfer of shares, mergers, divisions and transformations.
  - Branches, representative offices, foreign companies and investment zones.
  - Dissolution, liquidation and related registration/publication.
- Article 1 of regulation: any natural person with legal capacity and any legal person whose objects include company formation may be a founder; joint-stock company requires at least three founders and partnership limited by shares at least two (one general partner), except one-person company.
- If number of partners falls below minimum, company must restore minimum or convert to one-person company within six months; otherwise dissolution follows by law and remaining partner may be personally liable during that period.
- Regulation's Article 2 defines Law, Minister, Authority, administrative authority and competent departments.
