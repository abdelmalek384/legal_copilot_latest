## 2. Promulgation and commencement

- Law 17/1999 was published in Official Gazette issue 19 (bis), 17 May 1999.
- Article 1 repeals Commercial Code 1883 except the first chapter of Book II on partnerships, which is retained.
- The original Law 337 Penal Code repeal date was changed by later amendments.
- Commercial Code generally commenced 1 October 1999.
- Cheque provisions have a separate delayed commencement. Secondary legal metadata identifies 1 October 2005 for the cheque chapter and 4 July 2003 for Articles 535 and 536; verify the exact final text of Laws 168/2000, 150/2001 and 158/2003 before relying on the dates.

## 3. Actual Arabic text — general commercial rules, Articles 1–3

### Article 1
> تسري أحكام هذا القانون على الأعمال التجارية، وعلى كل شخص طبيعي أو اعتباري ثبتت له صفة التاجر.

### Article 2
> تسري على المواد التجارية أحكام الاتفاق بين المتعاقدين، فإذا لم يوجد هذا الاتفاق، سرت نصوص هذا القانون أو غيره من القوانين المتعلقة بالمواد التجارية، ثم قواعد العرف التجاري والعادات التجارية، فإذا لم يوجد عرف تجاري أو عادة تجارية وجب تطبيق أحكام القانون المدني.
>
> لا يجوز تطبيق الاتفاقات بين المتعاقدين، أو قواعد العرف التجاري أو العادات التجارية متى تعارضت مع النظام العام في مصر.

### Article 3
> إذا كان العقد تجارياً بالنسبة إلى أحد طرفيه، فلا تسري أحكام القانون التجاري إلا على التزامات هذا الطرف وحده، وتسري على التزامات الطرف الآخر أحكام القانون المدني ما لم ينص القانون على غير ذلك.

## 4. Actual Arabic text — commercial acts, Articles 4–9

### Article 4
- Commercial acts include purchase of movables for resale/lease, resale/lease of those movables, and formation of commercial companies.

### Article 5
- Professional activities are commercial, including supply of goods/services, industry, land/inland-water transport, commercial agency/brokerage, insurance, banking/exchange, warehousing, publishing/printing/translation/media/communications/advertising, commercial exploitation of software/satellite broadcasting, extraction of natural resources, poultry/livestock raising for sale, construction/real-estate contracting and related property dealing, tourism/export/import/customs-release/employment offices, hotels/restaurants/cafes/entertainment, and distribution of water/gas/electricity/energy.

### Article 6
- Maritime and aviation activities are commercial, including ship/aircraft construction, repair/maintenance, purchase/sale/lease, supplies, maritime/air carriage, loading/unloading and employment of crew/pilots.

### Articles 7–9
- Article 7: analogous activities with similar characteristics/purposes are commercial.
- Article 8: acts by a merchant relating to their trade are presumed commercial.
- Article 9: sale by a farmer of their own agricultural products is not a commercial act.

## 5. Merchant status and capacity — Articles 10–20

- Article 10: a person who professionally carries out commercial acts in their own name/account is a merchant; companies taking statutory commercial forms are merchants regardless of their purpose.
- Article 11: capacity to trade generally requires age 21; a person who completed 18 may trade subject to applicable nationality law conditions and Egyptian court permission; persons under 18 may not trade in Egypt.
- Articles 12–13: court may permit or stop a minor/incapacitated person's commercial activity; liability is limited to assets invested in the trade under the statutory conditions.
- Articles 14–15: foreign married woman's trading capacity and matrimonial property arrangements; registry/publication rules govern opposability to third parties.
- Article 16: small craftspeople with low expenses and daily subsistence income are excluded from Commercial Code provisions.
- Articles 17–19: prohibited persons who trade are still treated as merchants; merchant status applies to concealed/nominee traders and can be presumed by public self-description, subject to rebuttal.
- Article 20: State and public-law persons are not merchants, but their commercial acts are governed by the Code unless specially excluded.

## 6. Commercial books and records — Articles 21–29

- Article 21: merchants with invested trading capital above EGP 20,000 must keep books required by the nature/importance of the business, especially daily journal and inventory books.
- Articles 22–23: daily entries of commercial operations and personal withdrawals; inventory and annual balance sheet/profit-and-loss statement.
- Article 24: preserve copies of commercial correspondence and documents in an organized manner.
- Article 25: books must be free from gaps/erasures; pages are numbered and certified; books are presented to the Commercial Register at financial-year end/closure.
- Article 26: books and supporting documents must be retained five years from certification/closure; correspondence copies five years from sending/receipt, with qualifying microfilm alternative.
- Article 27: entries by authorized employees are treated as merchant entries unless rebutted.
- Article 28: court may order production of commercial books; direct inspection by the opposing party is limited to company/common-property disputes and the statutory cases; unjustified refusal may create an evidentiary presumption.
- Article 29: penalty for violating this chapter or implementing decisions.

## 7. Commercial Register and business/goodwill — Articles 30–46

- Articles 30–33: Commercial Register entries, merchant identification, certificates/extracts, third-party effect and non-opposability of unregistered mandatory data.
- Articles 34–43: definition of business/goodwill (customer connection and commercial reputation), intangible/movable elements, writing/registration/publication requirements for sale/pledge/lease of business, debts and non-compete restrictions.
- Articles 44–46: securities-market rules; trading in listed securities through an accepted broker and written client authorization.

## 8. Commercial obligations and contracts — Articles 47 onward

- Article 47: joint obligors on a commercial debt are jointly and severally liable unless law/agreement provides otherwise; same for multiple guarantors.
- Article 48: commercial guarantee rules and no right to require prior enforcement against debtor unless agreed.
- Article 49: merchant performing commercial services for another is presumed entitled to remuneration unless proved otherwise.
- Article 50: commercial loans and interest on amounts advanced for customers, subject to agreement and Central Bank reference rate.
- Article 51: commercial instructions/authorizations do not automatically terminate on merchant's death, subject to heirs' right to cancel if they stop the business.
- Articles 52–61 regulate commercial exploitation/imbalance, seasonal delivery, standard of care of an ordinary merchant, deadlines, rescission, payment hours, notices, grace periods and payment discharge.

## 10. Critical repeal/special-law controls

### Bankruptcy Book V — repealed by Insolvency Law 11/2018
- Law 11/2018 on Restructuring, Preventive Composition and Bankruptcy replaces/repeals the bankruptcy and preventive-composition provisions of Book V of Commercial Code 17/1999.
- Do not retrieve old Book V provisions as current bankruptcy law.
- Link Commercial Code 17/1999 to Insolvency Law 11/2018 and Economic Courts Law 120/2008.

### Companies
- Commercial Code preserves the old 1883 partnerships chapter but commercial company forms are mainly governed by Companies Law 159/1981 and its Executive Regulation.
- Do not treat the preserved partnerships chapter as the complete law of all companies.

### Securities and banking
- Capital-market transactions must be read with Capital Market Law 95/1992, FRA rules, CBE Law 194/2020 and sectoral regulations.
- Banking operations are subject to Central Bank Law 194/2020 and CBE regulations; Commercial Code provisions do not displace special banking rules.

### Civil Code and consumer law
- Civil Code 131/1948 applies where commercial law/contract/agreement/custom does not resolve the issue, subject to public order.
- Consumer Protection Law 181/2018 may impose special protections on commercial suppliers.
