## Structure of the 179-article law

- Promulgation provisions (Articles 1–9): repeal of Income Tax Law 157/1981; transition of old disputes/exemptions; certain pre-existing dispute settlements; Executive Regulation and commencement.
- Book I, general provisions: definitions, residence, Egypt-source income, permanent establishments, tax periods and accrual.
- Book II, natural persons: salary income, commercial/industrial income, professional/non-commercial income, real-estate wealth, tax rates and exemptions.
- Book III, legal persons: scope, tax base, deductions, bad debts, interest deductibility, exemptions, dividends, capital gains, related-party transactions and accounting.
- Book IV, withholding/deduction and collection at source.
- Book V, tax returns, assessment and administrative provisions.
- Book VI, miscellaneous/final and transitional provisions (subject to amendments and repeals).

## Opening text inspected

### Promulgation
- Article 1: attached Income Tax Law governs.
- Article 2: repeals Law 157/1981, while preserving specified old appeal committees and pre-existing exemption periods under transition rules; also repeals item 1 of Article 1 of Law 147/1984.
- Article 3: repeals listed provisions of Investment Guarantees and Incentives Law 8/1997, while preserving pre-existing exemption periods and conditions.
- Article 4: transitional relief for certain previously unregistered persons, subject to no prior registration/return/audit and registration/filing within one year.
- Article 5: certain tax disputes filed before 1 October 2004 with annual base not exceeding EGP 10,000 are extinguished, subject to taxpayer's right to insist on continuing within six months.
- Article 6: offers settlement of specified pre-October 2004 tax disputes using graduated settlement percentages of 10%, 25% and 40% on tax/other amounts by disputed tax-base bands.
- Article 8: Finance Minister issues Executive Regulation within six months.
- Article 9: effective day after publication, with separate commencement for salary income and natural/legal persons.

### Article 1 — definitions
- Defines tax, Finance Minister, ETA Head, ETA, taxpayer, capital companies, partnerships, de facto companies, project, related person, arm's-length price, royalties and dividends.
- Related persons include spouses/ascendants/descendants, partnerships/partners, 50% ownership/control relationships and companies under common 50% ownership/control, subject to the statutory rules.

### Articles 2–4 — residence, Egypt-source income and permanent establishment
- Natural-person residence includes permanent home in Egypt, presence more than 183 days within 12 months, and Egyptian public-service employment paid from Egyptian Treasury while abroad.
- Legal-person residence includes incorporation under Egyptian law, central/effective management in Egypt, or State/public ownership exceeding 50%.
- Egypt-source income includes services performed in Egypt, work paid by Egyptian employer, artist/athlete activities, permanent-establishment income, Egyptian real estate, dividends/profit shares from Egyptian companies, interest paid by resident/government, rent/royalties borne by Egyptian residents and other Egypt activities.
- Permanent establishment includes management place, branch, office, factory, workshop, mines/oil/gas wells/quarries, farms/plantations, sales outlets, construction/installation projects and certain supervisory/consultancy services lasting more than 90 days in any 12-month period; further dependent-agent and resource-exploration rules apply.
- Article 4 bis excludes certain preparatory/auxiliary activities (storage/display, purchasing, information collection, etc.) subject to anti-fragmentation and closely-related-enterprise rules.

### Article 5 — tax period
- Default tax period is calendar year, or any 12-month financial period used for accounts.
- Shorter/longer period may be used under Executive Regulation; tax becomes due day after period ends and also upon death, departure or cessation in specified cases.

### Articles 6–13 — individual tax scope and salary
- Article 6 annual tax applies to resident natural persons on worldwide income where Egypt is centre of commercial/industrial/professional activity, and to non-residents on Egypt-source income.
- Sources include salaries, commercial/industrial, professional/non-commercial and real-estate wealth.
- Article 8 graduated individual income-tax brackets shown in this transcription: 0%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 22.5%, 25%, and a 27.5% upper band for annual net income above EGP 1.2m; exact bands and thresholds are amended over time and must be reconciled with all current amendments and Law 151/2026.
- Article 9 salary income includes periodic/non-periodic payments, bonuses, incentives, commissions, allowances, profit shares and cash/in-kind benefits; includes certain foreign-source income for work performed in Egypt and specified board/director compensation.
- Article 11 taxes certain payments received from persons other than the primary employer at 10% without deductions, subject to conditions.
- Article 12 excludes pensions and end-of-service rewards.
- Article 13 lists personal exemption, social insurance contributions, private insurance fund contributions, qualifying life/health insurance premiums and specified collective in-kind benefits.
