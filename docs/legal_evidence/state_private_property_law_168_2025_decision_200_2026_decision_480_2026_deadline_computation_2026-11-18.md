# Deadline computation — Law 168/2025 Article 3 and Presidential Decision 480/2026

**Research date:** 18 November 2026  
**Primary metadata:** Official Gazette DSpace record for Decision 480/2026, issue 45 bis (ج), 10 November 2026, publisher Amiri Press; original 178.37 KB PDF is listed but Download opens a request-a-copy form.  
**Official regulation source:** SIS-hosted complete Official Gazette PDF for PM Decision 200/2026, issue 3 bis (A), 18 January 2026; Article 4 of the issuing provisions says it takes effect the day after publication.  
**Law text:** Law 168/2025, Official Gazette issue 32 bis (و), 13 August 2025; DSpace original bundle is request-only; full searchable reproduction available through court-hosted/secondary sources.

## 1. Statutory rule and trigger

Law 168/2025 Article 3 provides that the relevant disposition application is submitted within six months beginning from the date the Executive Regulation enters into force. It authorizes the President to extend that period for further equivalent periods, subject to an aggregate maximum of three years.

PM Decision 200/2026 was published on 18 January 2026. Its issuing Article 4 states that it takes effect on the day after publication. Therefore, the Executive Regulation entered into force on **19 January 2026**. The IDSC's 25 January 2026 newsletter also reports that the Law 168/2025 system began operating on 19 January 2026.

This is the statutory trigger for Article 3's six-month application window. The Law 168/2025 promulgation clause (effective 30 days after its own 13 August 2025 publication) is not the trigger for this particular six-month period.

## 2. Initial six-month period

Civil Code Article 3 requires Gregorian-calendar calculation unless otherwise provided. Civil and Commercial Procedure Law Article 15 provides that, for a period fixed in months for taking a procedure, the triggering day is not counted and the period expires at the end of the corresponding day in the terminal month. Court of Cassation Appeal 1088/58 (22 May 1990), in the context of the four-month Civil Code pre-emption period, applies this rule by starting on the day after the triggering event and expiring on the corresponding calendar date.

Applying that rule to an application deadline:
- Triggering event: Executive Regulation takes effect **19 January 2026**.
- Day of event excluded: 19 January.
- Period runs from 20 January.
- Corresponding date six months later: **19 July 2026**.
- 19 July 2026 was a Sunday (not the ordinary Friday/Saturday public-sector weekend in Egypt).

**Working conclusion:** absent a special administrative filing rule or a contrary official interpretation, the initial Article 3 application deadline was the end of **19 July 2026**, not 18 July 2026. This corrects the earlier provisional 18 July date in checkpoint 92. The calculation is supported by general procedural time-computation rules and a Court of Cassation application, but the original Gazette issue for Decision 480 and any official filing circular should still be obtained.

The two operative provisions reproduced by multiple press sources are:
- Article 1: "تمد المدة المشار إليها بالفقرة الأولى من المادة (3) من قانون بعض قواعد وإجراءات التصرف في أملاك الدولة الخاصة الصادر بالقانون رقم 168 لسنة 2025، لمدة أخرى مماثلة."

The decision does not state a separate express commencement date, does not identify a calendar end date, and does not expressly say whether the new six-month period runs from the original expiry date, the decision's issuance date, or its publication/effectiveness date.

## 4. Final deadline — what can and cannot presently be concluded

The original six-month period appears to have expired on 19 July 2026. Decision 480 was issued/published on 10 November 2026, leaving an apparent interval from 20 July to 9 November 2026.

There are at least two plausible legal constructions, and the current primary material is insufficient to choose conclusively:

**Construction A — extension as a continuation of the original period:** the additional six months are added to the original period, producing a terminal date of **19 January 2027**. This requires the extension decision to operate so as to bridge/revive the period despite being issued after the original period had expired.

**Construction B — reopening/renewal from the extension decision's publication/effectiveness:** the additional six-month filing window begins when Decision 480 becomes effective on publication (or, if the relevant rule excludes the publication day, on the following day). Depending on the applicable commencement rule, this produces a corresponding terminal date of **10 or 11 May 2027**. This construction treats Decision 480 as opening a new six-month window rather than merely adding six months to the old expiry date.

The literal phrase "لمدة أخرى مماثلة" establishes the length of the additional period (six months), but by itself does not settle its start date. The DSpace catalogue confirms publication on 10 November; it does not establish the substantive date from which the extension is counted. News reports located to date reproduce the two provisions but do not supply a reasoned legal computation or official deadline announcement.

**No final calendar deadline is certified in this checkpoint.** The repository should not encode 19 January 2027 or 10/11 May 2027 as a definitive deadline until one of the following is obtained:
1. the original Official Gazette pages for Decision 480/2026, including any additional commencement wording or issue-level publication data;
2. an official Cabinet/Presidency/State Council/competent administrative authority circular explaining the extension start and treatment of the expired interval; or
3. a binding judicial interpretation.

## 5. Separate six-month period in Law 168/2025 Article 11

Do not conflate Article 3's application period with the separate Article 11 provision concerning extinction of criminal proceedings for encroachment upon delivery of the encroached land within the statutory period. Decision 480's operative Article 1 expressly refers only to Article 3(1). It does **not** expressly amend or extend Article 11's separate period. Any claim that the criminal-proceeding consequence was also extended requires a distinct legal basis.
