## 1. Date derivation from the captured text

Article 3 says the decision is published in the Official Gazette and takes effect on the day following publication. With the recorded Gazette date of 19 November 2026:

- **Commencement:** 20 November 2026.
- Article 1 requires the covered entities to send the initial project statement to both CAPMAS and the Ministry of Finance Central PPP Unit no later than 1 January following commencement.
- **Initial reporting date:** 1 January 2027.

Article 1 then requires quarterly updates at the end of each three-month period counted from the stated 1 January date. The ordinary calendar sequence is:

| Reporting cycle | Calendar quarter end |
|---|---|
| First update after initial submission | 31 March 2027 |
| Second update | 30 June 2027 |
| Third update | 30 September 2027 |
| Fourth update | 31 December 2027 |

These are calendar-date calculations from the captured wording, not a separate authority-issued timetable. Do not silently substitute a date announced in a news article. If the Gazette date, official text, or an implementing circular is corrected, recalculate and preserve the superseded calculation.

## 2. Scope and mandatory project fields

The duty applies to ministries, departments, agencies, local administrative units, public service/economic authorities, and companies wholly owned by the State or a public legal person, where they have infrastructure, services or public-utility projects they wish to implement with private-sector participation.

For each project, Article 1 requires:

1. project name;
2. implementation location;
3. sector;
4. investment cost;
5. duration;
6. planned tender date;
7. planned contract date;
8. applicable law, regulation or rules;
9. contracting method; and
10. reasons for that method.

The statement goes to **both** CAPMAS and the Central PPP Unit at the Ministry of Finance. Article 2 assigns the Central PPP Unit the classification, database, periodic update and publication/access functions. National-security projects may be excluded where national-security considerations require non-disclosure.

## 3. Legal boundaries for retrieval and answer generation

- Treat this as a binding published Prime Minister decision, not a voluntary policy announcement.
- Do not infer that inclusion in the database approves a project, selects a contractor, authorizes direct contracting, or satisfies PPP/procurement approvals.
- Keep the decision linked to PPP Law 67/2010, its Executive Regulation (Decision 238/2011), Procurement Law 182/2018 and the specific contracting rules cited in the decision.
- Do not conflate the reporting obligation with the procurement route. A question about whether a particular route is lawful requires retrieval of the applicable route-specific provisions and approvals.
- Preserve the national-security exception as a conditional exception, not a blanket exclusion for all sensitive projects.

## 5. Minimal acceptance checks for a legal-research implementation

1. A query for the instrument number returns Decision 3660/2026, not Law 3660/2026.
2. A query for the initial reporting date retrieves Article 1 and Article 3 together; the answer explains the 20 November commencement calculation and 1 January 2027 deadline.
3. A query for quarterly reporting returns the Article 1 wording and the derived calendar quarter ends, clearly labelling the dates as calculations.
4. A query asking whether the decision authorizes direct contracting must not answer yes; it must retrieve the route-specific PPP/procurement rules or abstain.
5. A query about security exclusions must preserve the condition that national-security considerations require withholding data/publication.
6. Every answer exposes the instrument, article, Gazette issue/date and source tier. Do not label it lawyer-reviewed or verified.
