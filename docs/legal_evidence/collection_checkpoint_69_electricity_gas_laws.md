### Scope and structure
- Governs electricity utility and all electricity activities in Egypt.
- Reorganizes Egyptian Electric Utility and Consumer Protection Regulatory Agency (EgyptERA) and sets regulator powers.
- Regulates generation, transmission, distribution, sale, purchase, supply, grid operation, renewable energy integration, consumer rights and electricity-market transition.
- 80 articles across eight books:
  1. Definitions.
  2. Electricity utility regulation and regulator.
  3. Electricity activities: generation, transmission, distribution, customers.
  4. Energy-efficiency improvement.
  5. Electricity utility facilities.
  6. Transition period and competitive electricity market.
  7. Penalties.
  8. Final provisions.
- Licensing, tariff-setting, grid codes, connection, supply quality, metering, consumer complaints, market competition and regulatory sanctions are core operative areas.

### Amendment chain

### Scope and regulatory framework
- Regulates gas-market activities, excluding petroleum concession agreements issued by law, without prejudice to Egyptian General Petroleum Corporation Law 20/1976.
- Establishes Gas Regulatory Authority (GASREG) to license and regulate shipping, transport, storage, distribution, supply, marketing, trading, LNG activities and related gas-market operations.
- Requires non-discriminatory third-party access to networks/facilities, fair competition and equal treatment among market participants.
- GASREG licenses, renews, suspends and cancels licences; sets network/facility use codes and tariffs; reviews complaints; identifies eligible consumers and alternative supplier; publishes market information.
- Law regulates participants, licence requirements, activity separation, tariffs/fees, customer eligibility, network codes, competitive market and penalties.
- Executive Regulation covers licensing applications, technical/financial eligibility, fees/security, evaluation, issuance/renewal/amendment/transfer/suspension/cancellation, network access, tariffs, unbundling, non-discrimination, alternative supplier, competitive market and complaints.
