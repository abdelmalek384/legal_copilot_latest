## 2. Article map

- **Article 1 — definitions:** NFSA, food trading, importer, interested party, food consignment, food safety/quality requirements, risk-based sampling, release channel, temporary release, conformity verification, hazard and detention.
- **Article 2 — scope:** applies to imported food consignments under Decision 7/2020 that are selected for orange-channel random sampling or red-channel mandatory verification after customs procedures. It excludes importers with specified prior violations within the preceding 12 months, final judicial findings, or failures to re-export/destroy non-compliant consignments.
- **Article 3 — importer obligations:** no marketing before NFSA conformity decision; supply full supporting information; no concealment or false data; preserve seals and consignment identity; transfer/store safely; use appropriately licensed dry/chilled/frozen storage; notify material changes; ensure adequate storage capacity; use monitoring technology; control third-party storage; permit sampling; comply with destruction/re-export; pay fees/logistics; physically segregate the consignment from other consignments.
- **Article 4 — NFSA controls:** set and apply temporary-release rules; verify conformity under risk principles; notify importer of scope of checks; document detention; monitor transport/storage/seals/temperature; issue results and final release if compliant; apply non-compliance provisions if not.
- **Article 5 — temporary-release procedure:** importer transfers and stores the consignment within 72 hours after documentary/visual checks, subject to customs rules; port branch samples and seals vehicles, records drivers/vehicle identifiers and notifies destination branch; destination branch verifies seals, quantities and storage conditions; compliant goods receive release; rejected goods are re-exported or destroyed. Outside-customs storage/re-export procedure includes a 14-working-day transfer period from final rejection notification, subject to the text and applicable conditions; importer may lodge a grievance.
- **Article 6 — non-compliant consignments:** NFSA applies risk-based measures; food presenting a consumer-health risk is not eligible for Egyptian-market circulation and must be destroyed or re-exported as directed.
- **Article 7 — importer failure:** failure to comply with temporary-release responsibilities or undertakings may trigger measures and cost recovery within the period stated in the article.
- **Article 8 — importer licence suspension/cancellation:** suspension up to one year for specified breaches; cancellation for serious/repeated breaches, unpaid NFSA charges, mislocation or diversion of detained consignments, repackaging/substitution or tampering with monitoring systems. The decision provides notice and a written defence period before suspension/cancellation.
- **Article 9 — commencement:** generally effective the day after publication. Article 3(c) is delayed for six months from publication, with the Board permitted to renew that period for similar periods. This special commencement must be checked against any later renewal/implementation decision.

## 3. Crosswalk

- Decision 7/2020 supplies the risk-based import channels and sampling framework.
- Decision 9/2021 supplies the post-customs temporary-release, custody, storage, monitoring and rejection/re-export/destruction procedure for qualifying orange/red consignments.
- Decision 270/2024 expressly cites Decision 9/2021, confirming its continuing relevance to the fast-release framework; this does not itself prove every provision remains unchanged.
- Read with Decision 10/2021 on recognition of exporting-country competent authorities, Decision 269/2022 and Decision 270/2024.
