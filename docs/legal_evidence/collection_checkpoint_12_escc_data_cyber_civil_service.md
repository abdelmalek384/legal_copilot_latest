# Collection checkpoint 12 — official Gazette texts: personal data, cybercrime and civil service

**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Primary source class:** Official Gazette PDFs hosted by the Egyptian Supreme Constitutional Court (ESCC) and Central Agency for Organization and Administration (CAOA). The documents below are primary-source copies; their later amendments/currentness still need to be reconciled.

## 1. Personal Data Protection Law 151/2020

- Official Gazette PDF: https://escc.gov.eg/assets/docs/law-151-2020.pdf
- Gazette: issue 28 bis (e), 15 July 2020.
- Searchable full PDF text inspected (723 extracted lines).
- Promulgation provisions:
  - Applies to personal data processed electronically, partially or wholly, by a holder, controller or processor, in relation to natural persons.
  - Extraterritorial application covers Egyptian perpetrators inside/outside Egypt, non-Egyptians resident in Egypt, and certain non-Egyptians abroad where conduct is criminal where committed and the data concerns Egyptians or foreign residents in Egypt.
  - Exclusions include personal-use data held by natural persons, official statistical/legal processing, certain exclusively journalistic processing subject to conditions, judicial records/investigations/cases, national-security data, and data at the Central Bank and entities supervised by it (with stated exceptions for money-transfer and exchange companies).
  - The competent minister was required to issue the Executive Regulation within six months.
  - Economic courts have jurisdiction over offences under the attached law.
  - Covered entities must regularize within one year from issuance of the Executive Regulation.
  - Commencement: after three months from the day following publication.
- Article 1 definitions include personal data, processing, sensitive personal data, data subject, holder, controller, processor, availability, data security, breach/violation, cross-border data movement, electronic marketing and national-security entities.
- Sensitive data definition includes physical/mental/genetic health, biometric data, financial data, religious beliefs, political opinions and security status; children's data is always sensitive.
- Important: the current Executive Regulation is Decision 816/2025 (recorded in the existing master index/checkpoint 11 in earlier project work). The 2020 law alone is not enough for current compliance analysis.
- Consolidation/currentness tasks: verify all later amendments, implementing rules, licence/permit requirements, commencement, Data Protection Centre decisions and Gazette corrections.

## 2. Cybercrime / Information Technology Crimes Law 175/2018

- Official Gazette PDF: https://escc.gov.eg/assets/docs/law-175-2018.pdf
- Gazette: issue 32 bis (c), 14 August 2018 (cover heading in the official PDF).
- Searchable full PDF text inspected (416 extracted lines).
- Article 1 definitions include NTRA, competent ICT minister, electronic data/information, personal data, government data, electronic processing, information technology, service provider, user, software, information system, information network, website, website manager, account, email, interception, hacking, content, digital evidence, expertise, traffic data, computer, electronic media, national security and national-security entities.
- Digital evidence is defined as electronic information with evidentiary value stored, transmitted, extracted or obtained from computers/networks and capable of collection/analysis through specialized technological devices/programs/applications.
- Need extract all 45 provisions and map later amendments, relevant procedural law, communications law and NTRA/FRA/CBE rules. Do not treat article 1 definitions as full text completion.

## 3. Executive Regulation of Law 175/2018 — PM Decision 635/2020

- Official Gazette PDF: https://escc.gov.eg/assets/docs/law-175-2018-regulations.pdf
- Decision: Prime Minister Decision 635/2020.
- Searchable PDF text inspected (201 extracted lines).
- Decision's promulgation clause makes the attached Executive Regulation applicable to Law 175/2018 and states commencement the day after Gazette publication.
- Regulation definitions include encryption, encryption key, critical information infrastructure, industrial control systems and vulnerabilities.
- Critical information infrastructure includes systems/networks/assets where unauthorized access, disclosure, analysis or alteration can disrupt essential State services or cause major national economic/social loss. Examples include electricity, natural gas, petroleum, telecom, finance/banks, industry, transport/aviation, education/research, broadcasting, water/sewage, health, government services and emergency services.
- Article 2 technical/organizational requirements for service providers include:
  - Encryption at a level no weaker than AES-128 or equivalent.
  - Malware/attack protection and updates.
  - Secure protocols such as HTTPS.
  - Network/file/database access permissions and designated responsible persons.
  - Asset/equipment and software/application/database inventories.
  - Password controls per the regulation's annex.
  - Documented installation and operating procedures.
  - Service-level agreements and allocation of responsibilities with contracted parties.
  - Periodic system/software/application updates and testing before updates.
  - Annual penetration/security testing.
  - Firewalls/NGFW/UTM to protect networks and systems.
- This is a major operational compliance text. Extract remaining articles and annexes, and reconcile the regulation against any later technical standards and NTRA/CERT decisions.

## 4. Civil Service Law 81/2016

- Official Gazette PDF hosted by Central Agency for Public Mobilization and Statistics / official government site: https://www.esa.gov.eg/files/laws/10.pdf
- Gazette: issue 43 bis (A), 1 November 2016.
- Searchable full PDF text inspected (962 extracted lines).
- Promulgation scope: civil service in ministries, their departments, government agencies, local administration units and public authorities, unless their establishment laws/decisions provide otherwise.
- Repeals Civilian State Employees Law 47/1978 and conflicting provisions.
- Executive Regulation was to be issued by Prime Minister after taking Civil Service Council opinion; pending that, existing regulations/decisions continue insofar as not inconsistent.
- Article 1 states civil posts are a citizen right based on competence/merit, a public-service duty, and prohibits discrimination in applying the law on religion, sex or other grounds.
- Article 2 definitions include competent authority, unit, leadership/supervisory posts, employee, functional wage, complementary wage, full wage, budget year, competent minister and CAOA.
- Article 3 establishes Civil Service Council and its advisory role on civil-service development, draft legislation/regulations, performance assessment, training, ethics and budget proposals.
- The official CAOA law index separately lists Executive Regulation PM Decision 1216/2017 and related decisions; link and harvest the implementing rules and all amendments.
- Currentness requires checking subsequent amendments, annual wage/allowance laws, executive regulation amendments and Constitutional Court judgments.
