## 1. Law 10/2003 — scope and regulator
- Regulates all types of telecommunications except matters specifically excluded by law or required by national security.
- Establishes National Telecommunications Regulatory Authority (NTRA) as the independent regulator of the telecommunications sector.
- Regulatory principles include transparency of information, free competition, universal service and protection of user rights.
- NTRA regulates networks/services, licensing, numbering, spectrum, interconnection, quality, consumer protection, infrastructure sharing, competition, dispute resolution and technical standards.
- Law contains provisions for operators, service providers, equipment, frequencies, network interconnection, international gateways, universal service, consumer rights, tariffs, licences, inspection, penalties and NTRA governance.

## 2. Selected legal provisions and regulator powers
- Article 1 defines telecommunications, services, networks, users, providers, operators, equipment, spectrum, frequencies, interconnection, national security and emergency services.
- Article 2 sets principles: transparency, free competition, universal service and consumer protection.
- Article 3 establishes NTRA and its public legal personality/independence.
- Law prohibits establishing/operating networks, providing telecom services, international call routing or advertising such services without NTRA licence, subject to statutory exemptions.
- NTRA grants licences, sets licence conditions, monitors service quality, protects consumers, manages spectrum and resolves disputes among operators/users.
- Exact article-level obligations for subscriber registration, confidentiality, lawful disclosure, traffic data and national-security requests must be extracted from the full amended text; do not infer them from Cybercrime Law 175/2018.
