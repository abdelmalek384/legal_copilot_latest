## 2. Scope and promulgation — actual text

### Promulgation Article 1
> تسري أحكام القانون المرافق على البنك المركزي والجهاز المصرفي وشركات الصرافة وشركات تحويل الأموال وشركات الاستعلام والتصنيف الائتماني وشركات ضمان الائتمان ومشغلي نظم الدفع ومقدمي خدمات الدفع.

### Promulgation Article 2
- Where the Law or its regulations/decisions contain no special rule, Companies Law 159/1981 applies to covered banks and companies.
- Commercial Code 17/1999 applies to banks' transactions with customers, whether merchants or non-merchants, regardless of transaction nature, subject to the Law's special rules.

### Promulgation Article 4
- Covered entities must regularize their status within one year of commencement; CBE Board may extend for one or more periods not exceeding two additional years.

### Promulgation Article 5
- CBE Board issues implementing regulations and decisions, published in Al-Waqa'i; earlier rules continue insofar as not inconsistent until new rules are issued.

### Promulgation Article 7
- Repeals former Central Bank and Banking Sector Law 88/2003 and specified provisions in other statutes; conflicting bank-specific rules on minimum capital, supervisory powers, early intervention, bank resolution, account secrecy and foreign-exchange dealings are displaced, subject to international agreements.

## 3. Actual law structure and key provisions

### Central Bank institutional status and functions
- Articles 1–6 define the CBE as an independent public regulatory authority with legal personality, affiliated with the President and enjoying technical/financial/administrative independence.
- Article 4 sets minimum paid-up capital of EGP 20 billion.
- Article 5 includes monetary policy, currency issuance, exchange-rate policy, licensing/supervision, macroprudential policy, crisis management, bank resolution and foreign-reserve management.
- Articles 10–11 regulate emergency liquidity support and extraordinary financing to banks subject to solvency, security and duration requirements.
- Article 16 creates a customer-data registration system for financial inclusion and banking studies subject to confidentiality.
- Articles 17–43 govern Governor/Board appointment, reporting, budget, profits and accountability.

### Monetary policy and payment systems
- Articles 44–57 govern the CBE's role as government adviser/financial agent, government financing, Coordination Council, Financial Stability Committee and National Payments Council.
- Article 50 establishes the National Payments Council, chaired by the President, to reduce cash use and promote electronic payments/financial inclusion.
- Articles 58–68 govern issuance/circulation of currency, monetary instruments and reserve assets.
- Articles 201–206 govern payment systems/services, fintech and CBE licensing/supervision of payment-system operators and payment-service providers.

### Banking supervision and resolution
- The Law regulates bank licensing, registration, governance, capital adequacy, ownership/control, mergers, branches, foreign exchange, bank secrecy, inspection, sanctions, early intervention and resolution of failing banks.
- Articles 154–174 regulate resolution of distressed banks, including a reasoned resolution decision, temporary payment stays, transfer of assets/liabilities, licence cancellation and liquidation.
- Article 154 resolution decision is effective for one year from publication/notice and may be extended by the CBE Board up to the statutory maximum.
- Articles 172–174 regulate cessation of operations, licence cancellation, deregistration and liquidation.

### Customer protection and complaints
- Article 217 requires a CBE Board regulation on customer rights, including transparent disclosure, understandable contract terms, non-discrimination, oversight of outsourced customer services, free/rapid complaints mechanisms and secure/confidential customer data/accounts.
- Article 218 establishes a special CBE unit for customer complaints; it must examine complete complaints within 30 days.
- Article 219 establishes one or more dispute-resolution committees chaired by a judicial authority member, with CBE staff and an independent expert.
- Article 220 requires a reasoned final decision within 60 days; parties may challenge before the Administrative Court of the State Council within 30 days.
- These rules are relevant to Legal Copilot's banking/fintech consumer-finance answers.

## 5. Cross-law relationships

- CBE Law 194/2020 ↔ Companies Law 159/1981, Commercial Code 17/1999, AML Law 80/2002, Personal Data Protection Law 151/2020, E-Signature Law 15/2004, Non-Cash Payments Law 18/2019, Consumer Protection Law 181/2018, Payment Systems/Fintech rules, Capital Market Law 95/1992 and FRA statutes.
- Banking secrecy, customer data, criminal investigation requests, AML reporting and consumer complaint handling must be reconciled by exact article and competent authority.
- CBE-supervised institutions and FRA-supervised non-bank financial institutions must not be treated as the same regulatory category.
