# Collection checkpoint 81 — Law 151/2026: full-text extraction of Income Tax Law amendments

## 1. Official source

- Law 151/2026 amends Income Tax Law 91/2005.
- Official ETA PDF (10 pages):
  https://eta.gov.eg/sites/default/files/2026-08/law.no_.151.of_.2026.pdf
- Gazette issue 30 bis (A), 28 July 2026.
- PDF text extracted directly from the official ETA-hosted Gazette PDF (218 line references).
- Law commences the day after publication (29 July 2026).

## 2. Articles amended

Article 1 replaces text in:
- Article 28(1), item 4.
- Article 43.
- Article 46 bis 3.
- Article 46 bis 4.
- Article 50(1), items 7 and 10.
- Article 52(1), item 1.
- Article 56.

Article 2 adds:
- Article 23, item 10.
- Article 31, item 7.
- Article 33, item 5.
- A fourth paragraph to Article 56.

Article 3 introduces a new incentive for certain listed companies.

Article 4 establishes a transitional tax settlement/regularization mechanism for small enterprises with annual turnover not exceeding EGP 10 million for specified tax periods.

Article 5 repeals the tax exemption under Article 18 of Income Tax Law 91/2005 while preserving earlier implementation rules for the period up to 2027 as stated.

Article 6 repeals specified provisions inserted by Law 30/2023 (Articles 39 bis 5, 46 bis 5, 46 bis 7, 49 bis 1 and the relevant part of Article 7 of Law 30/2023).

## 3. Article 28(1)(4) — bad debts

- Allows deduction of bad debts where the establishment took serious collection action but did not collect within 12 months after the due date.
- Serious collection steps include:
  1. obtaining an order for payment where legally available;
  2. obtaining a first-instance judgment ordering the debtor to pay;
  3. pursuing the debt in bankruptcy proceedings or concluding a preventive composition.
- Excludes debts not exceeding EGP 10,000 per debtor where total bad debts do not exceed 1% of debtor balances at tax-year end, subject to the Executive Regulation.

## 4. Article 43 — tax on disposal of built real estate/land

- Tax rate: 2.5% of the total disposal value of built real estate or land prepared for construction, excluding villages.
- Applies regardless of number of disposals, whether property is sold as-is or after construction, whether the whole property or a unit/part is transferred, and whether the buildings stand on land owned by seller or third party.
- Applies to an heir's disposal of inherited real estate/land, whether as-is or after construction.
- Applies to disposals of real estate/land owned by the seller, whether as-is or after construction, even if multiple disposals occur.
- Transactions are not treated as professional trading unless the Tax Authority proves the activity is professional for profit under Article 19(7).
- Taxable value is the value stated in the disposal contract; burden of proving non-compliance with that value rests with the Authority.
- Exempts contribution of in-kind real-estate shares to a joint-stock company's capital if the corresponding shares are not disposed of for five years.
- Tax is due within 60 days from disposal; delay charge under Article 110 applies from the day after the deadline.
- Gifts/donations/wills/usufruct rights for more than 50 years to persons other than spouse/ascendants/descendants are treated as sales.
- Compulsory sales, expropriation for public benefit/improvement and gifts to Government/local units/public legal persons/public-benefit projects are not taxable under the article.
- Tax paid is credited against tax due under Article 19(7), where applicable.

## 5. Articles 46 bis 3 and 46 bis 4 — capital gains on unlisted securities and shares

- Article 46 bis 3 taxes capital gains from disposal of shares/quotas in companies or unlisted securities, whether gains arise in Egypt or abroad, including disposals by non-residents, with tax paid within 60 days of transaction under Executive Regulation procedures.
- Excludes non-resident gains from disposal of Treasury bills.
- Article 46 bis 4 determines taxable gains by difference between sale/exchange/other disposal price and acquisition cost, after deducting brokerage commission.
- If sale/exchange is at fair value, acquisition cost is increased by the value equivalent to CBE credit/discount rate for each holding year, if disposal occurs after three years; weighted-average acquisition cost applies.
- This requires careful cross-linking to Capital Market Law 95/1992, securities listing rules, tax treaty rules, non-resident withholding and valuation.

## 6. Article 50 — exemptions for listed securities gains and qualifying dividends

- Article 50(1)(7) excludes from taxable income returns on non-government bonds listed on Egyptian exchanges and gains from listed Egyptian securities, but losses from those transactions may not be deducted or carried forward.
- Excludes gains from listed securities where the transaction results in delisting of the company's shares.
- Article 50(1)(10) exempts dividends received by a parent/holding company from resident/non-resident subsidiaries where:
  - parent holds at least 25% of subsidiary capital or voting rights; and
  - it has held that shareholding for at least two years or commits to retain it for two years from acquisition.
- The exact conditions and tax treaty interaction must be reviewed before application.

## 7. Article 52 — interest deduction and national infrastructure projects

- General thin-capitalization restriction: interest on loans/advances paid by legal persons is limited where debt exceeds four times average equity, calculated under Egyptian Accounting Standards.
- New exception for legal persons implementing national infrastructure projects:
  - debt may exceed four times average equity if loans/advances are from unrelated parties;
  - project company must contribute at least 25% of total project investment;
  - the exception lasts until completion of the essential works of the national project.
- Excludes banks, insurance companies and companies conducting financing activities as specified by Minister of Finance decision.
- Article 56 fourth paragraph provides a related exemption for interest on loans/credit facilities obtained by public-sector/public-business-sector/private-sector companies from unrelated parties to finance national infrastructure projects, subject to:
  - loan/facility term at least five years;
  - company contributes at least 25% of total investment;
  - exemption ends when the relevant project works are completed.
- This exception must be matched to the Prime Minister's list of qualifying national projects and the Minister of Finance implementing decisions.

## 8. Article 56 — dividends and distributions

- General tax on distributions: 10% of dividends without deduction of costs.
- Rate: 5% for dividends on securities listed on Egyptian Stock Exchange, without deduction of costs.
- Applies to distributions by companies/partnerships, including special economic-zone companies, to resident/non-resident natural/legal persons, subject to the article's rules.
- Dividends by a non-resident legal person through an Egyptian permanent establishment are deemed distributed within 60 days after the permanent establishment's financial year-end.
- Certain dividends received by resident legal persons from other resident legal persons and corresponding costs are excluded from the tax base under the conditions set by the Executive Regulation.
- The paying entity must withhold tax and remit it to the Central Tax Withholding and Collection Administration within five working days at the beginning of the month after collection.

## 9. Article 23(10), Article 31(7), Article 33(5) — solidarity contribution

- Adds the value of the solidarity contribution under Article 40(9) of Universal Health Insurance Law 2/2018 as a deductible/recognized item in the relevant income-tax provisions, with corresponding provisions in Articles 31 and 33.
- This links to Law 155/2026, which amends Article 42 of Universal Health Insurance Law and makes the contribution tax revenue collected by ETA.
- Read both Laws 151/2026 and 155/2026 together; the former addresses income-tax treatment and the latter the collection/treasury routing.

## 10. Article 3 — IPO/listing incentive

- Companies offering their shares for listing on the Egyptian Exchange under an approved prospectus receive a tax credit equal to 15% of tax due under the income-tax return for three years from the offering date.
- Conditions:
  - market value of the company's shares at offering must be at least EGP 50 billion;
  - offered shares must be at least 20% of company shares or have a value of at least EGP 10 billion.
- Incentive is granted only once in the company's life.
- Minister of Finance may extend the incentive period for a similar period under conditions coordinated with FRA.
- It cannot be combined with tax incentives under other laws.
- Executive Regulation must define review, verification, suspension and recovery of the incentive if conditions are lost.

## 11. Article 4 — transitional settlement for small enterprises

- Applies to establishments/companies with annual turnover not exceeding EGP 10 million for tax periods beginning 1 January 2022 through the period before March 2025.
- Tax is assessed at specified percentages/fixed amounts based on annual turnover, including:
  - 0.5% for turnover from EGP 1m to under EGP 2m;
  - 0.75% for turnover from EGP 2m to under EGP 3m;
  - 1% for turnover from EGP 3m to EGP 10m;
  - fixed amounts for businesses below EGP 1m, with bands at EGP 250,000 and EGP 500,000.
- Taxpayer retains the right to elect assessment under the ordinary Income Tax Law.
- The exact lower turnover bands and settlement conditions should be visually verified from pages 7–8 and integrated with Law 6/2025 and Law 5/2025.

## 12. Article 5–6 — repeal/transition

- Article 5 repeals the exemption in Article 18 of Income Tax Law 91/2005, while preserving prior rules for the tax period up to 2027 as stated in the text.
- Article 6 repeals specified provisions introduced by Law 30/2023, including Articles 39 bis 5, 46 bis 5, 46 bis 7, 49 bis 1 and relevant part of Article 7.
- This requires a detailed version graph for the 2023 changes; do not simply delete those provisions without recording their repeal date and transitional effects.
