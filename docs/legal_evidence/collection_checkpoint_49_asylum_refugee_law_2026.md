- Decision dated 21 May 2026; it provides commencement three months after publication. Secondary metadata calculates commencement as 21 August 2026.
- It issues the Executive Regulation of Law 164/2024 and provides transitional rules for refugee/asylum-seeker cards issued by UNHCR in Egypt before commencement.
- Existing UNHCR cards remain valid until expiry or until the Permanent Committee issues new registration documents/cards, whichever occurs first. Cards expiring during the first six months of the new regime receive the transitional extension specified by the Decision.
- The Decision requires the Permanent Committee to organize cooperation with UNHCR and implement the new registration/documentation system.

## 3. Regulation structure and key provisions (official Amiri Press searchable text)

### Definitions and central data system
- Article 1 defines the competent ministry as Ministry of Interior, the competent committee as the Permanent Committee for Refugee Affairs, UNHCR, refugee, asylum seeker and priority-care categories.
- Priority-care groups include persons with disabilities, older persons, pregnant women, unaccompanied children and victims of trafficking, torture or sexual violence.
- Article 3 requires a central database of refugees/asylum seekers, including basic and biometric data, application stages, issued documents and support/services.
- Data must be updated and secured; processing is subject to Personal Data Protection Law 151/2020 and confidentiality rules and must be limited to purposes connected to asylum examination, file management, documentation and service coordination.

### Application and reception
- Articles 4–6 regulate legal capacity/representation, family applications, required data/documents, declarations and temporary residence pending decision under Foreigners' Entry and Residence Law 89/1960.
- A legally qualified representative is to be provided without charge where an applicant lacks capacity and has no legal representative.
- Adult family members are not included in a family application without their written consent and may request separate applications.
- Application materials include identity, nationality/residence history, reasons for asylum, supporting evidence, health/psychosocial needs, prior asylum applications/protection, prior refusals/removals and travel route.
- Applicants are asked to provide available originals or best available copies; reasons for missing documents are recorded.

### Reception, biometrics and medical screening
- Article 7 regulates initial registration interviews, information about rights/obligations and appeal deadlines, privacy/dignity and female staff where possible.
- Biometric data are recorded after notice of their nature, purpose and authorized access.
- Initial medical examination is to take place within 30 days after registration, with prior notice and confidentiality.

### Decision timeframes and temporary measures
- Article 14 provides a decision timeframe not exceeding six months from application for an applicant who entered lawfully, and one year for an applicant who entered unlawfully; priority-care cases receive priority in examination.
- Article 15 allows specified temporary measures pending decision for national-security/public-order reasons, including periodic attendance, medical/psychological examination where necessary and notice of address/contact changes within 24 hours.
- Certain exceptional measures include geographic restrictions, travel-permit requirements and deferral of decision; they run for three months and may be renewed for similar periods by committee decision, subject to proportionality and appeal.
- Article 16 regulates express/implicit withdrawal, file closure, reopening and the right to submit a new application.
- Article 17 sets procedural and substantive rejection grounds.
- Articles 18–20 regulate notification, grievance to the Committee within 15 days and judicial challenge before the Administrative Court of the State Council.

### Documents and refugee status
- Article 28 provides for asylum-seeker registration cards, valid six months and renewable, as evidence of registration (not a passport or official travel document).
- Article 29 provides for refugee-status documents valid three years and renewable.
- Article 30 regulates renewal and review of continued grounds for refugee status.

### Rights and services
- Article 22 provides refugees access to the same preventive/curative health services and basic care offered to Egyptian citizens, subject to the Law/Regulation and Ministry of Health decisions.
- Article 23 coordinates support for asylum seekers, including basic humanitarian/social services and children's access to basic education.
- Article 24 sets obligations of asylum seekers, including attendance and notice of departure/return.
- The complete rights/obligations chapter must be extracted and reviewed against Law 164/2024, the 1951 Refugee Convention, 1967 Protocol, 1969 OAU Convention and relevant domestic laws.

## 6. Legal Copilot data protection and safety controls

- Refugee/asylum records contain highly sensitive personal and biometric data. Do not ingest individual applicant files, identities, addresses, case details or UNHCR records into the general legal corpus.
- Store only public legislation, official forms, public guidance and de-identified procedural information unless a lawful, explicit and secure case-specific data basis exists.
- Access to personal asylum data must be role-restricted, purpose-limited, logged and protected under Law 151/2020 and the Asylum Regulation.
- Do not provide automated determinations of refugee status; legal decisions require competent authority processes and qualified counsel.
