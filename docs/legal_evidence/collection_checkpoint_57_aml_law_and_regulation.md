## 1. AML Law 80/2002 — updated text and scope
- FRA consolidated PDF contains 446 extracted lines and shows amendments through Law 154/2022.
- Article 1 defines funds/assets (including virtual assets), money laundering, predicate offence, proceeds, AML/CFT Unit, financial institutions, designated non-financial businesses/professions, bearer negotiable instruments, competent minister, relevant authorities, law-enforcement bodies and parallel financial investigations.
- Financial institutions include banks, exchange companies, remittance providers, securities firms, investment-fund operators, postal financial services, real-estate finance/securitization, financial leasing, factoring, insurance, private insurance funds, central depository and other licensed financial activities.
- Designated non-financial businesses/professions include real-estate brokers, precious-metal/gem dealers, lawyers/accountants when conducting specified transactions for clients, gambling clubs and other designated professions.
- Article 2 criminalizes intentional conversion/transfer, acquisition, possession, use, management, safekeeping, deposit, investment, value manipulation or concealment of proceeds from a predicate offence.
- Article 3 establishes independent AML/CFT Unit at CBE, with a Board of Trustees and executive director; Law 154/2022 amended structure and added Article 3 bis.
- Article 4 requires Unit to receive suspicious transaction reports, investigate/analyse information, maintain database and exchange information with competent domestic/foreign bodies.
- Article 5 permits Unit to report evidence to Public Prosecution and request precautionary measures under criminal procedure.
- Article 7 requires competent supervisors to ensure institutions/designated professions implement AML/CFT controls and provide requested data.
- Article 8 requires financial institutions and designated professions to report suspicious transactions promptly regardless of value and apply customer due diligence.
- Article 9 requires records and customer/beneficial-owner information to be retained for at least five years after account closure, subject to longer retention request.
- Article 9 bis 1 (added Law 154/2022) requires implementation of targeted financial sanctions under relevant UN Security Council resolutions, including freezing and refusal of financial services to listed persons/entities.
- Article 10 protects good-faith reporting from criminal/civil liability where reasonable grounds exist.
- Article 11 prohibits tipping-off the customer/beneficial owner or unauthorized persons about reporting/investigation.
- Article 12 requires disclosure to Customs on entry/exit of foreign currency and bearer negotiable instruments as defined by the regulation.
- Remaining articles regulate confiscation, international cooperation, penalties, legal persons, investigation and criminal procedure cross-references.

## 2. AML Law amendment chain
- Law 78/2003.
- Law 181/2008.
- Law 36/2014.
- Law 17/2020: changes terminology from "funds or assets" to "funds" in specified provisions and expands definitions/obligations.
- Law 154/2022: adds/updates virtual assets, parallel financial investigations, AML/CFT Unit governance and targeted financial sanctions.
- FRA page labels current consolidated Law 80/2002 version as updated in 2022; verify no later legislative amendments and check all subsequent AML decisions.

## 3. Executive Regulation PM Decision 951/2003
- FRA current PDF is an updated version incorporating:
  - Decision 1463/2006.
  - Decision 2367/2008.
  - Decision 1569/2016.
  - Decision 2910/2019.
  - Decision 457/2020.
  - Decision 3331/2023.
- Decision 3331/2023 (Gazette 35 supplement A, 31 August 2023) replaces numerous regulation provisions, including Articles 1, 3, 4, 12 bis, 15, 21 bis, 22 bis, 22 bis (A), 22 bis (B), and others; it adds new Articles 2 bis, etc. Full affected-article mapping must be transcribed.
- Executive Regulation addresses CDD, customer identification/verification, beneficial ownership, risk-based approach, suspicious reporting, record retention, internal controls, compliance officer, training, wire transfers, correspondent relationships, PEPs, cross-border operations, non-profit organisations, high-risk jurisdictions and supervisory responsibilities.
- The 2025 FRA-hosted updated PDF is an official regulator compilation; compare its text against Gazette originals of each amendment.

- FRA Board Decision 161/2024: supervisory controls for AML/CFT for non-bank financial activity entities (updated version).
- FRA Board Decision 100/2020 (updated 2024): governance rules for non-bank financial companies.
- FRA Board Decision 53/2018 (updated 2024): licensing/continued licensing and ownership rules.
- AML/CFT Unit Decision 1/2024: mechanism for targeted financial sanctions under UN Security Council resolutions.
- FRA Circular 8/2026 (8 November 2026) updates customer due diligence procedures for FRA-supervised financial institutions, replacing the February 2020 version; source and operative details recorded in checkpoint 8.
- These are sector-specific supervisory instruments; do not confuse with the general AML Law/Executive Regulation.
