# Collection checkpoint 48 — Maintenance, inheritance and wills: Laws 25/1920, 77/1943 and 71/1946

## 1. Law 25/1920 — maintenance and selected personal-status matters
- Gazette: issue 54, 15 July 1920.
- Full text sources:
  - https://www.laweg.net/framePlain.aspx?ItemID=128&NID=48772&Type=14&action=ViewActivePages
  - https://lawhub.info/eg/?p=11578
  - Official/secondary record: https://manshurat.org/node/879
- Governs spousal maintenance, idda maintenance, separation for failure to pay maintenance, illness/absence, defects and related personal-status issues.
- Article 1: wife is entitled to maintenance from valid marriage contract where she makes herself available, even if wealthy or different in religion; includes food, clothing, accommodation, treatment and other customary/legally due needs. The article also specifies circumstances affecting entitlement and lawful absence/work exceptions.
- The law's amendment chain includes Law 33/1926 and Law 100/1985, plus related later amendments and Constitutional Court/Cassation rulings; exact article-level history must be extracted from the relations index and Gazette texts.
- Treat as substantive personal-status statute, not a procedure code.

## 2. Law 77/1943 — Inheritance
- Gazette: issue 92, 12 August 1943 (secondary Gazette metadata).
- Official government-hosted PDF (State Information Service / human-rights studies portal):
  https://hrightsstudies.sis.gov.eg/media/1396/قانون-الميراث-المصري-رقم-77-لسنة-1943.pdf
- Full text sources:
  - https://lawhub.info/eg/?p=11346
  - https://manshurat.org/node/12494
- Article 1: inheritance arises upon death of decedent or judicial declaration of death.
- Article 2: heir must be alive at decedent's death/declaration; unborn child may inherit if statutory conditions are met.
- Article 3: where two persons die and order of death is unknown, neither inherits from the other.
- Article 4: estate is applied in order: funeral/preparation expenses, debts, valid bequests within enforceable limit, then heirs; statutory rules address acknowledgment of lineage, excess bequests and Treasury fallback.
- Article 5: intentional unlawful killing is a bar to inheritance, subject to statutory elements/defences.
- Article 6: no inheritance between Muslim and non-Muslim; non-Muslims inherit from each other; difference in domicile does not prevent inheritance subject to stated foreign-law exception.
- Full inheritance shares, residuary heirs, exclusion, representation, unborn child, missing persons and correction of shares require article-level extraction; do not compute shares from a short summary.

## 3. Law 71/1946 — Wills
- Gazette: Al-Waqa'i issue 65, 1 July 1946; law signed 24 June 1946; effective one month after publication.
- Full 84-article transcription updated through 2026:
  https://alhasswalawfirm.com/law-library/law-71-1946
- Official/secondary source records:
  https://www.notariesofegypt.org/article.php?id=148
  https://manshurat.org/node/12495
  https://lawhub.info/eg/?p=11414
- Article 1: will is a disposition of the estate effective after death.
- Article 2: will may be made orally or in writing; where testator cannot do either, intelligible indication suffices; evidence of will/withdrawal after death is subject to documentary proof rules.
- Article 3: will must not be for an unlawful purpose or have motive inconsistent with statutory/religious principles; will by non-Muslim is valid unless prohibited by their applicable religious law as specified.
- Article 5: testator must have legal capacity to donate; special permission required in cases of interdiction for prodigality/weakness or age under 18 as specified.
- Article 17: intentional unlawful killing of testator/decedent may bar entitlement to voluntary/obligatory will.
- Article 18: testator may expressly or implicitly revoke all/part of a will.
- Article 37: will to heir or non-heir is valid; without heirs' approval, it is executed up to one-third; excess requires approval by competent heirs after death.
- Articles 62–65 cover valuation of usufruct/rights and periodic payments; other chapters address known/unknown beneficiaries, fetus, charitable purposes, mandatory bequest and estate administration.
- Full article-level extraction and exact amendments/court rulings remain outstanding.

## 4. Cross-law structure
- Law 25/1920 (maintenance) and Decree-Law 25/1929 (selected personal status) are substantive statutes.
- Law 77/1943 governs inheritance shares and estate distribution.
- Law 71/1946 governs wills and bequests, including one-third limits and obligatory bequest rules.
- Law 1/2000 governs personal-status litigation procedure.
- Law 10/2004 establishes Family Courts and settlement offices.
- Law 11/2004 establishes Family Insurance Fund.
- Civil Code 131/1948 and Evidence Law 25/1968 apply only where statutory cross-reference/gap-filling permits.
- Child Law 12/1996 and amendment 126/2008 affects child welfare/custody; court judgments may invalidate or modify application of statutory provisions.
