## Scope and promulgation
- Applies to assessment/collection procedures for Income Tax, VAT, State Financial Resource Development Fee, Stamp Tax and other similar/substitute financial levies, except where a specific law has a special rule and insofar as consistent.
- Valid tax assessment/collection procedures already completed under prior law remain valid; unfinished procedures transition to the new law.
- Executive Regulation to be issued within six months; effective day after Gazette publication.

## Main article map (selected provisions across the 93-article law)

### Articles 1–24: definitions, rights, obligations and tax administration
- Article 1 definitions: Minister, ETA Head, tax law, ETA, tax, other amounts, taxpayer, tax collector/liable person, tax period, tax return.
- Article 2 permits data/records/documents in any language with Arabic translation from an ETA-accredited entity.
- Article 3 enumerates taxpayer rights: awareness, free forms, notice of procedures, access to tax file, verify staff identity/authority, written answers, confidentiality, presence during field audit, refund of overpaid/wrongly paid tax and other statutory rights.
- Article 4 allows advance written request for ETA position on tax treatment of a proposed transaction; ETA decision within 30 days after complete documents and binds ETA unless undisclosed facts emerge.
- Articles 5–15: registration, recordkeeping, returns, cooperation with tax officials, confidentiality, information requests, related-party transfer-pricing files (master/local/country-by-country), reporting deadlines and penalties, licensing notices and government information exchange.
- Article 12 requires related-party master file, local file and country-by-country report, with ministerial exemption possible. Threshold in the original law is EGP 8m for certain master/local file obligations; later rules and amendments must be checked.
- Articles 16–24: ETA staff incentive regime, procurement authority, ETA representatives, judicial police status, staff conflict-of-interest and post-employment restrictions, State Lawsuits Authority role, disciplinary oversight, former tax employees barred for five years from handling files they previously examined.

### Articles 25–28: registration and tax identification
- Article 25: taxpayer/liable person registers within 30 days of starting activity or VAT-liability date; ETA may register a non-applicant based on available information and notify; non-registrants below VAT threshold may be required to register electronically for annual fee up to EGP 500.
- Article 26: one unified tax registration number for all tax types.
- Article 27: tax card/registration certificate issued within five working days; valid five years; required for public/private dealings and linked to incorporation/licensing.
- Article 27 bis added by Law 150/2026: temporary tax card for eight months to complete establishment/licensing procedures; tax dues during validity are payable from expiry; temporary card cannot issue e-invoices/e-receipts.
- Article 28: report registration-data changes within 30 days; heirs have 60 days after death.

### Articles 29–37: returns, periods and electronic invoicing
- Article 29: digital return/documents with electronic signature according to staged ministerial system; returns must be complete; electronic system fee capped at EGP 1,000 per year.
- Article 30: electronic signature has civil/commercial evidence-law effect if compliant with Electronic Signature Law 15/2004 and technical controls.
- Article 31:
  - VAT/table tax monthly, within month after period, including nil return unless exception approved for low-frequency exporter/importer/service provider.
  - Payroll/withheld income returns quarterly (Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct) and annual reconciliation in January.
  - Income tax returns annually: natural persons before 1 April; legal persons before 1 May or within four months after financial year-end; includes exempt periods, subject to specified exemptions.
  - Special returns on death, departure from Egypt, cessation of activity and transfer of business.
- Article 32: return corrections, extension and supplementary details.
- Articles 33–34: electronic receipts/invoices and records.
- Articles 35–37: electronic invoice/receipt issuance, required data, service providers and technical requirements.

### Articles 38–44: accounting, burden of proof, audit and assessment
- Article 38 as amended by Law 150/2026: subject to Law 6/2025 simplified SME regime and Companies Law 159/1981, taxpayers carrying on commercial, industrial, craft or professional activity must keep regular accounting books/records required by Commercial Code 17/1999, manually or electronically. Taxpayer must maintain electronic accounts for annual revenues/costs; records/invoices retained for five years after return period; Minister may issue simplified bookkeeping rules for categories.
- Article 39: ETA bears burden of proof when correcting/amending/rejecting a compliant return or amending assessment.
- Article 40: taxpayer bears burden in estimated assessment based on inaccurate/missing data, correction of own return and challenge to official report.
- Article 41: audit notice at least 10 days before, stating date/place/estimated duration; exceptions for treasury risk/suspected evasion with senior approval; taxpayer normally provides requested data within 15 days, extendable once.
- Article 42: tax officers with judicial-police authority may enter business premises during working hours without prior notice; after-hours entry requires authorization.
- Article 43: notices of amended/estimated assessment by registered mail, legally probative electronic method or documented delivery.
- Article 44: ETA may assess/amend tax within five years from end of return filing deadline; limitation interrupted by Civil Code causes, assessment notice, payment demand or referral to appeal committee.

### Articles 45–55: collection, delay, seizure, setoff
- Article 45: collection demands are enforceable instruments; ETA must notify within 60 days after taxpayer approval, appeal committee decision or court judgment.
- Article 45 bis added by Law 7/2025: delay consideration/additional tax capped at 100% of principal tax.
- Articles 46–49: execution/provisional seizure, administrative seizure and related enforcement rules.
- Articles 50–55: setoff, tax write-off, payment demands, limitation and collection; Article 50 permits setoff of taxpayer credits against payable tax/other Ministry of Finance revenues and allows clearance certificate within 40 days.
- Article 51 permits total/partial write-off in specified cases (death without apparent estate, no executable assets, final bankruptcy with closure, departure for ten consecutive years without executable assets); must preserve statutory minimum for taxpayer/heirs.

### Articles 56–67: tax appeal and settlement
- Article 56: internal review committee; taxpayer appeal filing must specify grounds precisely; committee session within 30 days and decision within 60 days, extendable for serious reasons.
- Articles 57–60: payroll withholding objections, committee composition, hearings, confidentiality, reasons and majority decisions.
- Article 61: tax appeal committees chaired by judicial member, with ETA officials and tax experts; decisions should be reasoned, state exact tax and calculations, and be issued within 60 days (extendable once).
- Article 62: appeal committee jurisdiction over unresolved objections; notice of hearing at least 10 days; may request documents.
- Article 63: confidential sessions and procedural safeguards.
- Article 64: majority decision; tax payable on committee decision even if challenged in court.
- Article 65: either party may challenge appeal committee decision before competent Administrative Court within 60 days; tax cases expedited and heard confidentially without State Commissioners.
- Article 66: taxpayer may seek settlement of appeal issues before decision; committee pauses; settlement record signed by both parties is enforceable.
- Article 67: final assessment may be corrected within five years in listed cases (no activity, exempt activity, non-taxable income, omitted exemptions, wrong rate/type, loss carryforward, failure to deduct taxes/rent/donations, duplicate assessment, cross-year income/expense allocation, decisive new documents).

### Articles 68–77: crimes, penalties, settlement
- Article 69: EGP 3,000–50,000 fine plus tax/other amounts for late return/payment up to 60 days, inaccurate return showing higher tax, obstruction of officials and listed compliance breaches; recidivism triples fine limits.
- Article 70: return delay beyond 60 days attracts EGP 50,000–2m fine; repeated failure over six monthly or three annual returns adds 6 months–3 years imprisonment or fine.
- Article 71: EGP 20,000–100,000 fines for listed registration, change, e-invoice and bookkeeping breaches; up to EGP 50,000 for failure to retain records.
- Article 72: conflict-of-interest violation by ETA staff under Article 20 attracts 1–3 years imprisonment and EGP 50,000–250,000 fine or either.
- Article 73: actual manager/partner/board chair may be liable for tax evasion by legal person; may prove lack of knowledge.
- Article 73 bis added by Law 211/2020: other tax-law criminalization/penalty provisions apply where no special rule.
- Article 74: prosecution/investigation requires written request by Finance Minister or delegate.
- Article 74 bis added by Law 211/2020: criminal limitation period starts five years after end of year in which tax became due.
- Article 75: settlement by paying 100% of tax dues before prosecution; 150% after prosecution but before judgment; 175% after final judgment.
- Articles 75 bis and 75 bis 1 added by Law 7/2025: alternative settlement compensation for non-tax-debt offences and Article 135 Income Tax Law 91/2005 withholding/deduction offences.
- Article 76: settlement for accountant offences at amount between statutory minimum and maximum fine.
- Article 77: settlement extinguishes criminal proceedings and consequences, including sentence; prosecution orders stay of sentence execution if settlement occurs during execution.

### Articles 78–81: final provisions
- Article 78 allows exchange of tax information under international tax agreements and protocols, without compromising trade/industrial/professional secrets; Law 176/2022 adds disclosure/bank information cross-reference.
- Article 79 allows temporary suspension of dealings with persons under tax-evasion investigation/prosecution in circumstances set out.
- Article 80 allows publication of names of persons with final custodial judgments for tax evasion.
- Article 81 extends law to Real Estate Tax Authority when Cabinet decides after its development/digitization.

## Amendment graph and version warnings

- Law 7/2025 (Gazette issue 6 bis (w), 12 February 2025): adds Articles 45 bis, 75 bis and 75 bis 1.
