## 2. Promulgation and general principles — Articles 1–5

### Article 1 — sources of law
> تسري النصوص التشريعية على جميع المسائل التي تتناولها هذه النصوص في لفظها أو في فحواها.
>
> فإذا لم يوجد نص تشريعي يمكن تطبيقه، حكم القاضي بمقتضى العرف، فإذا لم يوجد، فبمقتضى مبادئ الشريعة الإسلامية، فإذا لم توجد، فبمقتضى مبادئ القانون الطبيعي وقواعد العدالة.

### Article 2 — repeal of legislation
> لا يجوز إلغاء نص تشريعي إلا بتشريع لاحق ينص صراحة على هذا الإلغاء، أو يشتمل على نص يتعارض مع نص التشريع القديم، أو ينظم من جديد الموضوع الذي سبق أن قرر قواعده ذلك التشريع.

### Article 3 — calculation of time
> تحسب المواعيد بالتقويم الميلادي، ما لم ينص القانون على غير ذلك.

### Article 4 — lawful exercise of rights
> من استعمل حقه استعمالًا مشروعًا لا يكون مسؤولًا عما ينشأ عن ذلك من ضرر.

### Article 5 — abuse of rights
- Exercise is unlawful if the sole purpose is harming another; if the interest pursued is grossly disproportionate to harm caused; or if the interest pursued is unlawful.

## 3. Private international law — Articles 10–28

The searchable PDF exposes conflict-of-laws rules including:
- Article 10: Egyptian law characterizes legal relationships to identify the applicable law.
- Article 11: personal status/capacity generally follows nationality law, with specified protection for transactions concluded in Egypt.
- Articles 12–14: marriage validity, effects, divorce and separation; special rule where one spouse is Egyptian.
- Article 15: maintenance obligations between relatives follow the law of the person liable.
- Article 16: guardianship, curatorship and protection systems follow the law of the protected person.
- Article 17: inheritance, wills and dispositions taking effect after death follow the nationality law of the decedent/testator at death or relevant time, with separate form rules.
- Article 18: possession, ownership and other real rights follow the law of the situs; movable property follows the law of its location when the cause for acquisition/loss occurs.
- Article 19: contractual obligations generally follow the parties' common domicile law, otherwise the place-of-contract law, subject to party choice/circumstances; immovable-property contracts follow situs law.
- Articles 20–21: form of contracts and non-contractual obligations.
- Articles 22–28: procedural law, treaty priority, residual private international law, multiple nationality, internal plurality of laws and public policy exception.
- Exact statutory wording and exceptions must be extracted from the PDF; this summary is a map, not a complete choice-of-law opinion.

## 4. Natural/legal persons and capacity — Articles 29–53

- Article 29: natural personality begins with complete live birth and ends at death, subject to rights of the unborn child as provided by law.
- Articles 30–33: birth/death records, missing persons and nationality governed by special laws.
- Articles 34–38: family, degrees of kinship, names and surnames.
- Articles 40–43: domicile and elected domicile.
- Articles 44–49: age of majority, capacity, minors, incapacity, guardianship/curatorship and limits on waiver of capacity/personal liberty.
- Article 50: protection of personality rights and claim to stop unlawful infringement plus compensation.
- Article 51: protection of name against impersonation or unjustified use, with claim to stop infringement and compensation.
- Articles 52–53: legal persons, separate estate, capacity, litigation rights, domicile and representation.

## 5. Contract formation, consent and invalidity — Articles 89–141

### Article 89
- Contract is formed by exchange of two matching expressions of will, subject to statutory/formal requirements.

### Articles 90–98
- Expression of will may be by words, writing, customary sign or conduct; rules govern receipt, knowledge, offers, acceptance, silence, timing and place of contract.

### Articles 99–108
- Rules for auctions, adhesion contracts, promises to contract, earnest money, agency, undisclosed agency and self-dealing by agents.

### Articles 109–119
- Capacity and effects of acts by minors, persons under interdiction and persons subject to judicial assistance.

### Articles 120–124
- Fundamental mistake and its effect on contract validity.

### Articles 125–126
- Fraud, including intentional silence about material facts; fraud by a third party only affects the contract under the stated knowledge/constructive-knowledge conditions.

### Articles 127–128
- Duress/coercion and its effect on validity.

### Articles 129–130
- Exploitation/gross imbalance and judicial power to annul or reduce obligations, subject to the statutory time limit and the other party's option to cure the imbalance.

### Articles 131–141
- Subject matter, cause, invalidity, annulment, ratification and limitation of annulment/nullity actions.
- Raw extraction must be visually checked; do not ingest without article boundaries and amendment metadata.

## 6. Contract effects, hardship, good faith and termination — Articles 147–161

### Article 147
- Contract has the force of law between the parties and cannot be revoked/modified unilaterally except by agreement or statutory authority.
- Its second paragraph addresses exceptional general events that were unforeseeable and make performance excessively onerous (without making it impossible), threatening the debtor with grave loss; the judge may reduce the onerous obligation to a reasonable limit after balancing the parties' interests, and any contrary agreement is void.
- This is the statutory foundation commonly associated with exceptional circumstances/hardship; apply only after checking exact conditions and subsequent case law.

### Article 148
> يجب تنفيذ العقد طبقًا لما اشتمل عليه وبطريقة تتفق مع ما يوجبه حسن النية.
- Contractual performance includes not only express terms but also necessary incidents under law, custom, equity and the nature of the obligation.

### Article 149
- In an adhesion contract with arbitrary terms, the judge may modify the terms or exempt the adhering party, and any agreement to the contrary is void.

### Article 150
- Clear contract wording is not to be diverted through interpretation; where interpretation is needed, the common intention is sought in light of transaction nature, good faith and custom.

### Article 151
- Doubt is interpreted in the debtor's interest; in adhesion contracts, ambiguity cannot be interpreted against the adhering party.

### Articles 157–161
- In reciprocal contracts, non-performance after notice may allow the other party to demand performance or rescission with compensation where appropriate.
- Judicial rescission, express rescission clauses, impossibility of performance and restoration of parties to their pre-contract position are addressed.

## 7. Tort and non-contractual liability — Articles 163–180

### Article 163
> كل خطأ سبب ضررًا للغير يلزم من ارتكبه بالتعويض.

### Article 164
- A person with discernment is liable for unlawful acts; special rule for damage caused by a non-discerning person where no responsible supervisor exists or compensation cannot be obtained.

### Article 165
- A person is not liable if they prove damage resulted from an external cause beyond their control, such as force majeure, unforeseeable accident, victim's fault or third-party fault, unless statute/agreement provides otherwise.

### Articles 166–172
- Legitimate defence, public employee compliance with superior order under conditions, necessity, multiple tortfeasors, assessment/form of compensation and limitation of tort claims.

### Articles 173–178
- Liability for persons under supervision, employers for employees acting in/for their work, animals, building collapse and dangerous things/mechanical machines.

### Articles 179–180
- Unjust enrichment and its limitation periods.

## 8. Restitution, payment not due and limitation — Articles 181–202 and 371–379

- Articles 181–186: recovery of payment not due, good/bad faith and benefits/profits.
- Article 203 onward: specific performance and performance in kind.
- Articles 219–220 (later pages): notice/default and delay damages; exact text to be extracted.
- Articles 265–270: conditions and terms.
- Articles 282–296: joint and several creditors/debtors.
- Articles 342–351: payment and performance in lieu.
- Articles 352 onward: novation, assignment and related discharge.
- Article 371: release/forgiveness of debt.
- Article 373: impossibility due to foreign cause beyond debtor's control.
- Article 374: general limitation period of 15 years except special statutory periods.
- Article 375: five-year limitation for recurring periodic rights such as rent, interest, salaries and pensions.
- Article 376: five-year limitation for specified professional fees (doctors, lawyers, engineers, pharmacists, teachers, brokers and others).
- Article 377: three-year limitation for State taxes/fees and claims to recover unduly paid taxes/fees, subject to special laws.
- Article 378: one-year limitation for listed traders/craftsmen, hotel/restaurant claims, workers' wages and similar items, with oath rules.
- Special limitation statutes and later tax legislation may override these general provisions.

## 9. Sale, donation, partnership and lease — selected later provisions

### Articles 447–453 — defects/warranty in sale
- Seller's warranty covers defects reducing value or intended use, subject to buyer's knowledge/inspection and seller's concealment/fraud.
- Buyer must inspect and notify seller within a reasonable period; the warranty action generally prescribes one year from delivery, subject to fraud and agreement rules.

### Articles 477–481 — sale in illness/death and self-dealing
- Rules govern sales made during terminal illness and self-dealing by agents/representatives, brokers and experts.

### Articles 482–485 — exchange
- Exchange is a contract where each party transfers ownership of property other than money to the other; sale rules apply as compatible.

### Articles 486–495 — donation
- Donation is a gratuitous disposition; formal writing is generally required, subject to the movable-property delivery exception.
- Rules cover acceptance, future property, delivery, title and defects.

### Articles 514–517 — partnership
- Profit/loss sharing, prohibition on exempting a partner from all profits/losses (with limited work-only contribution exception), and management rules.

### Articles 558–563 — lease formation and duration
- Article 558 defines lease as granting use of a specified thing for a specified period in return for rent.
- Article 559 limits a person with management authority but no ownership to leases not exceeding three years unless authorized by competent authority.
- Articles 562–563 govern rent determination and leases with no fixed term.

### Articles 564–591 — lease obligations
- Lessor must deliver the property fit for agreed use; repair/defect, quiet enjoyment and maintenance duties are addressed.
- Lessee must use the property as agreed, preserve it, notify the lessor of urgent repairs/third-party interference, pay rent and return the property at lease end.
- Article 590 requires return at end of lease; continued retention without right may entail compensation.
- These are general Civil Code lease provisions; special rent laws (49/1977, 136/1981, 4/1996, 164/2025, 165/2025 and later instruments) may displace/modify them for covered tenancies.
