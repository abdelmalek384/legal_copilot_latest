## 2. Article 1 — export of hazardous materials

### A. Controls

1. Existing lists of hazardous materials issued by competent ministries remain in use, while observing the unified lists of hazardous materials and wastes to be issued by the technical committee formed under Article 53 of Law 202/2020.
2. The exporter must comply with paragraphs 1, 2 and 3 of Article 40 of the Executive Regulation of Import and Export Law 118/1975, issued by Ministerial Decision 770/2005.
3. Transport must be performed by a licensed hazardous-material transport company.
4. The export approval is valid for one year only.

**Important transition wording:** the decision does not say that the historical ministry lists are immediately discarded. It requires use of current ministry lists while observing the unified lists when issued. Do not treat this provision as proof of the contents or commencement of the unified list later adopted under Decision 151/2025.

### B. Application route

The application is submitted through WMRA's electronic licensing and permits website / National Waste Information and Data Management System.

#### Where the exporter is the manufacturing / producing company

The Gazette text lists:
- environmental approval (EIA) for the facility;
- licences issued by competent administrative authorities for the factory / exporting company;
- tax card;
- industrial register showing the hazardous materials intended for export, and commercial register;
- invoice or pro-forma invoice for the hazardous materials;
- award/order or contract between exporter and importer identifying quantity and contract validity dates, remaining valid during export;
- where the exporter is not itself the carrier: contract between exporter and transport company showing validity dates and remaining valid during export, copy of the carrier's competent-authority transport licence, and WMRA approval for the carrier's hazardous-material collection/transport activity.

#### Where export is conducted through an import/export company

The text lists:
- tax card;
- commercial register;
- contract between the import/export company and the factory / producing company, showing validity dates and remaining valid during export; the import/export company acts as agent for the producer;
- the producer/factory information required in the preceding producer-company section.

## 3. Article 2 — export of hazardous waste

### A. Substantive controls

The decision prohibits export of hazardous waste unless the three stated conditions are satisfied:

1. The destination/exporting country is described in the Gazette wording as lacking the technical capacity, necessary facilities, means or suitable sites for environmentally sound disposal, and the relevant recycling-factory need condition is met as stated in the text. Because the extracted Arabic syntax in this clause is awkward and may be affected by OCR/column ordering, do not paraphrase this limb into an operational rule without checking the page image and obtaining legal review.
2. The waste is requested as a raw material for recycling industries or import in the importing country.
3. The transboundary movement is carried out in accordance with other criteria that may be adopted by the Parties, provided they do not conflict with the objectives of the Basel Convention.

Additional controls:
- Existing hazardous-waste lists issued by competent ministries remain in use, while observing the unified lists to be issued by the Article 53 technical committee.
- Basel Convention rules and conditions governing transboundary hazardous-waste exports apply.
- Export procedures must not violate rules governing export of the particular commodity issued by the Minister of Trade and Industry.
- Transport must be through a licensed transport company.
- Export approval is valid for one year only.

### B. Application route and documents

The application is submitted through WMRA's electronic licensing and permits website / National Waste Information and Data Management System.

#### Where exporter is the factory / company generating the hazardous waste

The Gazette text lists:
- copy of the facility's environmental approval (EIA);
- copies of licences issued by competent administrative authorities;
- tax card;
- industrial register and commercial register;
- invoice or pro-forma invoice for the hazardous waste intended for export;
- where exporter is not itself the carrier: contract with the carrier showing validity dates, carrier's competent-authority licence and WMRA approval for hazardous-waste transport activity;
- completed Notification and Movement Documents for the shipment (Basel Convention Annex V A and V B), with the Gazette text referring applicants to the Basel Convention website;
- list of hazardous-waste generators where more than one generator is involved;
- shipping line and transit ports;
- contract between exporter and importer identifying the intended handling method (final disposal, recycling, reuse, recovery of primary components, etc.), quantity and validity dates, remaining valid during export;
- insurance and financial guarantees covering the transboundary shipment period, including rejection/return to exporter or transport accident scenarios, to cover potential environmental damage and resulting compensation;
- copy of the licence issued to the recycling or disposal facility in the importing country, certified by the relevant authorities and confirming environmentally sound management (ESM);
- description of the technology used for recycling or disposal;
- certificates of disposal or recycling for the exported hazardous-waste shipments;
- signed and stamped declaration by the applicant that all submitted data and documents are correct.

#### Where export is conducted through an import/export company

The Gazette text lists:
- tax card;
- commercial register;
- contract between the import/export company and the factory / company generating the hazardous waste, showing validity dates and remaining valid during export; the import/export company acts as agent for the producer;
- where the export company is not itself the carrier: contract with the carrier showing validity dates and remaining valid during export, plus the carrier's competent-authority licence and WMRA approval for hazardous-material collection/transport activity;
- information about the factory / company generating the hazardous waste as required in the preceding section.

## 4. Article 3 — publication and commencement

The decision is published in Egyptian Proceedings and takes effect on the day following publication.

Publication was 11 June 2025; the stated commencement rule therefore points to 12 June 2025.

## 5. Relationship to the WMRA fee and unified-list instruments

- **Decision 113/2022:** baseline WMRA fee schedule, cited in Decision 121/2025's preamble.
- **Decision 120/2025:** separately inserts Article 3 bis into Decision 113/2022, including a 1% export-approval fee capped at EGP 200,000 and a Basel notification / transit-extension fee of EGP 12,000 per notification for one year. Decision 121/2025 establishes substantive export controls; Decision 120/2025 establishes fee categories. They must be retrieved together for a fee-plus-conditions question.
- **Decision 150/2025:** later facility-level hazardous-material/waste and used-cooking-oil fees; current corpus capture is provisional pending official PDF page matching.
- **Decision 151/2025:** later adopts a unified list under Article 53; the annex is not yet captured. The use-current-ministry-lists / observe-unified-lists language in Decision 121/2025 is a transitional cross-reference, not the list itself.
- **Decision 346/2025:** later approves technical committee decisions, but its Gazette item is restricted and the operative content is not captured.

## 6. Retrieval safeguards

- Distinguish hazardous materials from hazardous waste; Article 1 and Article 2 have different conditions and document sets.
- Distinguish producer/manufacturer exporters from import/export intermediaries.
- Enforce one-year approval validity for both categories.
- For hazardous waste, retrieve Basel Notification and Movement documents, insurance/financial guarantees, destination facility licence/ESM, technology description and disposal/recycling certificates; do not reuse the shorter hazardous-material checklist.
- Do not claim that the export is automatically permitted merely because a current ministry list identifies the substance; all substantive, Basel, carrier, licensing and document requirements still apply.
- Pair this decision with Decision 120/2025 for fee questions, but do not merge their legal subject matter.
- For the Article 2 three-condition test, preserve the exact Arabic and require page-image/OCR verification and lawyer review before operationalizing the first limb.
