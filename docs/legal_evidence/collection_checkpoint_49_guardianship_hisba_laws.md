# Collection checkpoint 49 — Guardianship over Property Law 119/1952 and Hisba Law 3/1996

## 1. Law 119/1952 — guardianship over property
- Law No. 119/1952 on guardianship over property; Gazette issue 118, 30 July 1952.
- Full 91-article Arabic transcription, stated updated through 2026:
  https://alhasswalawfirm.com/law-library/law-119-1952
- Secondary full text:
  https://lawhub.info/eg/?p=11605
- Official/secondary record:
  https://manshurat.org/node/39556
- Alternate full text PDF:
  https://cdn-cms.f-static.com/uploads/756335/normal_5a0893ddde32b.pdf
- Article 1: father, then paternal grandfather where father has not appointed a guardian, exercises guardianship over minor's property, subject to law; guardian cannot resign without court permission.
- Article 2: guardian may exercise a right only if personally possessing the capacity required for that right.
- Article 3: donated property is excluded from guardianship where donor expressly so provides.
- Article 4: guardian manages minor's property and may dispose of it subject to statutory controls.
- Article 5: guardian may not donate minor's property except for humanitarian/family duty and with court permission.
- Article 6: guardian may not dispose of minor's real estate to himself, spouse or relatives to fourth degree without court permission; cannot mortgage minor's property for own debt.
- Article 7: father may not dispose of real estate, commercial store or securities above statutory historical value threshold without court permission; court may refuse where transaction risks minor's property or involves excessive undervalue.
- Full law regulates minors, guardianship, trusteeship, guardians appointed by will/court, incapacitated persons, missing/absent persons, court permission, estate/property management, inventory, accounting and penalties.
- Historical monetary thresholds are likely amended/obsolete; never use them without checking all later amendments, court decisions and current currency/valuation rules.

## 2. Law 3/1996 — procedure for Hisba claims in personal-status matters
- Gazette issue 4 bis, 29 January 1996; effective 30 January 1996.
- Official full text hosted by MKSEgypt:
  https://mksegypt.org/ar/laws/23913
- PDF copy:
  https://www.elsayyad.net/UploadFiles/LegislationDetails/1996-003-بشأن%20تنظيم%20إجراءات%20مباشرة%20دعوى%20الحسبة%20فى%20مسائل%20الأحوال%20الشخصية.pdf
- Full 8-article text:
  https://lawhub.info/eg/?p=11412
- Law makes Public Prosecution the sole party authorized to file Hisba claims in personal-status matters; complainant submits report and supporting documents.
- Public Prosecution investigates and decides whether to file before competent First Instance Court or archive; a General Attorney must give reasoned decision and notify interested persons within three days.
- Attorney General may cancel filing/archive decision within 30 days and make final disposition.
- If filed, Public Prosecution is plaintiff and has plaintiff rights/duties; complainant cannot intervene or appeal judgment.
- Court must hear case at first hearing with Public Prosecution representative even if defendant absent.
- Courts transfer pending Hisba claims without fees to Public Prosecution where no judgment has been issued.
- A legal-library review states it visually compared both pages of the Gazette scan and found no later amending law through 14 October 2026; verify independently before promotion.

## 3. Cross-law relationships
- Law 119/1952 connects to Personal Status Laws 25/1920, 25/1929, Inheritance Law 77/1943, Wills Law 71/1946, Child Law 12/1996, Personal Status Procedures Law 1/2000 and Family Courts Law 10/2004.
- Law 3/1996 is procedural and should be linked to Personal Status Procedures Law 1/2000 and Family Courts Law 10/2004.
- Distinguish guardianship over property (ولاية على المال) from guardianship over person (ولاية على النفس), custody (حضانة), trusteeship (وصاية) and curatorship/assistance; these have different legal bases and competencies.
