- This is a future-effective law; before 1 October 2027, Law 150/1950 as amended remains the general operative Criminal Procedure Law.

## Promulgation and transitional provisions
- Article 1: Code applies to criminal procedure without prejudice to procedural provisions in other laws.
- Article 2: appeals in absentia misdemeanour judgments issued before commencement continue under the former procedures.
- Article 3: felony appeal rules apply only to cases not decided by felony courts as of the commencement of Law 1/2024.
- Article 4: repeals Law 150/1950 and Law 140/2014 on extradition/transfer of sentenced persons when the new Code commences, plus inconsistent provisions.
- Article 5: Minister of Justice issues implementation decisions; earlier decisions continue insofar as they do not conflict.
- Article 6 as amended by Law 160/2026: commencement 1 October 2027.

## Actual Arabic text — selected provisions

### Article 3 — complaint and 90-day period
> لا يجوز رفع الدعوى الجنائية أو اتخاذ أي إجراء من إجراءات التحقيق فيها إلا بناء على شكوى شفهية أو كتابية من المجني عليه أو من وكيله الخاص، إلى النيابة العامة أو إلى أحد مأموري الضبط القضائي في الجرائم المنصوص عليها في المواد ١٨٥، ٢٧٤، ٢٧٧، ٢٧٩، ٢٩٢، ٢٩٣، ٣٠٣، ٣٠٦، ٣٠٧، ٣٠٨ من قانون العقوبات، وكذلك في الأحوال الأخرى التي ينص عليها القانون.
>
> ولا تقبل الشكوى بعد تسعين يومًا من يوم علم المجني عليه بالجريمة وبمرتكبها ما لم ينص القانون على خلاف ذلك.

### Article 11 — complaint exception for offences against public officials
- Investigation ordinarily cannot begin until the required complaint/request/permission is obtained.
- An exception allows investigation without complaint for Penal Code Articles 185, 303, 306, 307 and 308 where the victim is a public employee/representative/person entrusted with public service and the offence is connected to the function.

### Article 17 — limitation of criminal proceedings
- General limitation: 10 years for felonies, 3 years for misdemeanours and 1 year for infractions, unless otherwise provided.
- Listed serious offences are excluded; for specified public-official offences, time starts after end of service/loss of capacity unless investigation began earlier.

### Articles 24–32 — judicial police
- Article 24: judicial police officers search for offences/offenders and gather evidence for investigation/prosecution.
- Article 25: judicial police officers are subordinate to the Public Prosecutor and subject to prosecutorial supervision.
- Article 26 lists judicial police officers by territorial and national competence; the Minister of Justice may grant judicial-police status to public officials for offences connected to their functions.
- Articles 27–32 regulate complaints, reports, records, preservation of evidence, identity data, officer identification, reporting obligations and preliminary questioning.

### Article 37 — arrest, detention and rights
> فيما عدا حالة التلبس، لا يجوز القبض على أحد، أو تفتيشه، أو حبسه، أو تقييد حريته بأي قيد إلا بأمر قضائي مسبب يستلزمه التحقيق.
>
> وكل من يُقبض عليه أو يُحبس أو تُقيد حريته، تجب معاملته بما يحفظ عليه كرامته، ولا يجوز تعذيبه ولا ترهيبه ولا إكراهه ولا إيذاؤه بدنيًا أو معنويًا.
>
> وللمتهم حق الصمت، وكل قول يثبت أنه صدر من محتجز تحت وطأة شيء مما تقدم، أو التهديد بشيء منه، يُهدر ولا يعول عليه.

### Article 41 — notification of rights and 24-hour transfer
- Requires immediate notice of reasons for restriction of liberty and the accusation, written notification of rights, and ability to contact family and counsel.
- If the accusation is not rebutted, transfer to the competent investigative authority within 24 hours.

### Article 47 — home search
- Home searches are investigative acts and require a reasoned order by a Public Prosecution member based on an accusation against a person residing there of committing/participating in a felony or misdemeanour.

### Article 72 — notification details
- Parties designate an elected domicile or mobile number/email for service. Where details are missing, incomplete, incorrect or not updated, service at the court registry may be valid under the statutory conditions.

### Article 74 — copies of investigation papers
- Accused, victim, civil claimant/responsible party and their lawyers may obtain copies at their own expense during investigation unless investigative interests require otherwise; further access follows the article's conditions after investigation.

### Article 80 — communications and social-media surveillance
- With permission of the competent partial judge, a Public Prosecution member may order seizure of letters/messages/printed matter/parcels and monitoring of communications, social-media accounts and non-public content, email, text/audio/video messages and related media, or recording private-place conversations, where useful in a felony or misdemeanour punishable by more than three months.
- This is future-effective. Do not cite it as the current legal basis before 1 October 2027.

### Articles 107–116 — orders and alternatives to pretrial detention
- Articles 107–108 regulate attendance, arrest and bring-in orders and required identifying/accusation data.
- Article 111: orders are enforceable throughout Egypt; arrest/bring-in orders expire after six months unless extended.
- Article 112: immediate interrogation after arrest; if impossible, detention pending interrogation may not exceed 24 hours before transfer to the Public Prosecution.
- Article 114 alternatives to detention include home confinement, reporting to police, avoiding places, geographic restrictions, no-contact orders, temporary firearms restrictions and electronic tracking where available under a Minister of Justice decision.
- Article 115 permits replacement of a breached measure with pretrial detention.
- Article 116 requires a reasoned detention order with offence, penalty, reasons and detention-facility instructions.

### Articles 123–128 — pretrial detention limits and release
- Article 124: misdemeanour detention/measure generally may not exceed three months without timely referral; felony detention generally may not exceed five months without court extension, with further extensions up to 45 days each.
- Overall pretrial detention may not exceed one-third of the maximum custodial sentence, subject to ceilings of 4 months for misdemeanours, 12 months for felonies and 18 months for life/death offences.
- For life/death cases, appellate felony/Cassation courts may order 45-day renewable detention up to two years.
- Articles 125–128 govern provisional release, bail and payment methods. Verify exact conditions before deadline calculations.

### Articles 268–270 — public hearings
- Article 268 requires public hearings, subject to closed-session exceptions for public order/morals; broadcasting/transmission requires written permission of the presiding judge after hearing the Public Prosecution.
- Article 269 requires Public Prosecution attendance at criminal court sessions.
- Article 270 provides that the accused attends without restraints/chains, subject to necessary observation.

### Articles 333–339 — nullity
- Article 333: breach of a material procedural requirement results in nullity.
- Article 334: nullity relating to court formation/jurisdiction, personal liberty, home inviolability, privacy or public order may be raised at any stage and considered by the court on its own motion.
- Articles 335–339 govern waiver, correction and consequences of procedural nullity.

### Article 340 — mental/psychological disorder assessment
- Provides referral and observation where investigation requires assessment of mental/psychological disorder and its effect on perception/choice, including a 45-day aggregate observation period under the statutory conditions and a tripartite psychiatric committee report.

### Articles 405–413 — felony appeals
- Govern appeal of felony judgments, 40-day filing period, 60-day Attorney General appeal period, hearing, the accused speaking last, death-penalty handling and execution-stay rules.
- Reconcile with Law 1/2024 and the transitional rules.

### Articles 484–493 — limitation of sentence and rehabilitation
- Article 484: execution limitation periods of 20 years for felonies, 30 years for death, 5 years for misdemeanours and 2 years for infractions.
- Articles 485–489 govern commencement, interruption/suspension and death of the sentenced person.
- Articles 490–493 govern rehabilitation, waiting periods and payment of fines/restitution/compensation/costs, subject to statutory exceptions.

## Other Code areas
