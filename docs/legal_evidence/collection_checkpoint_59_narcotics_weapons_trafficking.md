### Core provisions and structure
- Regulates controlled narcotic substances, their use, possession, import/export, manufacture, prescription, dispensing and trade.
- Article 1 incorporates Schedule 1 narcotic substances and excludes specified preparations in Schedule 2.
- Article 1 bis (added by Law 134/2019) treats synthetic substances listed in Schedule 1 as narcotic substances; competent Minister sets criteria/controls.
- Chapters regulate cultivation, production, import/export, medical use, pharmacies, licensed establishments, possession, trafficking, criminal offences, confiscation and schedules.
- The statutory schedules are central operative legal content; each substance/mixture, concentration, preparation and effective date must be captured as structured schedule data, not inferred from narrative summaries.

## 2. Weapons and Ammunition Law 394/1954

- Official/secondary full text:
  https://www.laweg.net/framePlain.aspx?ItemID=74&NID=42589&Type=14&action=ViewActivePages
  https://lawhub.info/eg/?p=11748
  https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/قانون_الأسلحة_والذخائر_(eg)
- Gazette issue 53 bis (extraordinary), 8 July 1954.
- Full text and schedules require current versioning; PM Decision 1610/2015 is cited as an amendment to schedules in some consolidations.
- Article 1 requires licence for specified firearms and bladed weapons and prohibits licensing certain listed suppressors/optical devices; Minister of Interior may amend schedules subject to statutory constraints.
- Article 1 bis added by Law 5/2019 addresses air/gas pressure weapons and their ammunition under licensing/conditions set by Interior Minister.
- Law 163/2022 (Gazette issue 44 supplement A, 3 November 2022) amends Articles 4, 8, 10, 12, 26 and 30, including fees for storage of surrendered/revoked weapons and administrative enforcement.
- Official Gazette PDF of Law 163/2022:
  https://gate.ahram.org.eg/media/News/2022/11/6/2022-638033396014725011-472.pdf
- Additional amendment leads include Laws 34/1974, 101/1980, 165/1981 and 5/2019; verify all Gazette texts and schedule decisions.
- Keep statutory licensing/schedule rules separate from ministerial licensing procedures, administrative fees and criminal penalties.

## 3. Human Trafficking Law 64/2010 and Executive Regulation 3028/2010

### Sources
- Official bilingual law PDF hosted by UNODC:
  https://sherloc.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/document/egy/law-no--64-of-2010-regarding-combating-human-trafficking_html/Law_64-2010_Eng_and_Ara.pdf
- Official government human-rights studies PDF:
  https://hrightsstudies.sis.gov.eg/media/2118/1170249.pdf
- Full Arabic text:
  https://lawhub.info/eg/?p=11534
- Executive Regulation PM Decision 3028/2010 full 28-article transcription:
  https://alhasswalawfirm.com/law-library/decree-3028-2010
- Gazette Law 64/2010: issue 18 bis, 9 May 2010; effective 10 May 2010.
- Executive Regulation: Gazette issue 47 bis, 30 November 2010; effective 1 December 2010.

### Core provisions
- Article 1 defines organized criminal group (three or more persons), transnational crime, victim and related concepts.
- Article 2 criminalizes dealing in a natural person (sale, offer, purchase, use, transport, delivery, harbouring, receipt) by force/threat, kidnapping, fraud/deception, abuse of authority, exploitation of vulnerability/need or payment/benefit for control, for exploitation purposes.
- Article 3: victim consent is irrelevant where prohibited means are used; for child/incapacitated victim no prohibited means need be proven and consent is irrelevant.
- Articles 5–6 prescribe basic and aggravated penalties; aggravating circumstances include organized criminal group, transnational nature, serious threats/torture/weapons, family/guardian/control relationship and child/vulnerable victim.
- Article 11 provides liability of actual manager and legal person where offence committed by employee in its name/interest, subject to knowledge/dereliction conditions.
- Article 13 confiscation of proceeds/instruments, subject to bona fide third-party rights.
- Article 14 treats trafficking offences as predicate offences under AML Law 80/2002.
- Articles 21–23 protect victims, including non-liability for directly related offences, healthcare/psychological/social support, identity/privacy, legal assistance and witness protection.
- Articles 27–28 establish victim-support fund and National Committee to Combat Human Trafficking.
- Article 29 requires Executive Regulation; Decision 3028/2010 provides detailed protection/referral/assistance mechanisms.
