## 1. Scope and structure
- Penal Code defines substantive offences and punishments, separate from Criminal Procedure Law 174/2025.
- Main structure:
  - Book One: general provisions, territorial/extraterritorial application, categories of offences, criminal intent/participation, penalties and ancillary consequences.
  - Book Two: felonies and misdemeanours affecting State security, public authority, public administration, justice, public order, persons, property, public trust, money and other protected interests.
  - Book Three: contraventions and minor offences.
- Article 1: applies to anyone committing an offence under the Code within Egypt.
- Article 2: extraterritorial application to persons whose conduct abroad makes them principal/accomplice in an offence occurring wholly/partly in Egypt and specified offences against State security, forgery and currency.
- Article 3: Egyptian citizen committing a felony/misdemeanour abroad may be punished on return if conduct is also criminal where committed, subject to conditions.
- Article 4: prosecution for foreign offences is brought only by Public Prosecution; no new prosecution where acquitted abroad or final sentence served, subject to statutory rules.
- Article 5: offence punished under law in force when committed, except later more lenient law before final judgment; if later law decriminalizes act after final judgment, sentence execution and criminal effects cease.

## 2. Core criminal-law concepts
- Classifies offences as felonies, misdemeanours and contraventions according to statutory punishment.
- Regulates principal/attempt/accomplice liability, criminal intent, mistake, necessity, self-defence, insanity, minority, intoxication and mitigating/aggravating circumstances.
- Provides principal and accessory penalties, imprisonment, fines, confiscation, deprivation of rights, surveillance, closure, publication and other consequences.
- Each offence's elements, penalty range, aggravating factors, limitation and competent court must be parsed separately; no broad category summary can substitute for exact offence text.

## 3. Selected provisions from full text
- Articles 1–5: territorial scope, extraterritorial scope, nationality-based jurisdiction, prosecution and lex mitior.
- Articles 10–12: felony/misdemeanour/contravention classification by penalty.
- Articles 13–23: principal penalties including death, life imprisonment, aggravated/simple imprisonment, custody and fines; historical fine floors/ceilings may be altered by later special laws and inflation/monetary amendments.
- The Code includes extensive offences relating to:
  - State security, treason, terrorism and public order.
  - Bribery, embezzlement, abuse of public office and public funds.
  - Forgery, counterfeit currency, false statements and perjury.
  - Assault, homicide, bodily injury, threats, sexual offences and family-related offences.
  - Theft, fraud, breach of trust, extortion, damage and property offences.
  - Public morals, unlawful gambling, drugs and other special offences, some of which have been moved/overridden by separate statutes.

## 4. Amendment and special-law relationships
- Law 21/2018 is shown by WIPO Lex as an amendment to the Code.
- Law 71/2021 appears in one 2021 consolidated PDF.
- Law 141/2021 is identified by Manshurat as the latest amendment dated 15 August 2021.
- Special criminalization laws may supplement or override Code provisions, including:
  - Anti-Money Laundering Law 80/2002.
  - Cybercrime Law 175/2018 and Executive Regulation 635/2020.
  - Drug control statutes.
  - Arms/munitions statutes.
  - Human trafficking, organ transplantation and terrorism statutes.
  - Consumer, tax, customs, labour, environment, public procurement and financial-regulation penalty provisions.
- Keep special-law offences in their own statute records and cross-link the Penal Code only where explicitly applicable.

## 5. Relationship with Criminal Procedure Law 174/2025
- Penal Code Law 58/1937 defines crimes and penalties; Criminal Procedure Law 174/2025 governs investigation, prosecution, trial, appeal, execution and procedural safeguards.
- Criminal Procedure Law 174/2025 repeals former Criminal Procedure Law 150/1950, not Penal Code 58/1937.
- Do not merge substantive and procedural criminal law into one document.
