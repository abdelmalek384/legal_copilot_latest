## 1. Capital adequacy — capital tiers (Gazette pp. 8–14)

The current standard requires a capital adequacy ratio of at least 12% at all times. The official text sets out:

| Capital element | Minimum / maximum ratio |
|---|---:|
| Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1), after deductions | at least 6% |
| Capital conservation buffer | 2.5% |
| CET1 including conservation buffer | 8.5% |
| Additional Tier 1 (AT1) | not more than 1.5% |
| Tier 1 including conservation buffer | 10% |
| Tier 2 | not more than 2% |
| Total capital base including conservation buffer | at least 12% |
| Countercyclical capital buffer | 0%–2.5% |

**Arabic wording:**
- «يجب ألا يقل معيار كفاية رأس المال للشركة في كل وقت عن 12% من إجمالي الأصول المرجحة بأوزان المخاطر.»
- «رأس المال الأساسي المستمر (CET1) ... على الأقل 6%.»
- «الدعامة التحوطية (Capital Conservation Buffer) ... 2.5%.»
- «رأس المال الإضافي (AT1) يجب ألا يتجاوز 1.5%.»
- «رأس المال المساند (Tier 2) لا يتجاوز 2%.»
- «القاعدة الرأسمالية بعد إضافة الدعامة التحوطية على الأقل 12%.»

CET1 includes paid-up capital, retained earnings after proposed distributions, and reserves (legal, general, statutory and capital reserves). Deductions include treasury shares, future securitization profit margin, negative available-for-sale valuation reserve, negative foreign-currency translation reserve, goodwill (fully deducted), other intangibles (deducted over five years at 20% increments from January 2026 through January 2030), and certain financial-institution/subsidiary/affiliate investments. Deferred tax assets are compared with 10% of CET1; amounts above thresholds are deducted and risk-weighted at 150%.

Tier 2 includes general provisions on regular financing balances and subordinated loans. Subordinated loans must:
- have an original term of at least five years and be amortized in equal annual amounts;
- have at least 12 months remaining to maturity;
- be fully paid in cash;
- not be earmarked for a specific activity or asset;
- be unsecured and not senior to other creditors;
- not be repaid if repayment would reduce the capital base below the required solvency ratio.
Total subordinated loans must not exceed 50% of Tier 1 capital.

The countercyclical buffer is between 0% and 2.5%; FRA may determine it based on two of three indicators relating to economy-wide credit cycles, the company's portfolio growth and the activity's market growth.

## 2. Credit-risk weights (Gazette pp. 15–17)

The official table applies across four non-bank financing activity groups: mortgage finance; financial leasing/factoring; consumer finance; and MSME/microfinance.

| Asset / exposure | Risk weight |
|---|---:|
| Cash and cash equivalents | 0% |
| Treasury bills and government bonds | 0% |
| Bank deposits in EGP or foreign currency | 0% |
| Money-market fund certificates | 0% |
| Regular finance portfolio / leased assets / regular financial receivables | 100% |
| Consumer prepayment finance, regular and supported by evidence of spending for the allocated purpose | 100% |
| Consumer prepayment finance, regular but without evidence of spending for the allocated purpose | 150% |
| Net consumer prepayment finance overdue more than 30 days (after required provisions) | 200% |
| Receivables balances / customer balances | 150% |
| Net non-performing financing after provisions (activity-specific delinquency trigger) | 150% |
| Shares and corporate bonds | 100% |
| Investments in affiliates/subsidiaries | 100% |
| Intangible assets | 100% |
| Net fixed assets | 100% |
| Deferred tax assets | 150% |
| Other assets | 100% |

For non-performing exposures, the table distinguishes the overdue trigger by activity: microfinance and nano-finance (more than 7 days), MSME and consumer finance (more than 30 days), factoring (more than 60 days), and mortgage finance/financial leasing (more than 90 days). The 150% weight applies after deducting the required provisions.

## 3. Operational-risk capital and market risk (Gazette pp. 18–22)

The standard calculates operational-risk capital using the Basel III Business Indicator Component (BIC), internal loss multiplier (ILM) and supervisory marginal coefficient (αi):

«المتطلبات الرأسمالية لمقابلة مخاطر التشغيل (ORC) = مكون مؤشر الأعمال المرجح (BIC) × مضاعف الخسائر الداخلية (ILM).»

«الأصول المرجحة بأوزان المخاطر لمقابلة مخاطر التشغيل = المتطلبات الرأسمالية لمقابلة مخاطر التشغيل (ORC) × 12.5.»

The Business Indicator comprises:
1. interest, rent/lease and dividend component;
2. services component;
3. financial component.

The supervisory marginal coefficient varies by business-indicator band: 12% for the lower third, 15% for the middle third and 18% for the upper third. The Business Indicator is based on the average of the last three years. The loss component equals 15 times average annual operational losses over the preceding five years. Companies without a five-year internal-loss database receive 12 months to prepare it; until available, ILM is treated as 1.

Market-risk capital is measured by Value at Risk (VaR) or Expected Shortfall (ES) for the investment portfolio at a defined confidence level and time horizon; the standard describes three calculation methods, including a 95% confidence level and a volatility-based VaR formula.

## 4. Leverage and liquidity (Gazette pp. 23–27)

**Leverage:**
«يجب ألا تتجاوز إجمالي القروض والتمويلات — عدا القروض المساندة — التي تحصل عليها الشركة تسعة أمثال القاعدة الرأسمالية لها بعد استبعاد أرصدة القروض والتمويلات التي لا يتم تحمل مخاطرها.»

**Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR):**
«يجب ألا تقل نسبة تغطية السيولة (LCR) عن 100% في جميع الأحوال.»
Formula: high-quality liquid assets divided by net expected cash outflows over 30 days. High-quality liquid assets include cash, bank deposits, Treasury bills/bonds and money-market fund certificates. Net cash outflows equal expected outflows less the lesser of expected inflows or 90% of expected outflows.

**Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR):**
«نسبة صافي التمويل المستقر (NSR) = قيمة مصادر التمويل المستقر المتاح (مرجحة بآجال الاستحقاق) ÷ قيمة مصادر التمويل المطلوب (مرجحة بآجال الاستحقاق) ≥ 100%.»

The standard gives maturity weights for liabilities and assets: liabilities with more than one year remaining 100%, 6–12 months 75%, less than 6 months 50%; cash/bank deposits/government securities with less than six months 0%; finance portfolio balances by maturity 50%, 75% or 100%; other securities and investments 100%.

## 5. Doubtful-debt provisions — activity-specific tables (Gazette pp. 27–38)

### 5.1 Mortgage finance

- General provision: 1% on regular balances up to 90 days.
- Delinquency provisions (uncovered balance): 90–180 days: 10%; 180–275: 25%; 275–365: 50%; over 365: 100%. Interest is suspended from the second bucket onward.
- Restructured balances: 10% at restructuring; 40% for >90–180 days; 80% for >180–275 days; 100% thereafter.
- Collateral coverage recognized after valuation by FRA-registered appraiser: real estate 80% of current market value; cars/vehicles 70%; machinery/equipment/production lines 50%; intangible assets 0%.
- No yield recognized if overdue more than 180 days; for restructured finance, yield may be recognized after one year of regular payment or repayment of 25% of rescheduled debt, whichever is lower.

### 5.2 Financial leasing

- General provision: 1% on regular balances up to 90 days.
- Delinquency provisions: >90–180 days 10%; >180–275 days 25%; >275–365 days 50%; >365 days 100%; interest is suspended for the later buckets.
- Restructured balances: 10% at restructuring; >90–180 days 40%; >180–275 days 80%; >275 days 100%.
- Asset coverage ratios: real estate 80%, vehicles 70%, machinery/equipment/production lines 50%, intangible assets 0%, based on valuation by an FRA-registered appraiser.
- No yield recognized if overdue more than 180 days; restructured finance may resume yield recognition after one year regular payment or repayment of 25% of rescheduled debt, whichever is lower.

### 5.3 Factoring

- General provision: 1% on regular balances up to 60 days.
- Delinquency provisions: >60–90 days 10%; >90–120 days 25%; >120–180 days 50%; >180–275 days 70%; >275–365 days 80%; >365 days 100%.
- Restructured balances: 10% at restructuring; >60–90 days 40%; >90–180 days 80%; >180 days 100%.
- No yield recognized when payment is overdue more than 180 days; for restructured balances, yield may be recognized after one year of regular payment or repayment of 25% of rescheduled debt, whichever is lower.

### 5.4 Consumer finance

- General provision: 1% on regular balances up to 30 days.
- Delinquency provisions: >30–90 days 10%; >90–120 days 30%; >120–180 days 50%; >180 days 100%.
- Restructured balances: 10% at restructuring; >30–90 days 40%; >90–120 days 80%; >120 days 100%.
- Vehicle financing has a separate two-column provision table depending on whether the security/lien is registered; the OCR extraction shows lower/higher rates respectively: 10%/20%, 20%/30%, 50%/60%, 100%/100% across the four delinquency levels. Verify page-image column headings before implementation.
- No yield recognized if overdue more than 90 days; restructured finance may resume yield recognition after one year regular payment or repayment of 25% of rescheduled debt, whichever is lower.

### 5.5 MSME finance

- General provision: 1% on regular balances up to 30 days.
- Delinquency provisions: >30–90 days 10%; >90–120 days 30%; >120–180 days 50%; >180 days 100%.
- Restructured balances: 10% at restructuring; >30–90 days 40%; >90–120 days 80%; >120 days 100%.
- No yield recognized when overdue more than 90 days; for restructured balances, yield may be recognized after one year regular payment or repayment of 25% of rescheduled debt, whichever is lower.

### 5.6 Microfinance and nano-finance

- Microfinance (microcredit) general provision: 2% on regular balances up to 7 days; minimum provision rates by arrears: >7–30 days 10%; >30–60 days 25%; >60–90 days 50%; >90–120 days 70%; >120 days 100%.
- Nano-finance general provision: 3% on regular balances up to 7 days; minimum arrears provisions: >7–15 days 20%; >15–30 days 40%; >30–45 days 60%; >45–60 days 80%; >60 days 100%.
- Restructured microfinance balances (maximum three restructurings): 50% at restructuring; >15–30 days 80%; >30 days 100%. The source tables require image review for exact column alignment.

## 6. Concentration risk (Gazette pp. 38–41)

### Mortgage finance

- Residential-purpose finance to one customer, spouse and minor children must not exceed 15% of capital base. If exceeded, additional capital requirement: 10% for >15–25%; 20% for >25–50%.
- Non-residential finance has a separate concentration ceiling and additional-capital scale; exact threshold should be transcribed from the page image before implementation.

### Leasing and factoring

- Individual concentration: dealings with one customer and related parties must not exceed 30% of the capital base. Additional capital: 10% if >30–40%; 20% if >40–50%.
- Sector concentration is measured by sum of squared sector shares of the portfolio. Additional capital: 12% if >40–60%; 16% if >60–80%; 20% if >80%.
- Factoring export receivables arising from exports to international markets are exempt from the sector-concentration additional-capital requirement.

### Consumer finance

- Exposure to one customer may not exceed 10% of the capital base of the finance company/provider.

### MSME finance

- One customer's finance may not exceed 10% of the company's capital base.
- Finance to one sector may not exceed 25% of capital base; additional capital: 12% for >25–60%; 16% for >60–80%; 20% for >80%.

### Microfinance

- Finance to one sector may not exceed 40% of capital base; additional capital: 12% for >40–60%; 16% for >60–80%; 20% for >80%.
- Where a company holds multiple non-bank finance licenses, individual concentration is calculated using the activity with the highest concentration ratio.

## 7. Debt write-off (Gazette p.41)

«يجوز إعدام الديون وفقاً للشروط الآتية:
1- صدور قرار من مجلس إدارة الشركة بإعدام الديون.
2- تقديم مذكرة من إدارة الائتمان بالديون المراد إعدامها وأسباب الإعدام.
3- قيام الشركة بتكوين مخصص (100%) بكامل قيمة الرصيد المطلوب إعدامه.
4- تقديم تقرير من أحد مراقبي الحسابات المقيدين لدى الهيئة يفيد توافر الشروط الآتية:
(أ) أن يكون لدى الشركة حسابات منتظمة.
(ب) أن يكون الدين مرتبطاً بنشاط الشركة.
(ج) أن يكون قد سبق إدراج المبلغ المقابل للدين ضمن حسابات الشركة.
(د) أن تكون الشركة قد اتخذت إجراءات جادة لاستيفاء الدين ولم تتمكن من تحصيله.»

If the debt or part of it is later collected, the amount recovered is recognized as company income in the year of collection.

## 8. Creditworthiness, disclosure, reporting and stress tests (Gazette pp. 41–44)

**General creditworthiness requirements:**
1. A financing study assessing customer/project creditworthiness and ability to meet obligations.
2. Review all customer finance at least once each financial year.
3. Verify conditions, collateral and legal documents before disbursement.
4. Maintain a credit-information database to predict changes in customer status.
5. Review non-performing customers and prepare quarterly reports (or when FRA requests) for the board.
6. Maintain post-disbursement credit monitoring to ensure funds are used for the granted purpose and credit conditions are met.
7. Comply with Law 18/2019 on non-cash payment methods.

**MSME-specific requirements:**
- Finance is granted separately to each project; no collective financing to a group of projects.
- Early repayment fees may not exceed 5% of the amount prepaid, and the fee must be disclosed at grant.
- Foreign-currency finance may only be granted where the customer has available foreign-currency repayment sources and undertakes to use them.

**Consumer-finance digital scoring factors:** monthly income; income stability; financial solvency; I-Score history; job type; guarantor; age; education; dependants/financial burdens; housing type (owned/rented) and area; deposits/certificates pledged as security.

**Disclosure:** periodic and annual financial-statement notes must detail doubtful-finance provisions; consumer-finance companies must show separate accounts and provisions/portfolio segmented by product.

**Periodic reports:**
- Monthly solvency report at month-end: capital base, risk-weighted assets, capital adequacy, credit risk, operational-risk capital (ORC), market-risk capital (MRC), and PD/LGD assumptions/sample details.
- Leverage report.
- Short- and long-term liquidity report.
- Individual and sector concentration report.
- Reports submitted electronically to FRA within ten days of each month-end, with an annual auditor report on compliance with the solvency standards.

**Stress testing:** «على الشركة إجراء اختبار الإجهاد والتحمل المالي كل ستة أشهر»، with a report to FRA containing test results and the scenarios used.

## 9. Critical transition and interaction with legacy decisions

- Decision 137/2025's unified standards replace legacy sector-specific solvency standards only on **1 January 2027**.
- Decisions 191/2018 and 192/2018 remain the operative pre-2027 leasing/factoring solvency standards, subject to their own amendments, except that Part IV debt write-off may be applied early from 7 August 2025.
- The new standard is more granular: different delinquency buckets and provisions by activity; consumer finance has product-specific risk weights and scoring; microfinance and nano-finance have much shorter delinquency cycles; concentration caps differ by activity.
- Article 4 modifies mortgage-finance concentration in Decision 111/2015 only from the 2027 commencement date.
