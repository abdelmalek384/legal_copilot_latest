## 1. Scope and institutional structure
- Applies to Central Bank, banking sector, exchange companies, money-transfer companies, credit information/rating companies, credit guarantee companies, payment-system operators and payment service providers.
- CBE is an independent supervisory body with public legal personality, affiliated with President of Republic and technical/financial/administrative independence.
- CBE capital minimum EGP 20bn; may be increased by Board resolution through retained earnings/reserves or Treasury funding with Finance Minister approval.
- CBE functions include monetary-policy management, price/financial stability, bank licensing/supervision, payment systems, currency issuance, foreign reserves, financial inclusion, consumer protection, credit information and resolution of distressed banks.
- Law replaces prior Central Bank and Banking Sector Law 88/2003 and related inconsistent provisions.

## 2. Seven-book structure and key subject areas
- Book 1: CBE governance, Board, capital, objectives, monetary policy, currency, foreign reserves and independence.
- Book 2: banking sector regulation:
  - Bank licensing and ownership.
  - Minimum capital and branch/subsidiary rules.
  - Governance and board composition.
  - Capital adequacy, liquidity, risk management and internal controls.
  - Credit facilities, large exposures and related-party transactions.
  - Customer accounts, bank secrecy and information disclosure.
  - Corrective measures, administrative sanctions, resolution and liquidation.
- Book 3: exchange companies, money transfer, credit information/rating and credit guarantee companies.
- Book 4: payment systems, payment-system operators and payment service providers.
- Book 5: customer protection, complaints, transparency, financial education and dispute resolution.
- Book 6: offences and penalties.
- Book 7: final and transitional provisions.

## 3. Selected legal provisions
- Article 2: CBE is independent supervisory body; has legal personality and technical/financial/administrative independence.
- Article 4: minimum paid-up CBE capital EGP 20bn.
- Article 63 onward: bank licensing and ownership, subject to CBE approval and fit-and-proper requirements.
- Articles 74–89: ownership limits, bank governance, capital, liquidity, prudential rules and CBE supervisory powers.
- Article 89: required credit balances/reserves with CBE and related controls.
- Articles 140–144: corrective/supervisory measures and administrative penalties.
- Article 145: monetary penalty may not be below proceeds gained from violation and must be proportionate to seriousness/damage; CBE Board may reduce part based on remediation, excluding proceeds obtained.
- Articles 157 onward: resolution of distressed banks, transfer of assets/liabilities, stays of certain contractual termination rights and continuity of essential functions.
- Article 194: payment-system operators must allow banks/payment service providers to participate objectively and without discrimination, subject to stability; banks must allow payment-system operators/providers to open accounts.
- Article 195: CBE Board may prohibit/restrict/modify/suspend payment-system activities to protect stability or customer rights.
- Article 217: CBE Board issues customer-rights regulation requiring transparent disclosure, understandable contract terms, fair non-discriminatory treatment and responsibility for outsourced service providers.
- Article 221: competition law does not apply to licensed entities in the same way; anti-competitive practices are prohibited by this law and CBE rules.
- Articles 224–241: offences and penalties, legal-person liability, judicial-police status, prior written request by CBE Governor for prosecution in specified cases, settlement and allocation of fines.

## 4. Regulatory implementation and related decisions
- CBE official banking laws index lists:
  - Law 194/2020.
  - CBE Statute.
  - Executive Regulation of Central Bank and Banking Sector Law.
  - Presidential Decree 584/2021 establishing Coordinating Council.
  - Presidential Decree 89/2017 establishing National Payments Council.
  - Presidential Decrees 458/2018, 609/2019, 677/2019 and 678/2019 on CBE leadership/board.
- The law is implemented through a large body of CBE Board regulations/circulars on:
  - Bank licensing and ownership.
  - Governance and board composition.
  - Capital adequacy, liquidity and credit risk.
  - AML/CFT and sanctions.
  - Customer protection, complaints and disclosure.
  - Payment systems, e-money, cards, mobile payments and payment service providers.
  - Credit information and scoring.
  - Outsourcing, cybersecurity, cloud, operational resilience and business continuity.
  - Financial inclusion and consumer finance.
- Need collect each CBE rule as a separately versioned instrument; do not treat all circulars as one Executive Regulation.
