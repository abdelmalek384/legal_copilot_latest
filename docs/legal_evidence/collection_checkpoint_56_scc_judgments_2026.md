## 2. SCC case 98/43 Constitutional — Article 61 of Civil Service Law 18/2015

- Official full judgment HTML:
  https://www.sccourt.gov.eg/DjangoPortalResources/Rules_Html/17937_43_98_1_2.html
- Judgment date: 7 March 2026.
- The referral challenged Article 61 of Civil Service Law 18/2015 and Article 64 of Civil Service Law 81/2016, concerning suspension/withholding of half salary for employees held in pretrial detention or under a non-final criminal judgment.
- The Court noted its earlier judgment in case 100/43 Constitutional (4 November 2023), which had already held unconstitutional the first paragraph of Article 64 of Law 81/2016 insofar as it denied half salary to an employee whose criminal responsibility was finally negated by a final judgment or unappealable judicial decision.
- In case 98/43, the Court treated the Article 64 part as already resolved and held unconstitutional Article 61 of Law 18/2015 to the same extent.
- Holding: an employee who is detained pretrial and whose criminal responsibility is finally negated is entitled to recover the withheld half salary; withholding it permanently violates constitutional protections, including presumption of innocence, equality/justice, public employment rights, property and access to justice.
- The Court's reasoning discusses the constitutional duty to compensate for pretrial detention and the interaction with Criminal Procedure Law 174/2025 as it stood on 7 March 2026.
- **Temporal correction:** the judgment's discussion refers to the then-expected commencement of Law 174/2025 on 1 October 2026. Law 160/2026 later postponed commencement to 1 October 2027. The judgment's constitutional holding remains relevant; its statement about the future commencement date is superseded by Law 160/2026.
- This is a high-value binding constitutional judgment. Link the holding to Civil Service Law 18/2015 Article 61, Civil Service Law 81/2016 Article 64, Constitution Articles 4, 14, 33, 35, 54, 95–98 and 184, and Criminal Procedure Law 174/2025 (future-effective) / Law 150/1950 (current until 1 October 2027).

## 3. SCC case 35/45 Conflict — Agricultural Bank employment dispute

- Official full judgment HTML:
  https://www.sccourt.gov.eg/DjangoPortalResources/Rules_Html/18211_45_35_3_2.html
- Judgment date: 6 June 2026.
- Applicant: Chairman of the Egyptian Agricultural Bank; respondent: individual employee.
- The case concerns a conflict between a State Council Administrative Court judgment and an ordinary civil labour-court judgment over an employee's agricultural-land holding records and resulting agricultural possession card.
- The Court's jurisdiction-conflict decision is relevant to:
  - distinction between administrative and ordinary judicial jurisdiction;
  - effect of final judgments;
  - disputes involving public-sector/public-business entities and employment relationships;
  - agricultural holding records and administrative refusal to issue a card.
- Extract the full operative ruling and legal reasoning before creating a precedent summary; the current source excerpt includes procedural history but not the complete disposition in this checkpoint.

## 4. SCC 3 January 2026 — terrorism offences and constitutional challenge

- Official SCC judgment summary page:
  https://www.sccourt.gov.eg/SCC/?a=cp3&id=802
- The Court rejected a constitutional challenge to Penal Code Articles 86, 86 bis, 86 bis (a), 88 bis (c), and provisions of Counter-Terrorism Law 94/2015.
- The Court reasoned that Egypt's ratification/accession to relevant regional and international anti-terrorism instruments creates obligations to align domestic law with those commitments, while preserving public rights and freedoms.
- It cited Constitution Articles 59, 93 and 237 and explained that Counter-Terrorism Law 94/2015 supplements the Penal Code's terrorism provisions rather than repealing them.
- The judgment should be captured as a constitutional precedent linked to the exact challenged provisions and treaties, not as a statutory amendment.

## 6. Precedent ingestion rules

- Preserve court, case number, judicial year, hearing date, publication date, type (constitutional/conflict/execution), parties only where appropriate, challenged provisions, operative holding, reasoning, constitutional provisions, statutory version and subsequent treatment.
- Do not copy personal names from judgments into the general search index unless necessary and lawful; pseudonymize where possible.
- Distinguish binding erga omnes constitutional holdings from ordinary judicial reasoning and case-specific facts.
- Link every judgment to the exact historical version of the challenged statute and the date on which it applied.
- Do not treat a judgment summary as a substitute for the complete judgment.
