# Collection checkpoint 58 — Terrorism Law 94/2015, Terrorist Lists Law 8/2015 and Asset Management Law 22/2018

## 1. Counter-Terrorism Law 94/2015
- Official/secondary full text:
  https://manshurat.org/node/14679
  https://temp.manshurat.org/node/14679
- Gazette: issue 33 bis, 15 August 2015; effective 16 August 2015.
- Full consolidated text indicates latest amendment Law 15/2020 dated 3 March 2020.
- Promulgation Article 1 applies the attached Counter-Terrorism Law; matters not specifically regulated are governed by Penal Code 58/1937 and Criminal Procedure Law.
- The statute contains substantive offences and procedural/administrative rules relating to terrorist acts, financing, recruitment, training, travel, weapons, explosives, public facilities, media/publication, concealment and assistance.
- Article 7 treats as accomplice a person who knowingly facilitates a terrorist or terrorist group, directly/indirectly, by enabling an offence, preparation, shelter or meeting place.
- Article 8 addresses use of force by persons implementing the law, subject to necessity/proportionality requirements.

## 2. Law 8/2015 — Regulation of Terrorist Entities and Terrorists Lists
- Official Gazette PDF hosted by FRA:
  https://fra.gov.eg/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/قانون-8-لسنة-2015-فى-شأن-تنظيم-قوائم-الكيانات-الإرهابية.pdf
- Gazette issue 7 bis (z), 17 February 2015; effective 18 February 2015.
- FRA updated law page:
  https://fra.gov.eg/regulations/قانون-رقم-8-لسنة-2015-فى-شأن-تنظيم-قوائم-الكيانات-الإرهابية/
- Full text:
  https://lawhub.info/eg/?p=11464
  https://manshurat.org/node/63720
- Creates two lists prepared by Public Prosecution: terrorist entities and terrorists.
- Article 4: listing period up to five years; before expiry, Public Prosecution may seek extension; absent final judgment or extension, name must be removed when period ends.
- Article 5 publication rules amended by Law 2/2020.
- Article 6: affected persons and Public Prosecution may challenge listing within 60 days before a designated Criminal Chamber of Court of Cassation; challenge may seek exemption of frozen funds for basic living/medical/rent/food expenses.
- Article 7 sets statutory consequences of listing, including prohibition/suspension of entity activities, closure of premises, restrictions on funding/collection, asset freezing, travel restrictions and related legal consequences, subject to the law and judicial order.
- Article 8 regulates management of frozen assets.
- Article 8 bis added by Law 14/2020: Public Prosecutor may order provisional freezing/non-disposal of assets where serious information/evidence indicates assets derived from activities of listed/unlisted terrorist or entity or used to finance them; order must be presented to the designated circuit within one month for confirmation, cancellation or modification.
- Amendment graph: Laws 11/2017, 2/2020, 14/2020 and 15/2020 (verify exact provisions from Gazette texts).

## 3. Law 22/2018 — procedures for seizure, inventory, management and disposal of assets of terrorist groups/terrorists
- Official/secondary record:
  https://manshurat.org/node/25906
- FRA updated page:
  https://fra.gov.eg/regulations/قانون-رقم-22-لسنة-2018/
- Full text:
  https://lawhub.info/eg/?p=12627
- Gazette and promulgation date: 21 April 2018; effective 22 April 2018.
- Establishes a judicial/administrative mechanism and committee for implementing court orders concerning seizure, inventory, management and disposal of assets belonging to terrorist groups and terrorists.
- Article 1 defines court judgments, competent court (Cairo Court of Urgent Matters), committee, funds/assets (including electronic/digital form) and related terms.
- Further provisions regulate committee composition, inventory, custody, administration, management, sale/disposal, revenue, expenses, objections and appeal.

## 4. Cross-law relationships
- Penal Code 58/1937.
- Criminal Procedure Law 174/2025.
- AML Law 80/2002 and Executive Regulation 951/2003.
- Law 8/2015 terrorist-listing and Law 22/2018 asset-management are distinct legal mechanisms; do not conflate listing consequences with criminal conviction or asset confiscation.
- Constitution, Supreme Constitutional Court Law 48/1979, State Council Law 47/1972 and relevant international agreements.
- Preserve due-process safeguards, appeal periods, listing duration, freezing scope, review and exemptions exactly as written; no simplification that changes legal effect.
