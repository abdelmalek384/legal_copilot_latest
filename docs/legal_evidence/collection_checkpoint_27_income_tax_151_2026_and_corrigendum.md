## 1. Law 151/2026 — amendments to Income Tax Law 91/2005

### Article 1 — replaced provisions

The law replaces specified parts of Articles 28, 43, 46 bis (3), 46 bis (4), 50 (items 7 and 10), 52 (paragraph 1, item 1) and 56 bis (5) of Income Tax Law 91/2005.

**Article 28 — bad debts**
- For a debt to be treated as bad debt, the establishment must have taken serious collection measures and failed to collect it within 12 months from its due date.
- Serious measures include obtaining a payment order where permitted, obtaining a first-instance judgment requiring payment, or claiming the debt in bankruptcy proceedings/settlement of bankruptcy.
- Exception: individual bad debts not exceeding EGP 10,000, provided total bad debts do not exceed 1% of the taxpayer's debtor balances at the end of the tax year, subject to the Executive Regulation.

**Article 43 — real-estate disposal tax**
- Imposes tax at 2.5% of the total disposal value of built real estate or land prepared for construction, excluding villages.
- Applies whether property is disposed of in its existing state or after construction, whether all/part of property is disposed of, and whether construction is on land owned by taxpayer or a third party; applies whether contract is registered or not.
- Applies to an heir's disposal of inherited property/land, whether existing or after construction for private use, and to disposal of property acquired by seller, whether existing or after construction for private use.
- Transactions are not considered professional dealing unless Tax Authority proves they were conducted professionally for trading/profit under Article 19(7).
- Value is determined according to the value stated in disposal contract; burden of proof of a different value lies with Tax Authority.
- Exempts in-kind contributions of real estate to share capital of joint-stock companies, provided the corresponding shares are not disposed of for five years.
- Taxpayer must pay within 60 days from disposal; delay consideration under Article 110 runs from the day after that period expires.
- For purposes of this article, sale, gift, donation, will or grant of a right of usufruct for more than 50 years to persons other than spouses/ascendants/descendants is treated as sale.
- Compulsory judicial/administrative sales, expropriation for public benefit/improvement, and gifts/donations to Government, local administration, public legal persons or public-benefit projects are not taxable disposals.
- Tax paid is credited against total taxes due where Article 19(7) applies.

**Article 46 bis (3) — non-listed share disposal**
- Capital gains tax applies to gains from disposal of shares/quotas in companies or securities not listed on Egyptian Exchange, whether gains arise in Egypt or abroad.
- A non-resident disposing of such shares/quotas must calculate and remit tax on realized gains within 60 days of transaction, according to Executive Regulation procedures.
- Does not apply to gains by non-residents from disposal of Treasury bills.

**Article 46 bis (4) — calculating gains**
- Taxable gains from disposal of unlisted securities/shares/quotas are based on difference between sale/exchange/disposal price and acquisition cost, after deducting brokerage commission.
- If sale/exchange/disposal is at fair value, acquisition cost is increased by an amount equal to CBE's announced discount/credit rate for each year of holding, provided disposal occurs after three years.
- Acquisition cost is calculated on weighted-average basis under the Executive Regulation.

**Article 50 — exemptions**
- Adds/updates exemption for returns on government bonds listed on Egyptian exchanges and capital gains from listed securities, subject to no deduction of losses from such disposal.
- Excludes gains on listed securities where the disposal results in delisting of the company's shares.
- Adds exemption for dividends received by parent/holding companies from resident/non-resident subsidiaries, provided the parent/holding company owns at least 25% of capital or voting rights and has held/will hold the stake for at least two years from acquisition.

**Article 52 — interest deduction**
- Replaces rule for interest paid by legal persons on loans/advances exceeding twice average equity according to financial statements prepared under Egyptian accounting standards.
- Extends a four-times-equity rule for specified legal persons implementing national infrastructure projects designated by Prime Minister decision, provided loans are from unrelated parties and legal person contributes at least 25% of project investment; benefit ends when financing for core project works expires.
- Excludes banks, insurance companies and companies conducting financing activities designated by ministerial decision.

**Article 56 bis (5) — dividends**
- Dividends are generally subject to 10% tax without deduction of costs for resident/non-resident natural persons, legal persons, and entities in special economic zones; dividends in form of free shares are excepted.
- Rate is 5% for dividends from securities listed on Egyptian Exchange, without deduction of costs.
- Certain intercompany dividends between resident legal persons are excluded from the tax base under the conditions in the law/Executive Regulation.
- Intermediary entities withhold and remit tax to the Central Administration for Withholding and Collection within five working days at the beginning of the month following collection.

### Article 2 — additions
- Adds item 10 to Article 23: contribution to the comprehensive health insurance system under Article 40(9) of Law 2/2018.
- Adds item 7 to Article 31: capital gains from disposal of listed securities; losses from these disposals may not be deducted or carried forward, except where disposal results in delisting of company shares.
- Adds item 5 to Article 33: the same listed-securities capital gains treatment.
- Adds fourth paragraph to Article 56: exempts interest on loans/facilities obtained by public-sector/public-business-sector/private-sector companies from unrelated persons to finance specified national infrastructure projects, if loan term is at least five years and company participation in project is at least 25%; exemption ends when loan expires/core works completed.

### Article 3 — incentive for IPOs
- Companies listing their shares on the Egyptian Exchange under an FRA-approved prospectus receive an investment incentive equal to 15% deduction from income tax due in the tax return for three years from the offering date.
- Conditions:
  1. Fair market value of company shares at offering must be at least EGP 50 billion.
  2. Offered shares must be at least 20% of company shares or have value of at least EGP 10 billion.
- Incentive granted only once during the company's life; no combination with incentives under other laws.
- Finance Minister may extend duration by similar period subject to FRA coordination and prescribed conditions.
- Executive Regulation to define review/verification, suspension and clawback rules if conditions cease to be met.

### Article 4 — transitional treatment for taxpayers with annual turnover up to EGP 10 million
- Unfinalized tax assessments on establishments/companies with annual turnover not exceeding EGP 10 million for periods starting 1 January 2022 and ending before March 2025 are assessed under graduated rules, without prejudice to taxpayer's option to be assessed under ordinary Income Tax Law.
- The text provides turnover bands and rates:
  - EGP 2m to less than EGP 3m: 0.75%.
  - EGP 3m to EGP 10m: 1%.
  - For smaller turnover, separate fixed annual amounts are specified in the law (EGP 250,000/500,000/1m turnover bands, with EGP 1,000/2,500/5,000 amounts respectively).
- The first part of Article 4 includes a prior band for turnover below EGP 2m at 0.5%; preserve the full table and its conditions when ingesting, not a generalized rate summary.

### Article 5 — prior listed-securities gains and Treasury securities
- Subject to Article 4 of Law 30/2023, repeals the exemption introduced by Law 182/2020 for returns on Treasury bills/bonds and capital gains from trading them.
- Waives tax not paid on capital gains from listed shares during 16 June 2023 to commencement of Law 151/2026, as specified.

### Article 6 — repeal of Article 18
- Repeals Article 18 of Income Tax Law 91/2005.
- Preserves validity of rules, bases and procedures issued under Article 18 for tax periods 2027 and earlier.
- Also repeals Articles 39 bis, 46 bis (5), 46 bis (7), 49 bis and Article 7 of Law 30/2023 as specified in the text.

### Article 7 — commencement
- Effective the day after Gazette publication.

## 3. Important implementation/transition notes

- ETA announcement dated 17 September 2026 says Article 18 repeal ends estimated/flat accounting and regular books/digital systems become operative from tax period 2028; existing instructions continue for periods through 2027. The Gazette law itself is the authority; the ETA announcement is explanatory context.
- Law 151/2026 must be linked to Law 91/2005, Law 2/2018 (comprehensive health insurance contribution), Capital Market Law 95/1992, accounting standards, CBE rates and the Executive Regulation of Income Tax Law 991/2005.
- Law 151/2026 is not a consolidated Income Tax Law. The full amendment chain remains to be reconciled.
