## 1. Structure and foundational rules
- 117 articles in the full-text transcription.
- Article 1: creditor proves the obligation; debtor proves discharge.
- Article 2: facts to be proved must be relevant, productive in the dispute and legally admissible.
- Articles 3–9: court's evidence-management powers, judicially delegated evidence procedures, time limits, expert/judge delegation, proof procedure modification and reasoning.
- Documentary evidence:
  - Official instruments and their evidentiary force (Articles 10–13).
  - Private instruments, signature/handwriting denial and fixed date (Articles 14–19).
  - Compelling production of documents held by opponent or third parties (Articles 20–27).
  - Examination of document authenticity, handwriting comparison, forgery allegations and original forgery actions (Articles 28–59).
- Testimony:
  - Witness evidence admissibility thresholds and exceptions (Articles 60–71).
  - Witness capacity, confidentiality/professional privilege and spousal communications.
  - Hearing and examination procedure.
- Other evidence:
  - Presumptions and judicial inferences.
  - Res judicata and evidentiary effect of judgments.
  - Expert evidence and judicial inspection.
  - Oaths, decisive and supplementary oaths.
- The precise current monetary limits for testimony, and all amended provisions, must be checked against subsequent laws and the current Civil Code/procedural law.

## 2. Selected rules captured from full text
- Article 10: official instrument is one in which a public officer/person entrusted with public service records what was done before them or received from interested persons, within legal competence and formalities; if it loses official status it may still count as private instrument if signed/sealed/fingerprinted by parties.
- Article 11: official instruments are evidence against all persons for matters recorded by the author within their function or occurring in their presence, unless forged by legally prescribed process.
- Articles 12–13: evidentiary force of official copies depends on matching original; where original absent, different classes of copies have different evidentiary value.
- Article 14: private instrument is attributed to signer unless handwriting/signature/seal/fingerprint is expressly denied; heirs/successors may swear they do not know it.
- Article 15: private instrument has fixed date against third parties only from specified events (registration, reproduction in fixed-date instrument, official endorsement, death/incapacity of signatory, or other conclusive event).
- Articles 20–27: conditions for ordering opponent/third party to produce relevant documents; request must describe document, content, fact to prove, indications of possession and legal basis.
- Articles 28–59: physical defects, handwriting comparison, forgery allegation procedure and independent forgery action.
- Article 60 (original text): in non-commercial matters, testimony restricted for legal acts above EGP 500 or indeterminate value unless agreement/statute permits; this amount is historically outdated and must not be used without amendment/currentness check.
- Articles 115–117: decisive oath may not concern public-order-inconsistent facts; once accepted it cannot be withdrawn; after oath, falsehood cannot be proved except via criminal judgment and related remedies.

## 3. Amendment chain and currentness
- Law 23/1992 and Law 18/1999 are identified as amendments in the Notaries of Egypt source.
- Law 76/2007 amends Evidence Law together with Civil and Commercial Procedure Law; exact affected articles must be extracted.
- Other amendments/court rulings may affect digital evidence, commercial books, expert evidence and monetary proof thresholds; verify complete Gazette amendment history.
- Electronic signature/electronic writing is also governed by Electronic Signature Law 15/2004 and Civil Code/Commercial Code provisions; do not treat this Evidence Law alone as the full digital-evidence framework.
- The Law 25/1968 original repeal clause interacts with Civil Code 131/1948 and Civil Procedure Law 13/1968; link the repealed provisions and transition dates.
