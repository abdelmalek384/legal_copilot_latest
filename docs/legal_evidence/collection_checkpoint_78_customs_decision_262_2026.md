## 2. Article 1 — replaces Article 232 of Executive Regulation 430/2021

The new Article 232 requires the customs declaration to be accompanied by the following documents, while avoiding duplicate submission of documents already filed electronically:

1. Copy of bill of lading, manually or electronically, except for pre-clearance.
2. Delivery order where the bill of lading is "To Order".
3. If the bill of lading is nominative, a declaration using the form issued by the Customs Authority Head, in which importer/agent undertakes to pay all dues owed to relevant entities connected with the shipment and to submit the bill of lading or electronic equivalent issued by carrier/shipping agent before final release.
4. Detailed commercial invoice, unless the packing list already contains the detailed package information for the imported goods.
5. Proof of origin where the importer claims a customs exemption or preference, plus other cases under the Import/Export Executive Regulation.

## 3. Practical legal effect

- This is a procedural customs-document amendment, not a change to the Customs tariff or import licensing rules.
- It clarifies which documents must accompany the customs declaration and allows no repeated filing of documents already submitted electronically.
- It distinguishes:
  - pre-clearance shipments;
  - "To Order" bills of lading requiring delivery order;
  - nominative bills of lading requiring a declaration/undertaking;
  - detailed invoice versus packing-list information;
  - proof of origin for tariff preferences/exemptions.
- Customs clearance workflow should validate the bill-of-lading type before requesting the relevant supporting document.
- The undertaking for nominative bills of lading is linked to payment of all dues to relevant entities and provision of the bill of lading before final release.

## 4. Relationship to other 2026 customs changes

- Decision 262/2026 (24 May 2026) replaces Article 232 of Regulation 430/2021.
- Decision 328/2026 (published 21 June 2026) is a separate later amendment identified in Customs Authority Procedure Notice 16/2026 (checkpoint 70); obtain and map its affected articles independently.
- Do not confuse the two decisions or treat one as superseding the other without comparing their subject matter.
- Import/Export Law 118/1975 and Executive Regulation 770/2005 continue to govern trade controls and origin documents, while Customs Law 207/2020/Regulation 430/2021 govern customs declaration/clearance.
