## 1. Scope and procedural framework
- Governs litigation procedures in personal-status and endowment matters.
- Where it has no special rule, applies Civil and Commercial Procedure Law, Evidence Law in civil/commercial matters, and Civil Code rules for estate administration/liquidation.
- Interim-matters judge at the Court of First Instance has exclusive authority to issue urgent orders on specified matters, including:
  - Complaint against notary refusal to authenticate marriage contract or issue refusal certificate (Egyptians/foreigners).
  - Extension of estate inventory deadline.
  - Protective/interim measures for estates without incapacitated/minor/absent persons.
  - Authorization to move money/securities/documents/jewellery at risk from incapacitated/minor/absent persons' assets to a bank/safe place.
  - Disputes over travel abroad after hearing interested parties.
- Procedural time limits are calculated by Gregorian calendar.
- Litigation capacity for personal-status matters concerning personal guardianship begins at 15 Gregorian years for a person of sound mind.
- Legal representative acts for persons without/full capacity; court appoints litigation guardian where necessary.
- No lawyer signature required on personal-status claim forms before partial courts; court may appoint counsel when necessary.
- Maintenance and related wages/expenses claims are exempt from all court fees at all stages.
- Courts may appoint social specialists and hear some matters in chambers with Public Prosecution presence where public order/morals require; judgments are pronounced in public.
- Public Prosecution must intervene in cases before first-instance and appellate courts or judgment is void.

## 2. Jurisdiction and family dispute procedures
- Allocates subject-matter jurisdiction between partial courts and first-instance courts for personal-status matters.
- Addresses jurisdiction over custody, child protection, maintenance, wages/expenses, dowry/equipment, marriage documentation, guardianship, wills, inheritance, endowments and related claims.
- Establishes rules for appeals, finality, enforcement, provisional orders and execution.
- Law 10/2004 Family Courts Law later created dedicated Family Courts and settlement offices, and allocates jurisdiction by reference to Law 1/2000.

## 3. Key evidence and limitation rules
- Article 7: after death of ancestor, claim acknowledging lineage or testimony to acknowledgment is not accepted on denial unless official documents, writings wholly in deceased's handwriting/signature, or conclusive evidence support it.
- Article 8: endowment claims require registered official deed; endowment/inheritance claims on denial generally not accepted after 33 years from accrual unless a valid excuse prevented filing.
- Additional evidence rules, jurisdiction, appeal and execution provisions require full article-level extraction.

## 4. Amendment and relationship graph
- Law 91/2000 amends Law 1/2000; Gazette 18 May 2000.
- Law 10/2004 Family Courts Law: creates Family Courts and Family Dispute Settlement Offices, requires pre-filing referral for reconcilable disputes subject to exceptions, and applies Law 1/2000 plus Civil Procedure/Evidence/Civil Code gap-filling.
- Link to Personal Status Law 25/1929 and subsequent amendments, Personal Status Law 100/1985, Child Law 12/1996 (as amended by Law 126/2008), Evidence Law 25/1968 and Civil Code inheritance provisions.
- Ministerial implementation decisions include Decisions 1086–1090/2000 (social specialists, child visitation locations, guardianship inventory, notaries/marriage officials and registry procedures) as secondary source leads; retrieve Gazette texts.

## 5. Important distinction
- Law 1/2000 is procedural; it does not replace the substantive Personal Status Law 25/1929 and other substantive personal-status statutes.
- Family Courts Law 10/2004 is an institutional/jurisdictional statute, not a full substantive personal-status code.
- Do not merge all family statutes into a single unversioned "Family Law" text.
