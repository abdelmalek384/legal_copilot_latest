### Article 1 — replaced provisions
Replaces Executive Regulation Articles 4, 10, 12, 13, 13 bis, 17, Article 19 paragraph 2 and Article 51 paragraph 1.

- Article 4: EEAA Board becomes the supreme authority managing the Agency; powers include national environmental plans, emergency plans, draft environmental legislation, work programmes, pilot projects, training, pollutant standards, EIA procedures, organizational structure, internal regulations, annual budget and permit/approval fees.
- Article 10: every natural/legal person, public/private, must submit an Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) to competent administrative/licensing authority before project commencement. EIA must follow EEAA criteria, specifications, pollutant loads and coordination requirements. Industrial-zone authorities must provide pollution-load and activity-distribution studies, integrated waste/wastewater/air/noise self-monitoring plans.
- EEAA may request missing data/designs once; applicant has 15 working days to respond; Agency opinion due within 30 working days after complete data received.
- Article 12: licensing applicant submits a complete project statement, pollutant loads, self-monitoring system, climate-change impact and mitigation plan. EIA must be prepared by an authorized environmental practitioner; preparer and applicant are responsible for information/documentation. EEAA maintains register of forms, assessments, pollutant loads and requests.
- Article 13: EEAA may consult public/private consulting bodies, research centres or universities to review EIA.
- Article 13 bis: reserves specified environmental work to licensed/accredited practitioners, including quantitative/qualitative environmental-risk studies, EIAs, environmental management systems, compliance/corrective plans, environmental economics/accounting, protected-area projects, pollutant dispersion modelling, biodiversity studies and environmental sustainability. Measurements must be made by accredited laboratories whose scope covers the pollutants.
- Registration/accreditation categories and experience levels are specified for environmental specialists (B/A) and consultants (B/A), with 5-, 10- and 20-year experience thresholds; certificates valid five years, subject to conditions.
- Article 17: establishment manager must keep paper and electronic environmental register documenting emissions/discharges/pollutant loads, treatment outputs and efficiency, self-monitoring procedures, tests/samples/measurements, emergency plans and responsible persons. Deviations must be notified immediately; register retained for 10 years after EEAA representative inspection/signature; annual discharge/concentration reports submitted.
- Article 19 paragraph 2: expansions/renewals determined by EEAA Executive Head decision after Board approval.
- Article 51 paragraph 1: shipping ports, oil-tanker reception ports and ship-repair docks must have adequate equipment for receiving dirty ballast water and liquid residues from tank washing.

### Article 2 — replacement annexes
- Replaces Annexes 1 and 1 bis (liquid waste discharge standards and continuous self-monitoring indicators) and Annex 7 Table 3 (maximum permissible noise levels).
- These numerical tables are legally significant and must be extracted as structured data with units, locations, dates and limits; do not rely on narrative summary.

### Article 3 — additions
- Adds two items to Article 5, new paragraphs to Articles 11, 21 and 51, new Article 18 bis, and two new items to Annex 6.
- Article 4 cancels Executive Regulation Articles 25–33 and 38.
