# Collection checkpoint 58 — SCC published digest of 20 constitutional principles

## 1. Official source and status

- Official Supreme Constitutional Court publication:
  https://www.sccourt.gov.eg/SCC/?a=cp3&id=876
- The page reproduces a speech/publication by the SCC President summarizing twenty principles derived from constitutional judgments under the 2014 Constitution.
- It is an official judicial-publication/synthesis source, **not itself a judgment or a substitute for the full judgments**.
- The individual judgments cited by the digest remain the binding primary authorities; use the digest as a discovery map only.

## 2. Principles visible in the official publication

### Principle 1 — separation and balance of powers
- Constitutional basis: Constitution Article 5.
- Case 86/38 Constitutional, judgment 2 July 2022.
- The Court treated Parliament's decision not to approve a decree-law, while approving its temporary prior operation, as a parliamentary act within the legislature's reserved competence rather than a legislative text subject to constitutional review in itself.
- The case illustrates institutional balance among President, Parliament and Constitutional Court.

### Principle 2 — equality of women and men in public appointments
- Constitutional basis: Constitution Article 11.
- Case 70/35 Constitutional, judgment 25 July 2015.
- The Court rejected a challenge to the Family Courts Law provision requiring at least one of the two experts serving with a Family Court to be a woman.
- The principle supports women's participation in public functions and the constitutionality of targeted representation measures in the specific context considered.

### Principle 8 — compensation/recovery for pretrial detention where criminal responsibility is finally negated
- Constitutional basis: Constitution Article 54.
- Case 100/43 Constitutional, judgment 4 November 2023.
- The Court held unconstitutional the first paragraph of Article 64 of Civil Service Law 81/2016 insofar as it deprived a public employee held in pretrial detention of half salary where criminal responsibility was finally negated by a final judgment or unappealable judicial decision.
- Case 98/43 Constitutional (7 March 2026) extended the same constitutional reasoning to Article 61 of Civil Service Law 18/2015 (see checkpoint 56).
- Link to Civil Service Law 81/2016 Article 64, Law 18/2015 Article 61, Criminal Procedure Law 150/1950 and future-effective Law 174/2025 as amended by Law 160/2026.

### Principle 9 — intellectual-property protection
- Constitutional basis: Constitution Article 69.
- The official digest identifies a principle protecting intellectual property and refers to a challenge concerning a statutory share of proceeds from exploitation of intellectual production after copyright protection expires.
- The precise case number, operative order and challenged law should be retrieved from the full judgment before summarizing the rule. Do not infer that all expired copyright works are unregulated or that every collective-management fee is constitutional.

### Principle 17 — State Council's exclusive jurisdiction over administrative disputes
- Constitutional basis: Constitution Article 190.
- The digest refers to judgments holding unconstitutional provisions in the General Sales Tax and Income Tax laws insofar as they assigned taxpayer appeals against Tax Appeal Committee decisions to ordinary first-instance courts.
- The principle is that administrative disputes fall within the State Council's exclusive jurisdiction under the constitutional allocation, subject to the precise nature of the dispute and any later legislative/constitutional change.
- Link to tax appeal committee statutes, State Council Law 47/1972 and current tax procedure laws.

### Principle 18 — absolute binding force of SCC judgments and decisions
- Constitutional basis: Constitution Article 195.
- Case 65/41 Execution Dispute, judgment 15 October 2022.
- The Court distinguished:
  - constitutional interpretations of specific provisions that are necessary to the judgment and bind everyone/all State authorities; and
  - other case-specific findings/operative matters that have res judicata effect between the parties and those required to implement the decision.
- This is important for determining the scope of erga omnes effect; do not treat every sentence in an SCC judgment as a general binding constitutional rule.

### Principle 19 — legal profession participates with judiciary in achieving justice
- Constitutional basis: Constitution Article 198.
- Case 28/38 Constitutional, judgment 3 November 2018.
- The Court rejected a challenge to the Lawyers Law requirement that pleadings before Courts of Appeal and equivalent levels be signed by a lawyer, with invalidity consequences for non-compliance.
- The Court reasoned that the requirement organizes the right to litigation and defence by ensuring serious, professionally prepared pleadings; it does not extinguish either right.

### Principle 20 — Preamble and all Constitution provisions form an integrated whole
- Constitutional basis: Constitution Article 227.
- Case 1/41 Constitutional, judgment 17 December 2022.
- The Court held unconstitutional a legislative instrument that had not been published in the Official Gazette but continued to be applied under the 2011 Constitutional Declaration and later encountered the 2014 Constitution.
- The digest explains that rights/freedoms provisions integrate with the Constitution as an organic, indivisible whole and cannot be suspended merely because an earlier declaration omitted them.

## 4. Retrieval and corpus rules

- Index each underlying SCC judgment by case number, date, challenged text, constitutional provisions, operative holding, necessary reasoning, publication and later treatment.
- Treat the SCC President's digest as a secondary official summary. Never cite it as though it were the operative judgment.
- The scope of binding force depends on the holding and reasons inseparably connected to it; preserve the Court's distinction between erga omnes constitutional interpretation and ordinary res judicata.
- For a provision held unconstitutional, record the precise text/scope and effect date; do not mark the whole article or statute invalid unless the judgment so holds.
- Maintain the SCC public-source register separately from the Court of Cassation portal's restricted commercial-use terms.
