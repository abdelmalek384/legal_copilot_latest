## 2. Matters reported by EEAA

The agency report states that the third meeting:
- reviewed rejected consignments, including products that had expired, changed characteristics, failed specifications, or exceeded permitted pesticide, chemical or dye residues;
- reported that such rejected consignments were being directed to cement kilns that had WMRA approval, rather than hazardous-waste landfill cells;
- addressed consignments that exporting countries declined to receive back;
- reported an agreement to prevent accumulation of those consignments and to dispose of them under environmental controls;
- stated that the National Food Safety Authority would inventory consignments already present in Egypt and coordinate with importers and Customs on re-export.

The report also describes the committee's broader remit as preparing, issuing and reviewing unified lists of hazardous materials and waste; setting controls for their handling and integrated management; determining methods to reduce their generation; and identifying the ministry or authority responsible for issuing relevant licences.

## 3. Legal and evidentiary limits

The report is evidence of what EEAA publicly communicated about committee activity on 9 September 2026. It is **not** sufficient to establish:
- the operative articles, legal scope, commencement or repeal provisions of Decision 368/2025;
- whether the described agreement was adopted as a formal committee resolution, and its exact text, vote, date or legal force;
- a generally applicable rule that every rejected consignment must be sent to a cement kiln;
- the technical acceptance criteria, facility approvals, waste classification, shipment-specific permissions or applicable Basel Convention steps;
- that an agency announcement itself amends Law 202/2020, Executive Regulation 722/2022, Decision 121/2025, Decision 151/2025 or any other instrument.

The statement about cement kilns is reported agency practice for the described rejected consignments, not a general legal permission or compliance instruction. The statement about NFSA-led inventory/re-export is likewise a reported coordination arrangement, not a substitute for shipment-specific legal requirements.
