## 1. Promulgation and opening rules
- Article 1 promulgation repeals the civil codes previously applicable before national and mixed courts (1883 and 1875) and replaces them with the attached Civil Code.
- Article 2 commencement: 15 October 1949.
- Preliminary Title, Articles 1–21 addresses:
  - Application of statutory text, custom, Islamic Sharia principles, natural law and justice (Article 1).
  - Repeal of legislation (Article 2).
  - Gregorian calculation of periods (Article 3).
  - Lawful exercise of rights and abuse of rights (Articles 4–5).
  - Temporal conflict of laws and limitation (Articles 6–9).
  - Conflict of laws in space and applicable law (Articles 10–21), including nationality, marriage, divorce, maintenance, guardianship, inheritance, wills, property, contracts and non-contractual obligations.

## 2. Code structure and coverage
- Preliminary Title: general rules and application of law.
- Book One: obligations in general:
  - Sources of obligations: contract, unilateral act, tort/unlawful act, enrichment without cause and law.
  - Effects, transfer, extinction, proof, limitation.
- Book Two: named contracts:
  - Sale, exchange, gift, partnership, loan, deposit, agency, lease, employment/service, construction/work contracts, settlement, insurance and other nominate contracts.
- Book Three: rights in rem:
  - Ownership, co-ownership, usufruct, use/habitation, easements, mortgages, pledges and priority/security rights.
- Code extends to Article 1,149; exact Book/Chapter/article segmentation should be parsed from the official PDF.

## 3. Selected foundational rules captured
- Article 1: statutory texts apply to matters they cover; absent text, judge applies custom, then principles of Islamic Sharia, then natural law and justice.
- Article 2: legislation is repealed by express later legislation, conflicting later text or new regulation of the same subject.
- Article 4: lawful exercise of a right does not create liability for resulting harm.
- Article 5: abuse of right where sole purpose is harm, interest is grossly disproportionate to harm, or interest pursued is unlawful.
- Article 10: Egyptian law governs characterization of relationships in conflict-of-laws disputes.
- Articles 11–21: conflict-of-laws rules for personal status, legal persons, marriage, divorce, maintenance, guardianship, inheritance/wills, property, contracts and non-contractual obligations.
- Article 147: contract is law between parties; exceptional general unforeseen events that make performance onerous with grave loss may allow judge to restore balance to reasonable limits; agreement excluding this rule is void.
- Article 148: contracts must be performed according to their terms and good faith; obligations include consequences required by law, custom and justice.
- Article 970 was amended by Law 55/1970 (related to acquisition/prescription of State/public property; exact text and effect need source matching).
