# Egypt: copyright treatment of official legal documents — research note

**Status:** preliminary research only; not a legal opinion, source-rights approval, or change to the acquisition/release gates.

**Purpose:** assess whether Legal Copilot can build a corpus from publicly accessible Egyptian primary legal texts without copying third-party legal databases or relying on permissions that have not been established.

## Primary statutory text located

Egyptian Intellectual Property Rights Law No. 82 of 2002 distinguishes protected works from excluded official documents:

- **Article 140** lists protected literary and artistic works, including written works, databases, and derivative works.
- **Article 138(6)** defines a derivative work to include compilations of works, including databases, where the selection or arrangement is creative.
- **Article 141(1)** excludes official documents, regardless of their source or language, including laws, regulations, resolutions, decisions, international conventions, court judgments, arbitral awards, and decisions of administrative committees with judicial competence.
- The final paragraph of **Article 141** preserves protection for collections of those official documents when the selection or arrangement is creative or involves qualifying personal effort.
- **Article 147** describes the exclusive exploitation rights of authors in protected works.

The Arabic text of Law No. 82/2002 is available in a government-hosted GAFI PDF. WIPO Lex provides a consolidated record marked as amended by Law No. 178/2020; its amendment summary identifies that 2020 amendment as affecting Article 185. This note does not independently establish whether any later amendment after the WIPO Lex consolidation date changed the provisions discussed here.

## Cautious implications for Legal Copilot

1. **The underlying text of an individual law is an official document.** Article 141 is a substantial legal basis for treating the official text of a law differently from a privately authored legal commentary or database.
2. **A curated legal corpus is a separate question.** A collection/database can qualify for protection where its selection or arrangement is creative. Do not assume that the statutory exclusion for each official document automatically removes all rights in a third-party compilation, editorial arrangement, headnotes, annotations, summaries, translations, or database structure.
3. **A government-hosted PDF is a source locator, not a complete rights determination.** Article 141 addresses copyright in official documents. It does not, by itself, resolve every issue concerning website terms, access controls, technical restrictions, privacy, contractual conditions, or rights in non-official material bundled with a Gazette issue.
4. **Public availability is not a licence statement.** Do not treat a publicly accessible URL, a manual capture, or a successful hash as evidence that commercial reuse of the scan, site presentation, or compilation is permitted.
5. **Avoid third-party compilations.** Prefer to obtain the official primary instrument, preserve its original file and provenance, extract the text independently, and build a neutral article index without copying third-party commentary or creative arrangement.

## Recommended provisional engineering policy

- Keep the source-permission register at its existing values. **Do not change collection or commercial-reuse permission to `allowed` based on this note alone.**
- Continue to acquire only publicly accessible official primary-source copies. Do not bypass access controls, request-copy restrictions, robots restrictions, or other technical barriers.
- Preserve original files, hashes, URLs, capture dates, and extraction provenance. Keep each law/version distinct and retain Gazette issue/page pinpoints.
- Keep legal authenticity, effective dates, amendment history, and lawyer approval as separate gates from copyright/source-rights assessment.
- Do not serve any candidate as authoritative until the existing legal release gate is satisfied.

## Questions requiring qualified Egyptian counsel

1. Does Article 141(1), as currently in force, permit commercial reproduction, indexing, and display of the text of an individual Egyptian statute obtained from its official Gazette publication?
2. Does an Official Gazette issue, as a chronological official publication, involve a creative selection or arrangement within the final paragraph of Article 141, or is it simply an official document/official compilation?
3. Are there material differences between reuse of the law's text, reuse of the Gazette scan/layout, and reuse of the hosting website's presentation or metadata?
4. Which conditions should be recorded separately for collection, indexing, display, commercial reuse, and attribution?
5. Have any amendments or binding decisions after the WIPO Lex consolidation date changed the relevant copyright provisions?
